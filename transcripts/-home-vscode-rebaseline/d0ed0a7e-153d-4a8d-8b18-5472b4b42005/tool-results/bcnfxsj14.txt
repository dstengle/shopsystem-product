    20	# Process: Small change
    21	
    22	**Purpose:** Take a request whose route is the small-change lane to a
    23	verified result. No bet is taken and no check of record is run in
    24	this process: both protect the appetite an initiative's bet spends
    25	(the [initiative typedef](../artifacts/initiative.md)'s term), and a
    26	simple change — the glossary's term — spends none of it worth a bet.
    27	
    28	**Guiding statement:** The definition on the request is the whole of
    29	what good looks like for the change: every role in the execution judges by
    30	it, and nothing outside it is made or counts as verified. Everything
    31	the execution decides lands on the request or in the changed
    32	artifacts' own histories; nothing binding lives only in the
    33	transcript.
    34	
    35	The definition of good for this process is the feature
    36	[feat-request-routing](../../features/feat-request-routing.md); each
    37	outcome below names the scenarios of it that it witnesses.
    38	
    39	**Outcomes:**
    40	- O1. The change is defined before it is made, by the lead-po role, on
    41	  the request: the definition opens with the request's id, states
    42	  what will be different as acceptance statements, names the maker
    43	  role and the verifying observation, and stands before any artifact
    44	  is touched — witnessed by `define`'s run-by and prompt, and by the
    45	  step order: `make` is first reached only through
    46	  `route-definition`'s else branch, after `define`; the only other
    47	  entry is `advance-round`, itself downstream of `define` (feature
    48	  scenario *a simple change is defined up front before it is made*).
    49	- O2. The change is checked against its definition by a role other
    50	  than the maker: `make` runs by `lead-solutions-architect` and
    51	  `check` by `lead-pm`, two role ids fixed in this definition, and the
    52	  verdict is written on the request by the checker with its role —
    53	  witnessed by the two steps' run-by and `check`'s prompt (feature
    54	  scenario *a simple change is checked by a role other than its
    55	  maker*).
    56	- O3. The result counts done only when the observation the definition
    57	  named shows the effect in the running system, and the request then
    58	  records the verified result with its evidence; between the request
    59	  and that result no bet is taken and no check of record is run —
    60	  witnessed by `route-verify`'s success exit, which requires the
    61	  observation's `exit 0` as the last line of `evidence`, by `record`'s
    62	  prompt, and by the step list, which carries no bet step and no step
    63	  run by the cold-reviewer role (feature scenarios *a simple change
    64	  reaches a verified result with no bet and no check of record* and
    65	  *the example change reaches a verified result through the lane*).
    66	- O4. A change found not simple once defined leaves the lane for a
    67	  discovery conversation with the reason, and the lane makes no change
    68	  for it: the route is changed by the lead-pm role and by no other —
    69	  witnessed by `route-definition`'s not-simple exit, which precedes
    70	  `make`, and `reroute`'s prompt (feature scenario *a request whose
    71	  change turns out not simple is routed to discovery*).
    72	- O5. Every run that reaches an exit ends in one of three stated
    73	  states on the request — done, routed to discovery, or returned to
    74	  routing with the findings recorded — closes the work item with a
    75	  reason citing the request's id, returns `change`, the request's
    76	  Result section, to the caller, and the make-check-verify loop is
    77	  capped — witnessed by `record`, `reroute`, and `hand-back`, each
    78	  writing its state on the request, by the run templates of
    79	  `close-done`, `close-not-simple`, and `close-failed`, by
    80	  `name-result`'s `set`, and by the failsafe rows of `route-check`
    81	  and `route-verify`. An execution halted by a failed step (Data,
    82	  Verification's rule) reaches no exit: it leaves the request as the
    83	  last completed step wrote it, the work item open, and no `change`
    84	  returned, and the failure is reported to the lead-pm role — the
    85	  halt is not a state of the request.
    86	
    87	**Roles:** the definer —
    88	[`../roles/lead-po.md`](../roles/lead-po.md) (writes the change's
    89	definition on the request; the definition is a requirement, and this
    90	role makes the requirements; it makes nothing else here). The maker —
    91	[`../roles/lead-solutions-architect.md`](../roles/lead-solutions-architect.md)
    92	(makes the change; the lane's scope — the glossary's *simple change* —
    93	is the lead shop's own definitions, one instance of them, and the
    94	tools that render them, and this role holds those: the compilers and
    95	the lint are its apparatus, as the skill-rendering and role-rendering
    96	processes record). The checker and recorder —
    97	[`../roles/lead-pm.md`](../roles/lead-pm.md) (checks the change
    98	against the definition, records the verified result, and alone
    99	changes the request's route or status — the request typedef's writer
   100	rules; the role the authority holds in person). The maker is fixed in
   101	`make`'s run-by, not passed in: a step's run-by is a literal role id
   102	under the process-definition typedef, and fixing both `make`'s and
   103	`check`'s roles is what makes *the checker is never the maker* a
   104	mechanical fact of this definition rather than a value a caller could
   105	set to the same role. A change whose maker must be another role is a
   106	change to this definition — a make step for that role — filed as the
   107	definition gap it is, never a run-time substitution. The cold-reviewer
   108	role has no step here: no check of record is run.
   109	
   110	**Carried by:** `.claude/skills/small-change/SKILL.md` — generated
   111	from this definition by
   112	[`../tools/compile_process.py`](../tools/compile_process.py), never
   113	edited by hand, and placed at the skills load point by the
   114	[skill-rendering](skill-rendering.md) process once this definition
   115	stands approved. Until that run the carrier does not exist, so its
   116	correspondence cannot be walked at screening time: skill-rendering's
   117	own check — a fresh render diffed against what stands — is that check.

   170	## Data
   171	
   172	Each entry names a process-local value. Simple types use JSON Schema
   173	names inline; conditions are CEL expressions over these names. Paths
   174	are relative to the lead shop's repository root, the execution's working
   175	directory. The feature named over Outcomes is the definition of good
   176	the scenarios are cited from. *Simple change*,
   177	*small-change lane*, *request* (the received-ask sense), and *check of
   178	record* are the [glossary](../glossary.md)'s terms; `glossary` is
   179	declared as data so that `define` judges simpleness against the term's
   180	one home and loads nothing else.
   181	
   182	`request` is the path of the request the lane takes up — an instance
   183	of the [request typedef](../artifacts/request.md)'s received-ask path,
   184	its `route` `small-change`. This process runs as a sub-process of the
   185	[request-intake](request-intake.md) process: its `open-lane` step maps
   186	`request` to this parameter and receives this execution's `result` as
   187	`change`; the execution is tracked on the work item that intake's `land`
   188	step opened for the routed ask and wrote to the request's `work-item`
   189	field. The work item lives in the work register, `bd` — the beads
   190	tracker; `bd close <item> --reason` closes an item with its reason.
   191	The lane owns that item once it takes up the request: `read-anchor`
   192	reads it from the request — a request without one is a failed step
   193	there, before any work — and every exit runs `bd close` on it with a
   194	reason citing the request's id, read by `read-id`; no intake step
   195	closes it. `routed-to` is intake's to write, on the lane's return.
   196	The intake is the lane's one caller: the request typedef names the
   197	lane as where the route leads, and no bead of its own is defined here.
   198	The execution's `result` is `change`: the request's Result section by
   199	fragment, `<request>#result` — the one place the definition, the check,
   200	and the verified result stand, and what the request typedef's
   201	`routed-to` points at for a simple change. `change` is the lane's
   202	record on every exit: the verified result on done, or the reason the
   203	lane has none on the other two. The Change made entry's `changed`
   204	list is what a reader follows from there to the artifacts.
   205	`produces` is empty because the execution creates no
   206	artifact — the change lands in artifacts of their own types, and the
   207	request already exists.
   208	
   209	**Where the definition lives.** The change's definition is written on
   210	the request itself, in its Result section (the typedef's section 4),
   211	and nowhere else. The alternative — a file of its own under a
   212	`changes/` directory referencing the request — was not taken, under
   213	`single-source-of-truth` and the feature's constraints C1 and C3: a
   214	separate file is a second home that must reference the request and be
   215	referenced back, an artifact with no type (the initiative's no-go: no
   216	new types), and a document a reader must find from the request rather
   217	than read on it. On the request, the definition references the request
   218	by being part of it — it opens with the request's id — and every
   219	later entry (the change made, the check, the verified result) sits
   220	beside it, so the request reads as one record from the originator's
   221	words to the demonstrated effect. `change` names that section, and
   222	the intake process's `record-route` step writes it into the request's
   223	`routed-to` — the typedef's "a change definition" as the place the
   224	route led. The Result section carries four entries under headings of this process's naming:
   225	*Definition* (written by `define`), *Change made* (by `make`, one entry
   226	per round), *Check* (by `check`, one per round), and *Verified result*
   227	(by `record`) — or, on an exit before the result, the entry `reroute`
   228	or `hand-back` writes.
   229	
   230	**What a step reads and writes.** A step reads only what it lists.
   231	`paths` is the list of artifacts the definition names, by path —
   232	`define` returns it alongside the definition, `make` reads them as its
   233	declared input and writes to them, and `check` reads them; a
   234	definition that names a rendering — a skill, a subagent file, a
   235	compiled diagram — lists the rendering's source and the rendering
   236	tool in `paths` as well, since `make` reads what `paths` names and
   237	nothing else, and a rendering is re-rendered, never edited; `changed`
   238	is the list of paths `make` changed in a round, returned by `make` and
   239	read by `check`, which fails a path in `changed` that is not in
   240	`paths`. The artifacts' own Document History rows carry each change,
   241	citing the request by id: the request's Change made entry references
   242	the changed artifacts, the artifacts reference the request, and
   243	neither restates the other. A definition or typedef is amended under its owner's
   244	decision: for a definition the product authority owns, the decision is
   245	the route recorded on the request by the lead-pm role, on the
   246	authority's ruling recorded in
   247	[adr-2026-09-04-request-front-end](../../decisions/adr-2026-09-04-request-front-end.md)
   248	that the lead-pm is an extension of the authority; the history row
   249	names the request. A rendering — a skill, a subagent file, a compiled
   250	diagram — is never edited by hand; it is re-rendered from its source,
   251	the rendering processes' rule. `make` never edits the definition: a
   252	definition found wrong is a `check` finding that stands to the cap,
   253	and the failed exit returns the request to routing.
   254	
   255	**Verification.** The *verifying observation* the definition names is
   256	one command, run from the repository root, whose exit status 0 shows
   257	the change's effect in the running system — a lint over the corpus, a
   258	compiler's check, a check step of another process, a headless
   259	fresh-session demonstration — and whose output is the evidence.
   260	`verify` runs it as `observation` and yields `evidence`: the command's
   261	output lines, standard error merged, then one closing line `exit <n>`
   262	carrying the command's exit status. The observation's nonzero exit is
   263	a failed verification — a finding routed back to `make` under the
   264	cap, never a halted run — while a nonzero exit of a `run` step itself
   265	(the shell failing, the bead missing, a close refused) is a failed
   266	step, not an empty result: the execution halts at that step and the failure
   267	is reported to the lead-pm role, the sibling processes' rule. `round`
   268	counts make rounds and is shared by the check and the verification:
   269	a fail from either advances it, and `round_cap` bounds both together.
   270	
   271	**Exit states.** Each exit writes its state on the request, closes
   272	the work item, and returns `change` to the caller; the evidence
   273	stands on the request, so the close reason cites the request's id
   274	rather than copying it, and a close that exits nonzero is a failed
   275	step. *Done*: the request's
   276	status `done`, written by `record` — the typedef names this step as
   277	that status's writer. *Not simple*: the route changed to `discovery`
   278	with the reason, status unchanged (`routed`), written by `reroute` —
   279	the typedef's rule that a later change of route is the lead-pm
   280	role's; no artifact touched; the request, its route now discovery, is
   281	the discovery conversation's input, opened on it by the intake
   282	process's dispatch on the execution the lead-pm role enters with the
   283	request. *Failed*: the findings standing at the cap recorded on the
   284	request, its route set to `awaiting` with the reason and its status
   285	to `recorded`, so the request is again visible as awaiting its route
   286	and the intake process's route step takes it up; the changed artifacts
   287	are not reverted — a revert is itself a change to governed artifacts
   288	and belongs to the route decided next, and the request records
   289	exactly what stands.
   290	`check_verdict` is `pass` or `fail`; `check_finding` is the statement
   291	or rule that fails, quoted, empty on pass. `not_simple` and `reason`
   292	are `define`'s finding that the change is not simple by the glossary's
   293	test — the lead-pm role judged it simple at routing, and the definer
   294	may find otherwise once the change is defined, the feature's rule.
   295	
   296	```yaml
   297	data:
   298	  request: {type: string, format: uri-reference}
   299	  change: {type: string, format: uri-reference}
   300	  request_id: {type: string}
   301	  work_item: {type: string}
   302	  glossary: {type: string, format: uri-reference, initial: basis/glossary.md}
   303	  not_simple: {type: boolean, initial: false}

   309	  check_finding: {type: string, initial: ""}
   310	  evidence: {type: array, items: {type: string}, initial: []}

   312	  round_cap: {type: integer, initial: 3}
   313	```

   315	## Steps
   316	
   317	```yaml
   318	start: name-result
   319	parameters: [request]
   320	result: change
   321	steps:
   322	  - id: name-result
   323	    name: Name the request's Result section as the execution's result
   324	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   325	    inputs: [request]
   326	    set:
   327	      change: request + "#result"
   328	    next: read-id
   329	
   330	  - id: read-id
   331	    name: Read the request's id
   332	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   333	    inputs: [request]
   334	    outputs: [request_id]
   335	    run: |
   336	      sed -n 's/^id: //p' ${request} | head -1 | grep .
   337	    next: read-anchor
   338	
   339	  - id: read-anchor
   340	    name: Read the work item the request names
   341	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   342	    inputs: [request]
   343	    outputs: [work_item]
   344	    run: |
   345	      sed -n 's/^work-item: //p' ${request} | head -1 | grep .
   346	    next: define
   347	
   348	  - id: define
   349	    name: Define the change
   350	    run-by: {role: lead-po, execution: agent}
   351	    inputs: [request, glossary]
   352	    outputs: [request, not_simple, reason, observation, paths]
   353	    prompt: |
   354	      Read the request — its sections 1 to 3: the originator's words,
   355	      the reader, the route and its reason — and the entry for simple
   356	      change in the glossary at glossary; nothing else. First judge
   357	      the change by that entry. If it is not a simple change, return
   358	      not_simple true with the reason, observation and paths empty,
   359	      and write nothing on the request: the lane makes no change for it.
   360	      Otherwise write the change's definition into the request's
   361	      Result section under the heading Definition, opening with the
   362	      request's id: what will be different when the change is done, as
   363	      one or more Given/When/Then acceptance statements a checker can
   364	      decide against the changed artifacts; the artifacts the change
   365	      touches, by path — where one is a rendering, its source and the
   366	      rendering tool listed with it, since the maker reads what paths
   367	      names and nothing else; the maker role — lead-solutions-architect, the
   368	      role the make step runs by; and the verifying observation — one
   369	      command, run from the repository root, whose exit 0 shows the
   370	      effect in the running system and whose output is the evidence.
   371	      The definition says what, never how, and stands before any
   372	      change: you touch no artifact but the request. Bump the
   373	      request's version with the history row. Return the request,
   374	      not_simple false, reason empty, observation as the command
   375	      exactly as written, and paths as the artifacts the definition
   376	      names.
   377	    next: route-definition
   378	
   379	  - id: route-definition
   380	    name: Route on the definition
   381	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   382	    inputs: [not_simple]
   383	    branches:
   384	      - label: "not-simple exit: the change is not simple — routed to discovery, no change made"
   385	        when: not_simple
   386	        next: reroute
   387	      - else: make
   388	
   389	  - id: make
   390	    name: Make the change
   391	    run-by: {role: lead-solutions-architect, execution: agent}
   392	    inputs: [request, paths, check_finding, evidence, round]
   393	    outputs: [request, changed]
   394	    prompt: |
   395	      Read the Definition in the request's Result section and the
   396	      artifacts at paths — nothing else; the definition is not yours
   397	      to edit, and paths is the whole of what you may change. Make
   398	      the change so that every acceptance statement holds, through
   399	      each artifact's own producing rules: a definition or typedef
   400	      amended under the decision the request records, with its
   401	      Document History row citing the request by id and its version
   402	      bumped; its schema changed where the definition names one; a
   403	      tool changed together with the definition that names it; a
   404	      rendering never edited by hand — re-rendered by the tool paths
   405	      names from the source paths names. Make nothing the definition
   406	      does not cover. When
   407	      check_finding is not empty this is a repair round: repair what
   408	      it quotes. When evidence is not empty and check_finding is, the
   409	      verifying observation failed: read the evidence and repair the
   410	      cause. Write under Change made in the request's Result section,
   411	      one entry for this round: your role as maker, every path
   412	      changed with its version before and after, and round. Return
   413	      the request and changed — the paths you changed this round.
   414	    next: check
   415	
   416	  - id: check
   417	    name: Check the change against the definition
   418	    run-by: {role: lead-pm, execution: agent}
   419	    inputs: [request, paths, changed, round]
   420	    outputs: [request, check_verdict, check_finding]
   421	    prompt: |
   422	      Read the Definition and the Change made entry for round in the
   423	      request's Result section, and the artifacts at paths and at
   424	      changed — nothing else. You are not the maker: the make step runs by
   425	      another role, and your verdict is judged against the definition
   426	      alone, not against what you would have made. For each acceptance
   427	      statement, decide whether the changed artifacts satisfy it; for
   428	      each change, whether it went through the artifact's own
   429	      producing rules — the history row citing the request, the
   430	      version bumped, no rendering edited by hand, nothing made that
   431	      the definition does not cover — a path in changed that is not in
   432	      paths fails this rule. Verdict pass only when every
   433	      statement holds and every change conforms; otherwise fail, with
   434	      check_finding the statement or rule that fails, quoted, and what
   435	      fails it. Write under Check in the Result section: the verdict,
   436	      your role, round, and the finding. Return the request, the
   437	      verdict, and the finding — empty on pass.
   438	    next: route-check
   439	
   440	  - id: route-check
   441	    name: Route on the check
   442	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   443	    inputs: [check_verdict, round, round_cap]
   444	    branches:
   445	      - label: "success exit: check passed — verify"
   446	        when: check_verdict == "pass"
   447	        next: verify
   448	      - label: "failsafe exit: round >= round_cap — failed, returned to routing"
   449	        when: round >= round_cap
   450	        next: hand-back
   451	      - else: advance-round
   452	
   453	  - id: verify
   454	    name: Run the verifying observation
   455	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   456	    inputs: [observation]
   457	    outputs: [evidence]
   458	    run: |
   459	      ( ${observation} ) 2>&1
   460	      printf 'exit %s\n' "$?"
   461	    next: route-verify
   462	
   463	  - id: route-verify
   464	    name: Route on the verification
   465	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   466	    inputs: [evidence, round, round_cap]
   467	    branches:
   468	      - label: "success exit: the observation exited 0 — the effect is demonstrated"
   469	        when: size(evidence) > 0 && evidence[size(evidence) - 1] == "exit 0"
   470	        next: record
   471	      - label: "failsafe exit: round >= round_cap — failed, returned to routing"
   472	        when: round >= round_cap
   473	        next: hand-back
   474	      - else: advance-round
   475	
   476	  - id: advance-round
   477	    name: Advance the round
   478	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   479	    inputs: [round]
   480	    set:
   481	      round: round + 1
   482	    next: make
   483	
   484	  - id: record
   485	    name: Record the verified result
   486	    run-by: {role: lead-pm, execution: agent}
   487	    inputs: [request, evidence]
   488	    outputs: [request]
   489	    prompt: |
   490	      Read the request's Result section and evidence. Write under
   491	      Verified result: the verifying observation the Definition named,
   492	      its evidence — the output lines and the closing exit 0 — the
   493	      date, and your role; state that the Definition, the Check's
   494	      verdict by your role, and this result stand, and that between
   495	      the request and this result no bet was taken and no check of
   496	      record was run. Set the request's status to done; bump its
   497	      version with the history row. Return the request.
   498	    next: close-done
   499	
   500	  - id: close-done
   501	    name: Close the work item as done
   502	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   503	    inputs: [work_item, request_id]
   504	    run: |
   505	      bd close ${work_item} --reason "done: ${request_id}"
   506	    next: end
   507	
   508	  - id: reroute
   509	    name: Route the request to discovery
   510	    run-by: {role: lead-pm, execution: agent}
   511	    inputs: [request, reason]
   512	    outputs: [request]
   513	    prompt: |
   514	      Change the request's route to discovery, with reason as its
   515	      route-reason — the status unchanged; write under the Result
   516	      section that the lane defined no change and made none, with the
   517	      reason. The originator reads the changed route and its reason
   518	      from the request, as the first route was read. Bump the
   519	      request's version with the history row. Return the request.
   520	    next: close-not-simple
   521	
   522	  - id: close-not-simple
   523	    name: Close the work item as not simple
   524	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   525	    inputs: [work_item, request_id]
   526	    run: |
   527	      bd close ${work_item} --reason "not simple: routed to discovery — ${request_id}"
   528	    next: end
   529	
   530	  - id: hand-back
   531	    name: Return the request to routing
   532	    run-by: {role: lead-pm, execution: agent}
   533	    inputs: [request, check_verdict, check_finding, evidence, round]
   534	    outputs: [request]
   535	    prompt: |
   536	      The lane reached its round cap with the change unverified. Write
   537	      under the Result section the findings standing at the cap — the
   538	      last check_verdict with check_finding, and evidence from the
   539	      last round in which the observation ran, with that round named;
   540	      where the cap fell on a check, evidence is from an earlier round
   541	      or empty, and you say which — and round; set the request's route to
   542	      awaiting with the reason "small-change lane failed at the round
   543	      cap" and its status to recorded, so the request is again visible
   544	      as awaiting its route. Revert nothing: the changed artifacts'
   545	      histories stand as the maker wrote them, and the request records
   546	      what stands. Bump the request's version with the history row.
   547	      Return the request.
   548	    next: close-failed
   549	
   550	  - id: close-failed
   551	    name: Close the work item as failed
   552	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   553	    inputs: [work_item, request_id]
   554	    run: |
   555	      bd close ${work_item} --reason "failed at the cap: returned to routing — ${request_id}"
   556	    next: end
