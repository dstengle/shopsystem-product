    13	# Artifact type: brief
    14	
    15	## Identity and ancestry
    16	
    17	- **Type:** `brief` — the PO role's bounded statement of a problem and
    18	  its scope for a Bounded Context shop: what the shop is asked for,
    19	  which framing it serves, what is in and out, and what the shop must
    20	  know to start. A request, not a specification: it names the problem
    21	  and the outcome, never the how.
    22	- **Produced by:** the [PO role](../roles/lead-po.md), from the PM
    23	  role's framing; checked by the
    24	  [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md) against the
    25	  [brief fitness set](../fitness/brief.fitness.md). **Consumed by:** the
    26	  receiving Bounded Context shop; the PM role at the check.
    27	
    28	## Required frontmatter
    29	
    30	`type: brief`, `id`, `status` (draft | checked | returned |
    31	pending-definition), `version`, `date`, `reader` (the
    32	receiving shop), `framing` (link to the framing it serves), `owner`,
    33	`created`, `updated`.
    34	
    35	## Required sections
    36	
    37	1. **What is requested** — the request ancestor's first section: the
    38	   first paragraph states what problem the shop is asked to solve and
    39	   the outcome the framing states.
    40	2. **From whom** — the request ancestor's second section: the
    41	   receiving shop, as `reader` names it.
    42	3. **Scope** — what is in; what is out, each exclusion with its reason;
    43	   for each neighbouring piece of work the framing or this brief names,
    44	   in or out or the rule that decides.
    45	4. **What the shop needs** — every term defined here or in the framing,
    46	   every referenced artifact linked, and every question the framing or
    47	   this brief raises either answered or listed as open.
    48	
    49	## Rules
    50	
    51	- The brief names no technology, structure, or interface form the shop
    52	  would otherwise choose; it may name the interaction types the
    53	  behavior must hold on, from the
    54	  [core-task list](../experience/core-tasks.md) — a what, not a form.
    55	- Declined scope carries its reason.
    56	
    57	## Commitment (Definition of Done)
    58	
    59	A brief is done when it has passed the PO output check against the
    60	brief fitness set and the framing. **Consequence on failure:** it is
    61	returned to the PO role with the criterion named, and no shop receives
    62	it.
    63	
    64	## Sources
    65	
    66	The [brief fitness set](../fitness/brief.fitness.md), whose scenarios
    67	this typedef inherits section by section; the [request typedef](request.md) as
    68	ancestor; the [decision-brief typedef](decision-brief.md)'s answer-first
    69	opening.
    70	
    71	## Derived review checklist
    72	
    73	- The first paragraph states problem and outcome; the reader named. *(§Required sections 1–2; fitness 5)*
    74	- No how; interaction types named as what. *(§Rules; fitness 2)*
    75	- Every neighbour the framing or brief names is placed. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 3)*
    76	- Terms, links, and open questions complete. *(§Required sections 4; fitness 4)*
    77	- Declined scope reasoned. *(§Rules; fitness 1)*

    13	# Artifact type: product-decision-record
    14	
    15	## Identity and ancestry
    16	
    17	- **Type:** `product-decision-record` — the record of one product-level
    18	  decision and its reasons: what was decided, by which role under which
    19	  right, against which alternatives, with what consequences, and how
    20	  hard it is to reverse. The product-side counterpart of the
    21	  architecture decision record.
    22	- **Produced by:** the [PO role](../roles/lead-po.md), for a decision the
    23	  PM role or the PO role has taken; checked by the
    24	  [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md) against the
    25	  [product-decision-record fitness set](../fitness/product-decision-record.fitness.md).
    26	  **Consumed by:** every role whose later decision the record bounds;
    27	  Bounded Context shops; the PM role at the check.
    28	
    29	## Required frontmatter
    30	
    31	`type: product-decision-record`, `id`, `status` (draft | checked |
    32	returned | pending-definition | superseded — replaces `checked` when the PO role
    33	files a later record naming this one as superseded), `version`, `date`,
    34	`decided-by` (the role), `right` (the decision right exercised, or
    35	`escalation`, in which case §1 names the escalation that settled it), `owner`, `created`, `updated`.
    36	
    37	## Required sections
    38	
    39	1. **Decision** — exactly one decision, as a sentence a reader can act
    40	   on.
    41	2. **Alternatives** — at least one the deciding role could have chosen,
    42	   with the reason it was not.
    43	3. **Consequences** — each: what changes, for whom, and what it costs or
    44	   forecloses.
    45	4. **Reversibility** — how hard the decision is to reverse and, if hard,
    46	   what would trigger revisiting it.
    47	
    48	## Rules
    49	
    50	- Whether the named role held the right it exercised is the PM role's
    51	  ruling at the check, not the record's claim.
    52	- A superseding record links the one it supersedes.
    53	
    54	## Commitment (Definition of Done)
    55	
    56	A record is done when it has passed the PO output check against its
    57	fitness set and the framing. **Consequence on failure:** it is returned with the
    58	criterion named and binds nothing.
    59	
    60	## Sources
    61	
    62	Nygard's architecture decision record (decision, consequences); MADR
    63	(considered alternatives); decider and reversibility as the shop's own
    64	additions, from the roles' decision rights; the
    65	[product-decision-record fitness set](../fitness/product-decision-record.fitness.md).
    66	
    67	## Derived review checklist
    68	
    69	- One decision, actionable. *(§Required sections 1; fitness 1)*
    70	- A real alternative with its reason. *(§Required sections 2; fitness 2)*
    71	- Decider and right, or the escalation, named. *(§Required frontmatter; fitness 3)*
    72	- Each consequence names what changes, for whom, at what cost. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 4)*
    73	- Reversibility and its trigger stated. *(§Required sections 4; fitness 5)*

    13	# Artifact type: acceptance-scenarios
    14	
    15	## Identity and ancestry
    16	
    17	- **Type:** `acceptance-scenarios` — the set of Gherkin scenarios that
    18	  states, as requirements, what counts as done for one behavior; the
    19	  set the PO role submits and the owning Bounded Context shop executes.
    20	  The scenarios are executable by the shop; the set as a document is
    21	  judged by the [acceptance-scenarios fitness set](../fitness/acceptance-scenarios.fitness.md),
    22	  never executed there.
    23	- **Produced by:** the [PO role](../roles/lead-po.md) with the owning
    24	  shop (scope and wording from the PO; steps and edge cases from the
    25	  shop; context ownership and feasibility from the solutions architect;
    26	  usability and accessibility criteria from the product designer);
    27	  checked by the [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md).
    28	  **Consumed by:** the owning shop's scenario register; the solutions
    29	  architect role for assignment; the
    30	  [interaction conformance check](../processes/interaction-conformance-check.md)
    31	  for the interaction types named.
    32	
    33	## Required frontmatter
    34	
    35	`type: acceptance-scenarios`, `id`, `status` (draft | checked |
    36	returned | pending-definition | assigned — replaces `checked` when the
    37	solutions architect role assigns the set), `version`, `date`,
    38	`behavior` (the behavior's name), `context` (the owning Bounded
    39	Context), `framing` (link), `owner`, `created`, `updated`.
    40	
    41	## Required sections
    42	
    43	1. **Contributors** — the owning shop named as the source of steps and
    44	   edge cases; where the behavior has an interaction, the product
    45	   designer role's usability acceptance criteria and the accessibility
    46	   criteria.
    47	2. **Interaction types** — those the behavior must hold on, from the
    48	   [core-task list](../experience/core-tasks.md), where the
    49	   behavior has an interaction.
    50	3. **Scenarios** — each with a tag carrying the set's `behavior` and
    51	   `context` values and a hash field; Given/When/Then with one action or event
    52	   in the When and an outcome observable in the running system in the
    53	   Then; no step names an implementation detail.
    54	4. **Edges** — each failure or boundary case the framing or the shop's
    55	   contribution stated in §1 names, as a scenario above or marked out of scope with
    56	   a reason.
    57	
    58	## Rules
    59	
    60	- The hash is of the scenario's text; a changed text is a new
    61	  scenario. Whether the hash matches is a mechanical check, to be filed
    62	  as a lint.
    63	- A scenario written without the owning shop is not co-produced and
    64	  fails the check.
    65	
    66	## Commitment (Definition of Done)
    67	
    68	A set is done when it has passed the PO output check against its
    69	fitness set and the framing. **Consequence on failure:** it is returned
    70	with the criterion named and no scenario enters a register.
    71	
    72	## Sources
    73	
    74	Gherkin (Given/When/Then, tags); Cucumber's guidance on the three
    75	amigos (co-production); the experience principles `core-task-parity`
    76	and `accessible-by-standard`; the
    77	[acceptance-scenarios fitness set](../fitness/acceptance-scenarios.fitness.md).
    78	
    79	## Derived review checklist
    80	
    81	- Each scenario one observable behavior, no how. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 1)*
    82	- Owning shop and both designer criteria present. *(§Required sections 1; fitness 2)*
    83	- Tag and hash present on every scenario. *(§Required sections 3; §Rules; fitness 3)*
    84	- Named edges covered or excluded with reason. *(§Required sections 4; fitness 4)*
    85	- Interaction types named. *(§Required sections 2; fitness 5)*

    16	# Fitness set: brief
    17	
    18	A brief is the PO role's bounded statement of a problem and its scope
    19	for a Bounded Context shop (the typedef is pending on this branch; the
    20	frozen corpus on `main` is the reference). These scenarios are the
    21	criteria set the [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md)
    22	screens a brief against, alongside the PM role's framing, which is
    23	always the criterion named `framing`. They are evaluated by the
    24	`cold-reviewer` role, never executed — no step definitions exist or
    25	will exist. The judge's model and prompt version are recorded with each
    26	round verdict.
    27	The judge reads only the criteria set, the framing, and the artifact;
    28	every scenario therefore asks for what the artifact itself carries, and
    29	a fact it must carry — a term's definition, a reference, a reason — is
    30	what these scenarios make it carry. These scenarios stand in for the
    31	pending typedef and are its first draft; the typedef inherits them.
    32	
    33	## Scenarios
    34	
    35	Scenario 1: declined scope carries its reason
    36	  Given each statement the brief marks out of scope
    37	  When it is read
    38	  Then it carries the reason it is out of scope
    39	
    40	Scenario 2: the problem, not the solution
    41	  Given the brief's statement of what the shop is asked for
    42	  When it is parsed for design or implementation choices
    43	  Then it states the problem and the outcome and names no how — no
    44	  technology, structure, or interface form the shop would otherwise
    45	  choose; naming an interaction type the behavior must hold on is a
    46	  what, not a form
    47	
    48	Scenario 3: scope has an edge
    49	  Given the pieces of work the framing or the brief itself names as
    50	  neighbouring
    51	  When each is read against the scope
    52	  Then the brief says whether it is in or out, or names the rule that
    53	  decides
    54	
    55	Scenario 4: the shop can act from it alone
    56	  Given the brief without its author present
    57	  When the receiving shop reads it
    58	  Then every term is defined in the brief or the framing, every
    59	  referenced artifact is linked, and every question the framing or the brief
    60	  itself raises is either answered or listed as open
    61	
    62	Scenario 5: the first paragraph states the problem and the outcome
    63	  Given the brief alone
    64	  When the reader stops after the first paragraph
    65	  Then the problem and the outcome are already stated (this set's
    66	  proposal for the typedef, after the decision-brief form)
    67	
    68	## Compile mapping (each Then → one judge-rubric assertion)
    69	
    70	| Scenario Then | Judge-rubric assertion |
    71	|---|---|
    72	| 1 — declined scope reasoned | "For each out-of-scope mark: is a reason stated? Cite any without one." |
    73	| 2 — problem, not solution | "List every sentence that chooses a technology, structure, or interface form (an interaction type named is not a form). Empty list = pass." |
    74	| 3 — scope has an edge | "For each neighbouring piece of work the framing or brief names: does the brief say in or out, or name the deciding rule? Cite." |
    75	| 4 — actionable alone | "List undefined terms, unlinked references, and questions the framing or brief raises that are neither answered nor listed as open. Empty list = pass." |
    76	| 5 — first paragraph | "Does paragraph 1 state the problem and the outcome? Quote it, or answer no." |

    16	# Fitness set: acceptance scenarios
    17	
    18	An acceptance scenario is a Gherkin scenario that states, as a
    19	requirement, what counts as done for one behavior; a set of them is
    20	what the PO role submits for a behavior (the acceptance scenarios
    21	themselves are executable by the owning shop; this fitness set judges
    22	their quality and is never executed). These scenarios are the criteria
    23	set the [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md) screens a
    24	submitted set against, alongside the framing (criterion `framing`).
    25	Evaluated by the `cold-reviewer` role. The judge's model and prompt
    26	version are recorded with each round verdict.
    27	The judge reads only the criteria set, the framing, and the artifact;
    28	every scenario therefore asks for what the artifact itself carries, and
    29	a fact it must carry — a term's definition, a reference, a reason — is
    30	what these scenarios make it carry. These scenarios stand in for the
    31	pending acceptance-scenario typedef and are its first draft; the
    32	typedef inherits them (the co-production statement and the tag's
    33	content are what this set asks the typedef to carry).
    34	
    35	## Scenarios
    36	
    37	Scenario 1: each scenario is one observable behavior
    38	  Given a scenario in the set
    39	  When its Given/When/Then is read
    40	  Then the When is one action or event, the Then an outcome observable
    41	  in the running system, and no step names an implementation detail
    42	
    43	Scenario 2: co-produced with the owning shop
    44	  Given the set's statement of who contributed
    45	  When it is read
    46	  Then the owning Bounded Context shop is named as the source of the
    47	  steps and edge cases, and, where the behavior has an interaction, the
    48	  product designer role's usability acceptance criteria and the
    49	  accessibility criteria are present (the lead-product-designer role's
    50	  accountabilities; the experience principle `accessible-by-standard`)
    51	
    52	Scenario 3: identity fields are present
    53	  Given each scenario
    54	  When its header is read
    55	  Then it carries a tag naming the behavior and its Bounded Context and
    56	  a hash field (whether the hash matches the text is a mechanical
    57	  check to be filed as a lint, not this judge's)
    58	
    59	Scenario 4: the named edges are covered
    60	  Given the failure and boundary cases the framing or the set's stated
    61	  shop contribution names
    62	  When each is read against the set
    63	  Then it is a scenario in the set or is marked out of scope with a
    64	  reason
    65	
    66	Scenario 5: interaction types are named
    67	  Given a behavior a person or agent reaches through an interaction
    68	  When the set is read
    69	  Then it names the interaction types the behavior must hold on (the
    70	  experience principle `core-task-parity`)
    71	
    72	## Compile mapping (each Then → one judge-rubric assertion)
    73	
    74	| Scenario Then | Judge-rubric assertion |
    75	|---|---|
    76	| 1 — one observable behavior | "For each scenario: is the When one action, the Then observable in the running system, and no step implementation-specific? Cite any failing scenario." |
    77	| 2 — co-produced | "Is the owning shop named as the source of steps and edge cases? Where the behavior has an interaction, are usability and accessibility criteria present? Cite or name the absence." |
    78	| 3 — identity fields | "For each scenario: tag naming behavior and context present? hash field present? Cite any scenario without both." |
    79	| 4 — named edges covered | "For each failure or boundary case the framing or the set's stated shop contribution names: cite the scenario or the out-of-scope note. Any uncovered = fail." |
    80	| 5 — interaction types named | "Does the set name the interaction types the behavior must hold on? Cite the sentence or its absence." |

    16	# Fitness set: backlog order
    17	
    18	A backlog order is the PO role's ordered list of requirements within
    19	the PM role's framing — the PO role's exclusive decision — as submitted for
    20	the PM role's check. These scenarios are the criteria set the
    21	[PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md) screens an order
    22	against, alongside the framing (criterion `framing`). Evaluated by the
    23	`cold-reviewer` role, never executed. The judge's model and prompt
    24	version are recorded with each round verdict.
    25	The judge reads only the criteria set, the framing, and the artifact;
    26	every scenario therefore asks for what the artifact itself carries, and
    27	a fact it must carry — a term's definition, a reference, a reason — is
    28	what these scenarios make it carry. These scenarios stand in for the
    29	pending backlog-order typedef — no definition yet treats the order as an
    30	artifact — and are its first draft; the typedef inherits them. Scenario 1 overlaps the
    31	process's `framing` criterion by design: the process passes one
    32	framing and an order spans many. What the
    33	judge cannot catch from the order alone — a recommendation omitted, a
    34	wrong context — is the solutions architect role's to raise through its
    35	interface with the PO role.
    36	
    37	## Scenarios
    38	
    39	Scenario 1: every item serves a framing
    40	  Given each item in the order
    41	  When its framing reference is read
    42	  Then it names the framing it serves, or is marked declined with a
    43	  reason
    44	
    45	Scenario 2: the order states the priority it follows
    46	  Given the order's statement of the roadmap priority it was made
    47	  against
    48	  When the order is compared with that statement
    49	  Then items serving a higher-priority framing precede items serving a
    50	  lower one, or the order states a reason for each exception
    51	
    52	Scenario 3: enabler work is placed or declined with reasons
    53	  Given the order's list of enabler recommendations received from the
    54	  solutions architect role
    55	  When the order is read
    56	  Then each recommendation appears in the order with its position
    57	  reasoned, or is declined with a reason
    58	
    59	Scenario 4: the order names each item's context
    60	  Given each item
    61	  When it is read
    62	  Then it names the Bounded Context that owns it, and an item crossing
    63	  contexts is marked as such with the escalation to the PM role named
    64	
    65	Scenario 5: the next item says whether it is ready
    66	  Given the first item not yet taken up
    67	  When it is read
    68	  Then the order marks it ready and links the brief or scenario set it
    69	  states is checked (passed the PO output check), or marks it not ready
    70	  and names what it waits on
