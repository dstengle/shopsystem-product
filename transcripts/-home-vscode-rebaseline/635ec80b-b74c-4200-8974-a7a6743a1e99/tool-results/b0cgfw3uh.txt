    12	# Base writing style
    13	
    14	These rules apply to everything written for a human reader, regardless of
    15	document type. Document types add a format layer on top of this base; they
    16	never override it.
    17	
    18	## Goal
    19	
    20	The reader gets the point of any paragraph in one read, any section in 30
    21	seconds, and the whole document in two minutes. Write for the first read.
    22	
    23	## Lead with the point
    24	
    25	At every scale, state the conclusion first and the support after.
    26	
    27	- A document opens with what it found or decided, not how it got there.
    28	- A section opens with its main claim, then evidence.
    29	- A paragraph opens with its point, then elaboration.
    30	
    31	If a document type defines a structure, that structure is just this rule
    32	applied to that type. If no structure is defined, use this rule to invent
    33	one.
    34	
    35	## Detail placement
    36	
    37	Precision serves the point; it never precedes it. Formal specification
    38	language, methods, raw data, scoring tables, and citations go after the
    39	claim they support — or in an appendix. The body is for understanding.
    40	
    41	## Sentences
    42	
    43	- One idea per sentence.
    44	- Active voice, with a named actor: "Reviewers delete unlinked checks,"
    45	  not "Unlinked checks are subject to deletion."
    46	- Prefer the short common word: "use" not "utilize," "end" not
    47	  "terminate," "before" not "prior to."
    48	
    49	## Terminology
    50	
    51	- Never use a metaphor as a technical term. If a word describes a thing
    52	  by what it resembles instead of what it literally does, replace it with
    53	  the literal description. Test: could a competent reader from outside
    54	  the team work out the meaning from the words alone? If not, rewrite.
    55	- Banned words. Never use these, in any form:
    56	  - "load-bearing" → say "other work depends on it" or "critical to
    57	    operations"
    58	  - "surface" (as in context surface, config surface) → say "input,"
    59	    "source," or "channel"
    60	  - "seam" → say "boundary" or "integration point"
    61	  - "scar tissue" → describe the actual problem, e.g. "an unreviewed
    62	    workaround that was never removed"
    63	- Explain every insider reference — a past incident, an internal system,
    64	  a person — in one plain sentence, or cut it. Assume the reader knows
    65	  none of our history.
    66	
    67	## Style examples
    68	
    69	These examples exist only to show sentence style. The domain (a public
    70	library) is deliberately unrelated to our work. Copy the style; never
    71	copy the content or vocabulary into real documents.
    72	
    73	bad:
    74	
    75	> Unreturned inventory constitutes scar tissue on the circulation loop
    76	> and rapidly becomes load-bearing for downstream shelving throughput.
    77	
    78	good:
    79	
    80	> Overdue books pile up at the front desk, and staff spend their time
    81	> reshelving late returns instead of helping visitors.
    82	
    83	The bad sentence uses two metaphors as terms and never names an actor.
    84	The good sentence says who is affected and what actually happens, in
    85	plain words.

    12	# Glossary
    13	
    14	## How the list combines
    15	
    16	The defined-term list is this glossary combined with every schema element
    17	name. Per the `use-defined-terms` principle, a writer choosing between
    18	terms uses one of these when one fits.
    19	
    20	## Terms
    21	
    22	- **artifact type** — a named, schema-defined document type (e.g.
    23	  `decision-brief`); the term the schema registry uses. Not "kind".
    24	- **data type** — a named, schema-defined structure that is not a
    25	  human-readable document (e.g. `review`); passed between process steps.
    26	- **simple type** — a JSON Schema primitive (`string`, `integer`,
    27	  `boolean`, `array`, `object`), usable inline without registration.
    28	- **typedef** — the single source a type is generated from; templates and
    29	  schema fragments are its renderings (owned by shopsystem-knowledge).
    30	- **schema** — the machine-checkable shape of a type: fields, types,
    31	  enums, required sections.
    32	- **process definition** — the source of truth for a process: header
    33	  (purpose, outcomes, roles), data section, steps section.
    34	- **step** — one unit of a process: an agent step (carries a prompt) or a
    35	  runtime step (carries `set`, `run`, or `branches`; no prose).
    36	- **rendering** — a generated output of a definition (a skill, a
    37	  diagram, a template); never edited by hand, never the source of truth.
    38	- **guiding statement** — an optional header element of a process
    39	  definition that directs judgment across the whole process; compiled
    40	  into every rendering.
    41	- **fitness set** — judged (never executed) Given/When/Then scenarios
    42	  scoring an artifact type's quality.
    43	- **guideline** — prose quality rules for an artifact type, each with a
    44	  test, a criterion, and a yes/no decision.
    45	- **role** — a named capability contract and set of accountabilities,
    46	  assigned by process steps to whoever fills it; sequencing lives in
    47	  process definitions, not roles.
    48	- **principle** — a standing rule about how we work: name, statement,
    49	  rationale, implications (see the principles document's opening
    50	  definition).
    51	- **seed layer** — the hand-approved definitions the regress terminates
    52	  at: the principle set plus one typedef per definition document type.
    53	- **owner** — the role that approves changes to a definition; named in
    54	  every definition's frontmatter.
    55	- **definition chain** — the six linked definitions of good for one
    56	  artifact type; required before instances are authored or rewritten.
    57	- **keeper** — a corpus record whose action is keep-rewrite; rewritten
    58	  forward through its type's approved chain, never used as-is.
    59	- **rewrite-forward** — the default for keepers: re-author to the
    60	  approved standard; repair-in-place only for records already at the bar.
    61	- **rebaseline** — Phase 1 of the re-founding arc: the active tree comes
    62	  to hold only chain-conforming records; retired mass moves to the
    63	  archive.
    64	- **scope** (of a principle set) — the level the set governs and whose
    65	  context it loads into: working (every activity), architecture (the
    66	  designed system), or experience (the product's interactions).
    67	- **transcript** — the runtime log of turns; unscoped, not a governed
    68	  artifact, never loaded into context.
    69	- **conversation** — a scoped, bounded discussion attached to exactly one
    70	  anchor; every conversation belongs to a process.
    71	- **anchor** — the governed record a conversation attaches to: a session
    72	  record, a review record, or a work item; carries the conversation's
    73	  state across transcript boundaries.
    74	- **conversation type** — discovery (anchor: a session record), review
    75	  (anchor: a review record and its outcomes), or work (anchor: a work
    76	  item).
    77	- **session record** — the anchor record of a discovery conversation:
    78	  outcome, produced and revised lists, open threads, select quotes.
    79	- **review** — one bounded conversation in which the authority decides
    80	  on presented material. Replaces "sitting".
    81	- **review record** — the anchor record of a review: the material
    82	  presented and the outcomes applied; each decision traces in the
    83	  changed artifacts' Document History, never in a standing ledger.
    84	- **park** — the failsafe for work that cannot pass review within its
    85	  round cap: set aside with a filed finding.
    86	- **round cap** — the declared maximum review rounds before a loop's
    87	  failsafe exit fires.
    88	- **action** — the approved choice for one record: keep-rewrite, keep,
    89	  retire, or terminal, with its target.
    90	- **authority-call** — a row marker on a migration-plan row awaiting a
    91	  decision from the authority; not an action — the row has no effect and
    92	  is out of scope for any run until ruled.
    93	- **action table** — the table of records and their actions; approved row
    94	  by row or in blocks at a review. Drives any bulk record change.
    95	- **close-out** — the mechanical execution of the migration plan's
    96	  pre-decided retire and terminal actions: snapshot, delete, archive
    97	  move, a loud post-check, and the branch promotion; no review loop.
    98	  All stages run consecutively at cut-over.
    99	- **snapshot tag** — the annotated git tag (`pre-migration`) on `main`'s
   100	  pre-execution commit, preserving the full corpus for terminal-recovery;
   101	  after close-out a terminal record exists only there.
   102	- **migration plan** — the rebaseline's action table plus the order of
   103	  the per-type migration runs. Replaces "rebaseline bill".
   104	- **run** — one execution of a process, anchored to a work item; states:
   105	  running, held, done, cancelled.
   106	- **hold** — a run state: paused with its step and data preserved in its
   107	  anchor, by inactivity or by an ask; a held run is resumed or
   108	  cancelled, never dropped.
   109	- **checkpoint** — updating a conversation's anchor when a transcript
   110	  ends mid-conversation; a checkpoint is not a close.
   111	- **branched conversation** — a conversation run as a sub-process of
   112	  another run; its anchor records the parent (`branched-from`).
   113	- **Bounded Context** — a deliberately drawn region of the product with
   114	  one internal language, one model, and one set of contracts to other
   115	  regions; drawn by design, never discovered after the fact (Evans,
   116	  *Domain-Driven Design*).
   117	- **shop** — the entity that builds, operates, and evolves part of the
   118	  product. Two types: the lead shop (the system-level coordinator; owns
   119	  product-level artifacts and no Bounded Context) and the BC-shop (owns
   120	  and produces exactly one Bounded Context).
   121	- **activity** — a discrete unit of work performed inside a shop,
   122	  following a contract or process, with inputs, outcomes, and provenance
   123	  recorded. What an actor is stays unconstrained; the activity has the
   124	  same shape whoever performs it.
   125	- **contract** — a named, versioned statement of what one entity
   126	  promises another: the schemas exchanged, the meaning of each
   127	  operation, the error behavior, and the relationship kind. Offered as a
   128	  product contract (on a Bounded Context) or an operational contract (on
   129	  a shop); nothing outside a contract is promised.
   130	- **relationship kind** — the named pattern a contract declares for how
   131	  two Bounded Contexts relate, drawn from the context-mapping catalogue
   132	  (Evans, *Domain-Driven Design*): customer–supplier, conformist,
   133	  anti-corruption layer, open host service, published language, shared
   134	  kernel, or separate ways.
   135	- **vehicle** — the message type a request to a Bounded Context shop
   136	  travels in; chosen from the pre-state, never from the request's
   137	  wording.
   138	- **pre-state** — what a Bounded Context actually is before a change,
   139	  read from its contracts and its scenario register, never from its
   140	  internals.
   141	- **framing** — the PM role's recorded statement of what a request is
   142	  about: the originator, the problem taken to be worth solving, and the
   143	  outcome it serves; the exclusive decision of `lead-pm`.
   144	- **acceptance scenario** — a Gherkin scenario that states, as a
   145	  requirement, what counts as done for one behavior; co-produced by the
   146	  PO role and the owning Bounded Context shop; held in that shop's
   147	  scenario register.
   148	- **scenario register** — a Bounded Context shop's own list of the
   149	  acceptance scenarios it holds, with their hashes and states; read in
   150	  full across every context when a claim spans contexts.
   151	- **ask** — a question one activity puts to another role in place of
   152	  its output, carrying a default and a checkpoint; the run holds, the
   153	  role answers, the step resumes — never a wait in place (data type
   154	  `ask`; process-definition typedef §Run lifecycle).
   155	- **clarify** — an ask from a Bounded Context shop to the lead shop on
   156	  scope, vocabulary, structure, or contract; answered by the role whose
   157	  domain the question falls in.
   158	- **decomposition** — the solutions architect's record of which Bounded
   159	  Context owns which capability, and the relationship kind of each
   160	  contract between contexts.
   161	- **enabler work** — technical work that makes a feature possible;
   162	  recommended by the solutions architect, placed in the backlog by the
   163	  PO role.
   164	- **interaction type** — one way a person or an agent reaches the
   165	  product: command line (CLI), full-screen terminal (TUI), graphical or
   166	  web (GUI), API and SDK, conversational, voice, or a generated document
   167	  or notification; each honours its own conventions within one
   168	  experience. The experience guidelines cover conversational and voice
   169	  together as the `assistant` guideline.
   170	- **experience guidance corpus** — the product designer role's
   171	  principle set and guidelines for the experience: design principles,
   172	  vocabulary and voice, core-task parity across interaction types,
   173	  interaction patterns per type, and the accessibility target; the
   174	  role's exclusive domain.
   175	- **originator** — whoever expresses intent at the product's edge: a
   176	  person, a shop, or a system outside the product.
   177	- **generated interface** — an interface whose form the product composes
   178	  at run time from the person's context, within constraints the
   179	  experience guidance corpus states; a form, not an interaction type.
   180	- **assistant interaction** — an interaction in which the product acts on
   181	  an intent the person stated rather than on a command — conversational,
   182	  voice, or an assistant acting for the person (a closed set); the locus
   183	  of control is the product's, so the experience principles bound it.
   184	- **intent** — a desired outcome expressed by an originator at the
   185	  product's edge; enters the product through a contract and keeps its
   186	  provenance through every translation and delegation.

    13	# Working principles (compiled into every session)
    14	
    15	These statements govern every activity in this shop. The full set —
    16	rationales, implications, fitness screen — is the source document
    17	named in the front-matter; on conflict the source wins.
    18	
    19	- **Define what good looks like up front** (`define-good-up-front`):
    20	  - Every activity MUST operate from a stated definition of what good looks like.
    21	  - That definition MUST drive both the performance of the activity and its check.
    22	  - The check MUST sit with a different role holding a different accountability.
    23	- **Govern the generating context** (`governed-context`):
    24	  - Everything loaded into an agent's generating context — prompts, skills, memories, primers — MUST trace to an approved definition or a governed record.
    25	  - An unsanctioned context channel MUST NOT be created or retained.
    26	- **Every activity belongs to a process** (`no-orphan-activities`):
    27	  - Every activity in the system MUST be part of a defined process with stated expected outcomes, expected outputs, and possible resulting actions.
    28	  - Every long-running loop MUST declare its exit — a reached-state success exit, a round or budget cap, or both.
    29	- **Use defined terms** (`use-defined-terms`):
    30	  - Important terms MUST be defined in the system, in the glossary or as a schema element.
    31	  - A term is important when a reader must know it to perform or check the work.
    32	  - When more than one term could carry a statement, the writer MUST use a defined term if one is available.
    33	- **Use external standards first** (`external-standards-first`):
    34	  - A definition MUST adopt an established external form where one fits.
    35	  - Bespoke structure MUST be justified by a recorded gap in the form it rejects.
    36	- **Single source of truth** (`single-source-of-truth`):
    37	  - Every fact, rule, or definition MUST have exactly one authoritative home.
    38	  - Every other appearance MUST be a reference or a generated rendering.
    39	- **Feedback loops have consumers** (`feedback-loops-with-consumers`):
    40	  - Every feedback channel MUST name its consumer and the resulting action.
    41	  - The effectiveness of processes, tools, and prompts MUST be measured.
    42	  - The definitions of processes, tools, and prompts MUST be updated from what is measured.
    43	- **Delivery is verified in the running system** (`delivery-verified`):
    44	  - Work MUST be counted done only when its effect is demonstrated in the running system.
    45	  - Artifacts existing, checks passing, or reviews approving MUST NOT count as done on their own.
    46	- **Load the least context** (`least-context`):
    47	  - An activity MUST load the minimum context necessary to accomplish its task.
    48	  - The activity's process MUST name what loads into context and the source each input comes from.
    49	  - Context from an unapproved source MUST NOT be loaded.

    13	# Data type: ask
    14	
    15	## Purpose
    16	
    17	A question one activity puts to another role, returned by an agent or
    18	human step in place of its outputs. Produced by any step that carries
    19	`asks`; routed by the runtime to whoever fills the role
    20	named in `to`; consumed by that role, which writes `answer`, and by the
    21	asking step, which resumes with the ask in its inputs. The process
    22	declares an `ask` data value (`$ref: ask`) and lists it in each asking
    23	step's inputs. Two resume paths: *answered* — the step acts on
    24	`answer`; *defaulted* — the step acts on `default`, because the
    25	process's `ask-cap` passed unanswered or because the step returned a
    26	second ask in the same run (at most one is allowed). See the
    27	[process-definition typedef](../artifacts/process-definition.md), §The
    28	steps section and §Run lifecycle. A `clarify` — a Bounded Context
    29	shop's question to the lead shop — conforms to this shape.
    30	
    31	## Schema
    32	
    33	```yaml
    34	schema:
    35	  type: object
    36	  fields:
    37	    to: {type: string}            # asker writes: the role asked; a derived check confirms it is in the step's `asks`
    38	    kind:                         # asker writes: what the asker lacks
    39	      type: string
    40	      enum: [pre-state, intent, reserved-decision, scope, vocabulary, structure, contract]
    41	      # pre-state: what a thing is before a change; intent: what the originator wanted;
    42	      # reserved-decision: a decision another role's domain holds; scope: whether
    43	      # something is in or out; vocabulary: what a term means; structure: which
    44	      # Bounded Context owns something; contract: what is promised across a boundary
    45	    question: {type: string}      # asker writes
    46	    default: {type: string}       # asker writes: what the step will do if the ask resolves defaulted
    47	    checkpoint: {type: string}    # asker writes: partial output sufficient to resume from
    48	    answer: {type: string, optional: true}       # answering role writes
    49	    answered_by: {type: string, optional: true}  # runtime writes: the role that answered
    50	    resolution:                                  # runtime writes
    51	      type: string
    52	      enum: [answered, defaulted, cancelled]     # cancelled: the held run was cancelled
    53	      optional: true
    54	```

    12	
    13	# Data type: screen-review
    14	
    15	## Purpose
    16	
    17	One screen round's verdict and findings against a named criteria set.
    18	Produced by the `screen` step of
    19	[`../processes/po-output-check.md`](../processes/po-output-check.md)
    20	and of
    21	[`../processes/interaction-conformance-check.md`](../processes/interaction-conformance-check.md);
    22	consumed by their routing, revision, and decision steps and, in the
    23	first, appended to the round log. Each finding names the criterion it fails — or
    24	`uncovered`, for a defect no criterion names — carries the quoted text,
    25	and states whether the screener could decide it (`confident`) or not
    26	(`wobbly`) — a wobbly finding quotes the whole passage, since the PM
    27	role decides from the review alone. `change` is the repair the screener
    28	proposes for that finding; `top_changes` the three the screener would
    29	make first. The `screen` step writes every field.
    30	
    31	## Schema
    32	
    33	```yaml
    34	schema:
    35	  type: object
    36	  fields:
    37	    verdict: {type: string, enum: [clean, findings]}
    38	    findings:
    39	      type: array
    40	      items:
    41	        type: object
    42	        fields:
    43	          criterion: {type: string}          # the criterion's name (in the PO check "framing" is always one), or the literal "uncovered"; a criterion the corpus cannot decide keeps its name and says "record absent:" in change
    44	          quote: {type: string}
    45	          change: {type: string}
    46	          decidability: {type: string, enum: [confident, wobbly]}
    47	    top_changes: {type: array, items: {type: string}, maxItems: 3}
    48	```

    16	# Fitness set: interaction
    17	
    18	These scenarios are the conformance screen the product designer role
    19	runs on a delivered interaction of any interaction type against the
    20	experience guidance corpus — the [experience principles](../experience-principles.md),
    21	the [common experience guideline](../guidelines/experience-common.md),
    22	and the per-type guideline for the interaction's type. They are judged
    23	by the `lead-product-designer` role, never executed. The judge's model
    24	and prompt version (or the person's name) are recorded with each
    25	verdict. The process that runs this screen at delivery is not yet
    26	defined; until it is, the product designer role runs it on request from
    27	the [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md) or a Bounded
    28	Context shop; the process is not yet filed as a work item. Where a
    29	scenario judges against a corpus record (under `basis/experience/`)
    30	that is absent, lacks the entry, or whose entry is marked
    31	`hypothesis`, the verdict is "undecidable" with that reason — record
    32	absent, record empty, or entry is a hypothesis — recorded as a finding
    33	against the corpus, never a pass.
    34	
    35	## Scenarios
    36	
    37	Scenario 1: one vocabulary, recorded mappings
    38	  Given every noun and verb the interaction shows or accepts
    39	  When each is compared with the corpus vocabulary
    40	  Then it is the vocabulary's word or a platform mapping the vocabulary
    41	  records
    42	
    43	Scenario 2: the platform's idiom
    44	  Given the platform guideline the corpus names for the interaction
    45	  type
    46	  When the interaction's form is compared with it
    47	  Then each departure is a variation the corpus records with a reason
    48	
    49	Scenario 3: errors guide recovery
    50	  Given each error the interaction can show
    51	  When it is read
    52	  Then it says what happened in the vocabulary and what to do next,
    53	  and any internal identifier stands alongside, not in place
    54	
    55	Scenario 4: core tasks and options hold
    56	  Given the corpus's core-task list and the options other interaction
    57	  types offer for those tasks
    58	  When the interaction is walked
    59	  Then every core task completes and every option offered elsewhere for
    60	  the task is present
    61	
    62	Scenario 5: accessible before delivery
    63	  Given the accessibility target the corpus names for the interaction
    64	  type
    65	  When the delivery's attached accessibility result is read
    66	  Then it exists, meets the target, and for a non-web type carries the
    67	  WCAG2ICT applicability record
    68	
    69	Scenario 6: the per-type rules hold
    70	  Given the per-type experience guideline for the interaction's type
    71	  When each of its rules' tests is run
    72	  Then each rule's criterion is met

    13	# Artifact type: experience-record
    14	
    15	## Identity and ancestry
    16	
    17	- **Type:** `experience-record` — one of the seven records of the
    18	  experience guidance corpus that the experience guidelines and the
    19	  interaction fitness set judge against: the vocabulary and its
    20	  platform mappings, the core-task list, the interaction patterns, the
    21	  design tokens, the hard-to-reverse classification, the persona and
    22	  voice, and the variations record. A record is a definition of good
    23	  for interactions — a table the product designer role maintains and
    24	  a screen reads — not prose.
    25	- **Produced by:** the product designer role, from user research and
    26	  the product's own definitions; every change is approved by the owner
    27	  named in the frontmatter, as for any definition. **Consumed by:** the
    28	  [interaction fitness set](../fitness/interaction.fitness.md)'s judge;
    29	  the experience guidelines' rules; Bounded Context shops building an
    30	  interaction; the PO role, for acceptance criteria.
    31	
    32	## Required frontmatter
    33	
    34	`type: experience-record`, `id`, `record` (one of `vocabulary`,
    35	`core-tasks`, `patterns`, `tokens`, `hard-to-reverse`, `persona-voice`,
    36	`variations` — a closed set), `owner`, `status`, `version`, `created`,
    37	`updated`; `maintained-by: lead-product-designer`. Wherever a record
    38	names interaction types it uses the closed token set `cli`, `tui`,
    39	`gui`, `api` (API and SDK), `conversational`, `voice`, `document` —
    40	the glossary's interaction types, one token each.
    41	
    42	## Required sections
    43	
    44	1. An **opening** stating which record this is, what reads it, and
    45	   what an entry means.
    46	2. **Entries** — one table, columns fixed per record kind, every kind
    47	   ending in `status` (`evidenced` — the source is a user test,
    48	   measured use, or a platform guideline — or `hypothesis` — the entry
    49	   is proposed from a product definition or by design judgment and
    50	   awaits evidence) and, except `variations`, `source`:
    51	   - `vocabulary`: term · meaning ("see glossary" for a glossary term)
    52	     · platform mappings (type: word, only where the platform's word
    53	     differs) · source · status.
    54	   - `core-tasks`: task · what a person or agent accomplishes ·
    55	     interaction types it holds on (every type, unless the next column
    56	     gives a reason) · types it is removed from, with the reason ·
    57	     options every type must offer · source · status.
    58	   - `patterns`: pattern · where it applies · the shape (in words) ·
    59	     platform guideline it follows, by name · source · status.
    60	   - `tokens`: token · value · used for · source · status.
    61	   - `hard-to-reverse`: a product action · why it is hard to reverse ·
    62	     what the assistant states before it · source · status.
    63	   - `persona-voice`: dimension or trait · setting · when it varies ·
    64	     source · status.
    65	   - `variations`: rule varied · interaction type · the variation · the
    66	     reason · recorded by (the guideline or the role; this record's
    67	     evidence is the reason, so it carries no source column) · status.
    68	   A record with no entries yet says so in one sentence under the
    69	   heading. The screen's verdicts: a record that does not exist —
    70	   "undecidable: record absent"; a record with no entry for what the
    71	   rule needs — "undecidable: record empty"; an entry marked
    72	   `hypothesis` — "undecidable: entry is a hypothesis". Each is a
    73	   finding against the corpus, never a pass and never a finding
    74	   against the delivery.
    75	3. **Checks** — which guideline rules and fitness scenarios read this
    76	   record: the document linked and the rule or scenario number.
    77	
    78	## Rules
    79	
    80	- An entry's `source` names the evidence — a user test, a platform
    81	  guideline, a product definition — never a preference
    82	  (`evidence-not-opinion`).
    83	- A term in the vocabulary that is also in the
    84	  [glossary](../glossary.md) carries the glossary's meaning, written
    85	  "see glossary"; the vocabulary adds the platform mappings.
    86	- The variations record is the one home for a per-type departure from
    87	  a corpus rule (`consistent-not-uniform` bullet 4).
    88	
    89	## Commitment (Definition of Done)
    90	
    91	A record is done when the screen that reads it can decide every rule
    92	that names it without asking the product designer role — every entry
    93	the rule needs present and `evidenced`. **Consequence
    94	on failure:** the rules that name it return "undecidable: record
    95	absent" and every interaction they screen carries that finding.
    96	
    97	## Sources
    98	
    99	Design-system practice (Atlassian's tokens as "the single source of
   100	truth"; GOV.UK's design system contribution model); Nielsen Norman
   101	Group's tone-of-voice dimensions (persona-voice); the experience
   102	principle set; the experience guidelines that name each record.
   103	
   104	## Derived review checklist
   105	
   106	- `record` is one of the seven; `maintained-by` present. *(§Required frontmatter)*
   107	- The table's columns match the record kind. *(§Required sections 2)*
   108	- Every entry carries a status, and a source that is evidence or a `hypothesis` mark; variations carry a reason instead of a source. *(§Required sections 2; §Rules)*
   109	- Interaction types use the closed token set. *(§Required frontmatter)*
   110	- The Checks section names at least one rule or scenario. *(§Required sections 3)*

    16	# Lead Product Designer
    17	
    18	You hold the role that owns the product's experience: whether people
    19	and agents can use what the product offers, through whichever
    20	*interaction type* they reach it by — command line (CLI), full-screen
    21	terminal (TUI), graphical or web (GUI), API and SDK, conversational,
    22	voice, or a generated document or notification. The product is one
    23	experience; you are the role that makes it so. The four product risks
    24	are value, usability, feasibility, and viability; usability is yours.
    25	
    26	**Standard of judgment:** consistent, not uniform — the same
    27	vocabulary, the same core tasks, and the same rules everywhere, with
    28	each interaction type honouring its own conventions. Evidence from
    29	people using the product, never opinion. An interface an agent uses
    30	is an interface you design.
    31	
    32	**Accountable for:**
    33	- The experience guidance corpus, a principle set with guidelines in
    34	  the definition-chain form: design principles as decision rules; one
    35	  vocabulary and voice; the rule that every interaction type supports
    36	  the same core tasks — the tasks a person must be able to complete
    37	  wherever the product is reached; interaction patterns per interaction type; the
    38	  accessibility target (WCAG 2.2 AA, with its non-web application for
    39	  terminal and document interfaces).
    40	- Conformance: every delivered interaction screened against the
    41	  corpus, a finding recorded for each departure and its resolution.
    42	- The information architecture and task flows of every interaction
    43	  type, readable from artifacts without asking their author.
    44	- Usability evidence on every candidate (a framed problem or a design
    45	  solution under consideration): what users were observed
    46	  doing, in a user test, a tested prototype, or measured task
    47	  completion, recorded with the framing it bears on.
    48	- Usability acceptance criteria supplied to the PO role for each
    49	  behavior, and the scenarios a usability test invalidates named.
    50	- Agent-facing ergonomics: the naming, predictability, and structure
    51	  of tool definitions, CLIs, and API contracts screened as interfaces,
    52	  with findings to the solutions architect.
    53	
    54	**Domain (exclusive):** the experience guidance corpus — this role
    55	alone decides what it says; conformance is that corpus applied to a
    56	delivered interaction.
    57	
    58	**Decision rights.**
    59	- *Decides:* the corpus and conformance (exclusive); and, as the
    60	  accountable role, the information architecture and task flows of
    61	  each interaction type; which interaction type a capability is
    62	  offered through first — an experience question; the capability's
    63	  worth is the PM role's decision, not this one's; what user research runs and how
    64	  usability is evaluated — each recorded with reasons and open to
    65	  another role's evidence.
    66	- *Recommends:* to the [PM role](lead-pm.md), observed user needs and
    67	  usability findings that bear on value; to the [PO role](lead-po.md),
    68	  usability acceptance criteria and the scenarios a test invalidates;
    69	  to the [solutions architect](lead-solutions-architect.md), the
    70	  ergonomics of contracts, CLIs, and agent tool definitions.
    71	- *Escalates to the PM role:* a value judgment and a usability finding
    72	  that cannot both stand; an experience rule the
    73	  [architecture principle set](../architecture-principles.md) cannot
    74	  accommodate.
    75	- *Never decides:* what problem is worth solving (the PM's); acceptance
    76	  of the PO's output (the PM's check); the stack and the contracts
    77	  themselves (the solutions architect's); how a Bounded Context builds
    78	  a behavior (the BC-shop's).
    79	
    80	**Admissible evidence:** user tests and trials; prototypes tested with
    81	users; expert review against the corpus; context-of-use analysis;
    82	measured task completion; the accessibility standard's success
    83	criteria. Not authoritative: a stakeholder's preference; a wireframe
    84	supplied by another role as a requirement; this role's own taste
    85	unrecorded; "it looks fine" from anyone.
    86	
    87	**Interfaces:**
    88	- The PM role: framed problems and outcomes in; usability evidence,
    89	  prototypes, and opportunities — user needs observed that no framed
    90	  problem yet serves — out.
    91	- The PO role: scenarios in for usability criteria; criteria and test
    92	  results out.
    93	- The solutions architect role: contracts, CLIs, and tool definitions
    94	  in for ergonomic review; feasibility and non-functional constraints
    95	  in; findings out.
    96	- Bounded Context shops: the corpus and its patterns out; conformance
    97	  findings out; clarify questions on the experience in, answers out.
    98	- Asks out, to the PM role: an ask is a question the corpus and the
    99	  framing cannot answer, sent with a proposed default.
   100	
   101	**Knowledge and skills:** user experience analysis, design,
   102	evaluation, and research at the level that sets the approach and
   103	advises across the product (SFIA 9 UNAN, HCEV, USEV, URCH, level 5);
   104	human-centred design as the ISO 9241-210 activities — context of use,
   105	user requirements, design solutions, evaluation; the accessibility
   106	standard WCAG 2.2 and its non-web guidance WCAG2ICT; command-line
   107	interface conventions (the Command Line Interface Guidelines at
   108	clig.dev; the Heroku CLI style guide); Microsoft's 18 Guidelines for
   109	Human-AI Interaction for any agent-facing interaction; Grice's
   110	cooperative principle for conversational interfaces; the
   111	[architecture principle set](../architecture-principles.md) as it
   112	bears on interfaces and the [working principle set](../principles.md)
   113	every activity runs under.
   114	
   115	**Anti-rationalization:**
   116	- "It's a CLI, developers don't need design." → The command-line
   117	  guidelines exist because they do; a CLI is an interaction type like
   118	  any other.
   119	- "We'll make it consistent later." → Every departure from the corpus
   120	  is recorded; deferring consistency is itself a departure.
   121	- "The PM already sketched it." → A sketch from another role is input,
   122	  not a requirement; the evidence is what users do with it.
   123	- "An agent doesn't care about ergonomics." → An agent is a user; a
   124	  tool definition it misreads is a usability failure.
   125	- "It looks fine to me." → This role's own preference is not
   126	  admissible evidence.

    21	
    22	# Process: PO output check
    23	
    24	**Purpose:** Check a piece of the PO role's output against the PM
    25	role's framing under criteria the definitions state, so that the PM
    26	role decides from a screen verdict — pass, fail with the criterion
    27	named, or a definition change — and never by reading every artifact.
    28	
    29	**Guiding statement:** The check is the criteria applied, not the
    30	checker's reading. A finding the criteria cannot name is a missing
    31	criterion, and the decision that follows is a definition change, not a
    32	verdict on the maker.
    33	
    34	**Outcomes:**
    35	- O1. The output is screened against a named criteria set and the
    36	  framing it claims to serve — witnessed by `screen`'s inputs and the
    37	  `screen-review` it returns.
    38	- O2. The PM role decides from the review and round log; the artifact
    39	  reaches the PM role only through the findings' quotes — witnessed by
    40	  `decide`'s inputs, which exclude the artifact.
    41	- O3. Every screen round is recorded and the loop exits on a clean
    42	  verdict, on findings no criterion names, or at the round cap —
    43	  witnessed by `log-round` and `route-screen`.
    44	- O4. A fail names the criterion missed; a definition change names the
    45	  gap, and the gap is filed in the named definition's Document History
    46	  — witnessed by the `check-decision` fields and `record`'s
    47	  `definition` and `gap_entry` outputs.
    48	- O5. A question the framing cannot answer leaves the run as an ask to
    49	  the PM role, with a default, and the run resumes — witnessed by
    50	  `revise`'s `asks` and the `ask` value.
    51	
    52	**Roles:** maker — [`../roles/lead-po.md`](../roles/lead-po.md)
    53	(submits and revises; never decides its own pass). screener —
    54	[`../roles/cold-reviewer.md`](../roles/cold-reviewer.md) (fresh
    55	context every round; judges against the criteria). the PM role —
    56	[`../roles/lead-pm.md`](../roles/lead-pm.md) (accountable for the
    57	decision; a human-held role that decides from the verdict; its
    58	assisting agent prepares the record and the gap entry).
    59	
    60	**Carried by:**

   124	
   125	```yaml
   126	start: screen
   127	parameters: [artifact, framing, criteria_path]
   128	result: decision
   129	steps:
   130	  - id: screen
   131	    name: Screen against the criteria
   132	    run-by: {role: cold-reviewer, execution: agent, fresh-context: true}
   133	    inputs: [artifact, framing, criteria_path]
   134	    outputs: [review]
   135	    prompt: |
   136	      Read the criteria set at criteria_path, the framing at framing,
   137	      and the artifact at artifact — nothing else. Judge the artifact
   138	      against every criterion and against the framing: does each part
   139	      of it trace to the framing, and does anything in it serve no
   140	      framed outcome? Report every finding with the criterion it fails
   141	      by name — "framing" for a part that does not trace to the framing
   142	      or serves no framed outcome — the quoted text, the change, and
   143	      whether you could decide it (confident) or not (wobbly); for a
   144	      wobbly finding quote the whole passage, since the PM role decides
   145	      from the review alone. A defect no criterion names is a finding
   146	      with criterion "uncovered". Verdict "clean"
   147	      only if there are no findings; otherwise "findings" with the top
   148	      three changes.
   149	    next: log-round
   150	    annotations:
   151	      fabro: {model: high-reasoning, node: separate-context-per-round}
   152	
   153	  - id: log-round
   154	    name: Record the round
   155	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   156	    inputs: [review, round_log]
   157	    set:
   158	      round_log: round_log + [review]
   159	    next: route-screen
   160	
   161	  - id: route-screen
   162	    name: Route on the screen
   163	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   164	    inputs: [review, round, round_cap]
   165	    branches:
   166	      - label: "success exit: clean"
   167	        when: review.verdict == "clean"
   168	        next: decide
   169	      - label: "definition exit: every finding is uncovered — nothing the maker can repair"
   170	        when: size(review.findings) > 0 && review.findings.all(f, f.criterion == "uncovered")
   171	        next: decide
   172	      - label: "failsafe exit: round >= round_cap — decide with findings open"
   173	        when: round >= round_cap
   174	        next: decide
   175	      - else: revise
   176	
   177	  - id: revise
   178	    name: Revise the output
   179	    run-by: {role: lead-po, execution: agent}
   180	    inputs: [artifact, review, framing, ask]
   181	    outputs: [artifact]
   182	    asks: [lead-pm]
   183	    prompt: |
   184	      Repair every finding whose criterion is named, "framing"
   185	      included; leave findings marked "uncovered" as they are — they
   186	      are the PM role's to decide. If a repair needs something the framing does not say —
   187	      the originator's intent, whether a thing is in scope, a decision
   188	      another role's domain holds — return an ask to lead-pm instead
   189	      of guessing: the question, its kind, the default you will apply
   190	      if unanswered, and a checkpoint holding the repairs made so far.
   191	      On the first pass ask is absent; if it carries an answer or
   192	      resolved defaulted, act on it and finish the repairs.
   193	    next: advance-round
   194	
   195	  - id: advance-round
   196	    name: Advance the round
   197	    run-by: {execution: runtime}
   198	    inputs: [round]
   199	    set:
   200	      round: round + 1
   201	    next: screen
   202	
   203	  - id: decide
   204	    name: Decide on the verdict
   205	    run-by: {role: lead-pm, execution: human}
   206	    inputs: [review, round_log, framing]
   207	    outputs: [decision]
   208	    prompt: |
   209	      From the final review and the round log, decide. "pass": the
   210	      screen is clean, or its only findings are uncovered and you judge
   211	      none of them needs a criterion — say so in the reasons. "fail": a
   212	      named criterion, "framing" included, is still missed at the round
   213	      cap — name it.
   214	      "definition-change": an uncovered finding should have been a
   215	      criterion, or a criterion the output met produced something that
   216	      does not serve the framing — name the definition and what it
   217	      lacks. Where both a missed named criterion and an open uncovered finding
   218	      stand at the cap, "fail" takes precedence and the reasons carry
   219	      the uncovered finding. Decide from the quotes in the review.
   220	      Record your reasons.
   221	    next: record
   222	
   223	  - id: record
   224	    name: Record the decision
   225	    run-by: {role: lead-pm, execution: agent}
   226	    inputs: [decision, artifact, round_log]
   227	    outputs: [artifact, gap_entry, definition]
   228	    prompt: |
   229	      Write into the artifact's Document History one review entry per
   230	      round with its verdict, then a state entry carrying the decision
   231	      and its reasons. Set the artifact's status: "checked" on pass,
   232	      "returned" on fail with the criterion named, "pending-definition"
   233	      on definition-change. On "definition-change", write a review
   234	      entry stating the gap into the Document History of the definition
   235	      the decision names; return that file's path as definition and the
   236	      entry's text as gap_entry. Otherwise return both empty. Return
   237	      the artifact.
   238	    next: end
   239	```
