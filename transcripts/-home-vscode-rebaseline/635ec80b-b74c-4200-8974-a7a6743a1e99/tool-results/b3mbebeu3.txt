    12	# Base writing style
    13	
    14	These rules apply to everything written for a human reader, regardless of
    15	document type. Document types add a format layer on top of this base; they
    16	never override it.
    17	
    18	## Goal
    19	
    20	The reader gets the point of any paragraph in one read, any section in 30
    21	seconds, and the whole document in two minutes. Write for the first read.
    22	
    23	## Lead with the point
    24	
    25	At every scale, state the conclusion first and the support after.
    26	
    27	- A document opens with what it found or decided, not how it got there.
    28	- A section opens with its main claim, then evidence.
    29	- A paragraph opens with its point, then elaboration.
    30	
    31	If a document type defines a structure, that structure is just this rule
    32	applied to that type. If no structure is defined, use this rule to invent
    33	one.
    34	
    35	## Detail placement
    36	
    37	Precision serves the point; it never precedes it. Formal specification
    38	language, methods, raw data, scoring tables, and citations go after the
    39	claim they support — or in an appendix. The body is for understanding.
    40	
    41	## Sentences
    42	
    43	- One idea per sentence.
    44	- Active voice, with a named actor: "Reviewers delete unlinked checks,"
    45	  not "Unlinked checks are subject to deletion."
    46	- Prefer the short common word: "use" not "utilize," "end" not
    47	  "terminate," "before" not "prior to."
    48	
    49	## Terminology
    50	
    51	- Never use a metaphor as a technical term. If a word describes a thing
    52	  by what it resembles instead of what it literally does, replace it with
    53	  the literal description. Test: could a competent reader from outside
    54	  the team work out the meaning from the words alone? If not, rewrite.
    55	- Banned words. Never use these, in any form:
    56	  - "load-bearing" → say "other work depends on it" or "critical to
    57	    operations"
    58	  - "surface" (as in context surface, config surface) → say "input,"
    59	    "source," or "channel"
    60	  - "seam" → say "boundary" or "integration point"
    61	  - "scar tissue" → describe the actual problem, e.g. "an unreviewed
    62	    workaround that was never removed"
    63	- Explain every insider reference — a past incident, an internal system,
    64	  a person — in one plain sentence, or cut it. Assume the reader knows
    65	  none of our history.
    66	
    67	## Style examples
    68	
    69	These examples exist only to show sentence style. The domain (a public
    70	library) is deliberately unrelated to our work. Copy the style; never
    71	copy the content or vocabulary into real documents.
    72	
    73	bad:
    74	
    75	> Unreturned inventory constitutes scar tissue on the circulation loop
    76	> and rapidly becomes load-bearing for downstream shelving throughput.
    77	
    78	good:
    79	
    80	> Overdue books pile up at the front desk, and staff spend their time
    81	> reshelving late returns instead of helping visitors.
    82	
    83	The bad sentence uses two metaphors as terms and never names an actor.
    84	The good sentence says who is affected and what actually happens, in
    85	plain words.

    12	# Glossary
    13	
    14	## How the list combines
    15	
    16	The defined-term list is this glossary combined with every schema element
    17	name. Per the `use-defined-terms` principle, a writer choosing between
    18	terms uses one of these when one fits.
    19	
    20	## Terms
    21	
    22	- **artifact type** — a named, schema-defined document type (e.g.
    23	  `decision-brief`); the term the schema registry uses. Not "kind".
    24	- **data type** — a named, schema-defined structure that is not a
    25	  human-readable document (e.g. `review`); passed between process steps.
    26	- **simple type** — a JSON Schema primitive (`string`, `integer`,
    27	  `boolean`, `array`, `object`), usable inline without registration.
    28	- **typedef** — the single source a type is generated from; templates and
    29	  schema fragments are its renderings (owned by shopsystem-knowledge).
    30	- **schema** — the machine-checkable shape of a type: fields, types,
    31	  enums, required sections.
    32	- **process definition** — the source of truth for a process: header
    33	  (purpose, outcomes, roles), data section, steps section.
    34	- **step** — one unit of a process: an agent step (carries a prompt) or a
    35	  runtime step (carries `set`, `run`, or `branches`; no prose).
    36	- **rendering** — a generated output of a definition (a skill, a
    37	  diagram, a template); never edited by hand, never the source of truth.
    38	- **guiding statement** — an optional header element of a process
    39	  definition that directs judgment across the whole process; compiled
    40	  into every rendering.
    41	- **fitness set** — judged (never executed) Given/When/Then scenarios
    42	  scoring an artifact type's quality.
    43	- **guideline** — prose quality rules for an artifact type, each with a
    44	  test, a criterion, and a yes/no decision.
    45	- **role** — a named capability contract and set of accountabilities,
    46	  assigned by process steps to whoever fills it; sequencing lives in
    47	  process definitions, not roles.
    48	- **principle** — a standing rule about how we work: name, statement,
    49	  rationale, implications (see the principles document's opening
    50	  definition).
    51	- **seed layer** — the hand-approved definitions the regress terminates
    52	  at: the principle set plus one typedef per definition document type.
    53	- **owner** — the role that approves changes to a definition; named in
    54	  every definition's frontmatter.
    55	- **definition chain** — the six linked definitions of good for one
    56	  artifact type; required before instances are authored or rewritten.
    57	- **keeper** — a corpus record whose action is keep-rewrite; rewritten
    58	  forward through its type's approved chain, never used as-is.
    59	- **rewrite-forward** — the default for keepers: re-author to the
    60	  approved standard; repair-in-place only for records already at the bar.
    61	- **rebaseline** — Phase 1 of the re-founding arc: the active tree comes
    62	  to hold only chain-conforming records; retired mass moves to the
    63	  archive.
    64	- **scope** (of a principle set) — the level the set governs and whose
    65	  context it loads into: working (every activity) or architecture (the
    66	  designed system).
    67	- **transcript** — the runtime log of turns; unscoped, not a governed
    68	  artifact, never loaded into context.
    69	- **conversation** — a scoped, bounded discussion attached to exactly one
    70	  anchor; every conversation belongs to a process.
    71	- **anchor** — the governed record a conversation attaches to: a session
    72	  record, a review record, or a work item; carries the conversation's
    73	  state across transcript boundaries.
    74	- **conversation type** — discovery (anchor: a session record), review
    75	  (anchor: a review record and its outcomes), or work (anchor: a work
    76	  item).
    77	- **session record** — the anchor record of a discovery conversation:
    78	  outcome, produced and revised lists, open threads, select quotes.
    79	- **review** — one bounded conversation in which the authority decides
    80	  on presented material. Replaces "sitting".
    81	- **review record** — the anchor record of a review: the material
    82	  presented and the outcomes applied; each decision traces in the
    83	  changed artifacts' Document History, never in a standing ledger.
    84	- **park** — the failsafe for work that cannot pass review within its
    85	  round cap: set aside with a filed finding.
    86	- **round cap** — the declared maximum review rounds before a loop's
    87	  failsafe exit fires.
    88	- **action** — the approved choice for one record: keep-rewrite, keep,
    89	  retire, or terminal, with its target.
    90	- **authority-call** — a row marker on a migration-plan row awaiting a
    91	  decision from the authority; not an action — the row has no effect and
    92	  is out of scope for any run until ruled.
    93	- **action table** — the table of records and their actions; approved row
    94	  by row or in blocks at a review. Drives any bulk record change.
    95	- **close-out** — the mechanical execution of the migration plan's
    96	  pre-decided retire and terminal actions: snapshot, delete, archive
    97	  move, a loud post-check, and the branch promotion; no review loop.
    98	  All stages run consecutively at cut-over.
    99	- **snapshot tag** — the annotated git tag (`pre-migration`) on `main`'s
   100	  pre-execution commit, preserving the full corpus for terminal-recovery;
   101	  after close-out a terminal record exists only there.
   102	- **migration plan** — the rebaseline's action table plus the order of
   103	  the per-type migration runs. Replaces "rebaseline bill".
   104	- **run** — one execution of a process, anchored to a work item; states:
   105	  running, held, done, cancelled.
   106	- **hold** — a run state: paused with its step and data preserved in its
   107	  anchor, by inactivity or by an ask; a held run is resumed or
   108	  cancelled, never dropped.
   109	- **checkpoint** — updating a conversation's anchor when a transcript
   110	  ends mid-conversation; a checkpoint is not a close.
   111	- **branched conversation** — a conversation run as a sub-process of
   112	  another run; its anchor records the parent (`branched-from`).
   113	- **Bounded Context** — a deliberately drawn region of the product with
   114	  one internal language, one model, and one set of contracts to other
   115	  regions; drawn by design, never discovered after the fact (Evans,
   116	  *Domain-Driven Design*).
   117	- **shop** — the entity that builds, operates, and evolves part of the
   118	  product. Two types: the lead shop (the system-level coordinator; owns
   119	  product-level artifacts and no Bounded Context) and the BC-shop (owns
   120	  and produces exactly one Bounded Context).
   121	- **activity** — a discrete unit of work performed inside a shop,
   122	  following a contract or process, with inputs, outcomes, and provenance
   123	  recorded. What an actor is stays unconstrained; the activity has the
   124	  same shape whoever performs it.
   125	- **contract** — a named, versioned statement of what one entity
   126	  promises another: the schemas exchanged, the meaning of each
   127	  operation, the error behavior, and the relationship kind. Offered as a
   128	  product contract (on a Bounded Context) or an operational contract (on
   129	  a shop); nothing outside a contract is promised.
   130	- **relationship kind** — the named pattern a contract declares for how
   131	  two Bounded Contexts relate, drawn from the context-mapping catalogue
   132	  (Evans, *Domain-Driven Design*): customer–supplier, conformist,
   133	  anti-corruption layer, open host service, published language, shared
   134	  kernel, or separate ways.
   135	- **vehicle** — the message type a request to a Bounded Context shop
   136	  travels in; chosen from the pre-state, never from the request's
   137	  wording.
   138	- **pre-state** — what a Bounded Context actually is before a change,
   139	  read from its contracts and its scenario register, never from its
   140	  internals.
   141	- **framing** — the PM role's recorded statement of what a request is
   142	  about: the originator, the problem taken to be worth solving, and the
   143	  outcome it serves; the exclusive decision of `lead-pm`.
   144	- **acceptance scenario** — a Gherkin scenario that states, as a
   145	  requirement, what counts as done for one behavior; co-produced by the
   146	  PO role and the owning Bounded Context shop; held in that shop's
   147	  scenario register.
   148	- **scenario register** — a Bounded Context shop's own list of the
   149	  acceptance scenarios it holds, with their hashes and states; read in
   150	  full across every context when a claim spans contexts.
   151	- **ask** — a question one activity puts to another role in place of
   152	  its output, carrying a default and a checkpoint; the run holds, the
   153	  role answers, the step resumes — never a wait in place (data type
   154	  `ask`; process-definition typedef §Run lifecycle).
   155	- **clarify** — an ask from a Bounded Context shop to the lead shop on
   156	  scope, vocabulary, structure, or contract; answered by the role whose
   157	  domain the question falls in.
   158	- **decomposition** — the solutions architect's record of which Bounded
   159	  Context owns which capability, and the relationship kind of each
   160	  contract between contexts.
   161	- **enabler work** — technical work that makes a feature possible;
   162	  recommended by the solutions architect, placed in the backlog by the
   163	  PO role.
   164	- **interaction type** — one way a person or an agent reaches the
   165	  product: command line (CLI), full-screen terminal (TUI), graphical or
   166	  web (GUI), API and SDK, conversational, voice, or a generated document
   167	  or notification; each honours its own conventions within one
   168	  experience.
   169	- **experience guidance corpus** — the product designer role's
   170	  principle set and guidelines for the experience: design principles,
   171	  vocabulary and voice, core-task parity across interaction types,
   172	  interaction patterns per type, and the accessibility target; the
   173	  role's exclusive domain.
   174	- **originator** — whoever expresses intent at the product's edge: a
   175	  person, a shop, or a system outside the product.
   176	- **intent** — a desired outcome expressed by an originator at the
   177	  product's edge; enters the product through a contract and keeps its
   178	  provenance through every translation and delegation.

    13	# Data type: ask
    14	
    15	## Purpose
    16	
    17	A question one activity puts to another role, returned by an agent or
    18	human step in place of its outputs. Produced by any step that carries
    19	`asks`; routed by the runtime to whoever fills the role
    20	named in `to`; consumed by that role, which writes `answer`, and by the
    21	asking step, which resumes with the ask in its inputs. The process
    22	declares an `ask` data value (`$ref: ask`) and lists it in each asking
    23	step's inputs. Two resume paths: *answered* — the step acts on
    24	`answer`; *defaulted* — the step acts on `default`, because the
    25	process's `ask-cap` passed unanswered or because the step returned a
    26	second ask in the same run (at most one is allowed). See the
    27	[process-definition typedef](../artifacts/process-definition.md), §The
    28	steps section and §Run lifecycle. A `clarify` — a Bounded Context
    29	shop's question to the lead shop — conforms to this shape.
    30	
    31	## Schema
    32	
    33	```yaml
    34	schema:
    35	  type: object
    36	  fields:
    37	    to: {type: string}            # asker writes: the role asked; a derived check confirms it is in the step's `asks`
    38	    kind:                         # asker writes: what the asker lacks
    39	      type: string
    40	      enum: [pre-state, intent, reserved-decision, scope, vocabulary, structure, contract]
    41	      # pre-state: what a thing is before a change; intent: what the originator wanted;
    42	      # reserved-decision: a decision another role's domain holds; scope: whether
    43	      # something is in or out; vocabulary: what a term means; structure: which
    44	      # Bounded Context owns something; contract: what is promised across a boundary
    45	    question: {type: string}      # asker writes
    46	    default: {type: string}       # asker writes: what the step will do if the ask resolves defaulted
    47	    checkpoint: {type: string}    # asker writes: partial output sufficient to resume from
    48	    answer: {type: string, optional: true}       # answering role writes
    49	    answered_by: {type: string, optional: true}  # runtime writes: the role that answered
    50	    resolution:                                  # runtime writes
    51	      type: string
    52	      enum: [answered, defaulted, cancelled]     # cancelled: the held run was cancelled
    53	      optional: true
    54	```

    12	# Data type: check-decision
    13	
    14	## Purpose
    15	
    16	The PM role's decision on a piece of the PO role's output, taken from
    17	a screen verdict. Produced by the `decide` step of
    18	[`../processes/po-output-check.md`](../processes/po-output-check.md);
    19	consumed by its routing and `record` steps and written into the
    20	checked artifact's Document History. `pass` accepts the output against
    21	the framing; `fail` names the criterion the output missed;
    22	`definition-change` says the criteria themselves were insufficient and
    23	names the gap to file. The `decide` step writes every field; that
    24	`criterion` accompanies `fail` and `gap` accompanies
    25	`definition-change` is a judged check on the decision, not a schema
    26	constraint.
    27	
    28	## Schema
    29	
    30	```yaml
    31	schema:
    32	  type: object
    33	  fields:
    34	    verdict: {type: string, enum: [pass, fail, definition-change]}
    35	    criterion: {type: string, optional: true}   # required when verdict is fail: the criterion missed, by name
    36	    gap: {type: string, optional: true}         # required when verdict is definition-change: the definition and what it lacks
    37	    reasons: {type: string}
    38	```

    12	# Data type: screen-review
    13	
    14	## Purpose
    15	
    16	One screen round's verdict and findings against a named criteria set.
    17	Produced by the `screen` step of
    18	[`../processes/po-output-check.md`](../processes/po-output-check.md);
    19	consumed by its routing, `revise`, and `decide` steps and appended to
    20	the round log. Each finding names the criterion it fails — or
    21	`uncovered`, for a defect no criterion names — carries the quoted text,
    22	and states whether the screener could decide it (`confident`) or not
    23	(`wobbly`); the decider opens the artifact only for a wobbly finding.
    24	
    25	## Schema
    26	
    27	```yaml
    28	schema:
    29	  type: object
    30	  fields:
    31	    verdict: {type: string, enum: [clean, findings]}
    32	    findings:
    33	      type: array
    34	      items:
    35	        type: object
    36	        fields:
    37	          criterion: {type: string}          # the criterion's name, or the literal "uncovered"
    38	          quote: {type: string}
    39	          change: {type: string}
    40	          decidability: {type: string, enum: [confident, wobbly]}
    41	    top_changes: {type: array, items: {type: string}, maxItems: 3}
    42	```

    15	
    16	# Lead PM
    17	
    18	You hold the role where intent enters the shop and where the product's
    19	outcome is answered for. You turn what an originator — whoever expresses intent at the
    20	product's edge — asks for into a recorded *framing* — the problem the shop takes the request to be
    21	about and the outcome it serves — and you check what the PO role makes
    22	from that framing against it. The product authority holds this role in
    23	person; that is the role's authority. Agents assist the role's
    24	activities (see Interfaces); the frontmatter contracts those agents,
    25	and the person holding the role is bound by the definitions, not by
    26	the tool list.
    27	
    28	**Standard of judgment:** outcomes, not outputs — you frame a problem
    29	to solve, never a feature to build. You engage an originator's
    30	statements as an interlocutor and record convergence rather than
    31	inferring it. Material reaches you decidable — a screen verdict, a decision brief,
    32	or an ask (a question a definition cannot answer, sent with a
    33	proposed default) — or it goes back to the role that sent it with the
    34	missing criterion named.
    35	
    36	**Accountable for:**
    37	- The recorded framing of each piece of intent: the originator, the
    38	  expressed outcome, the problem taken to be worth solving, and the
    39	  contract — product or operational — it entered through.
    40	- The value and viability judgment on every candidate problem: whether
    41	  it is worth solving and whether the product can sustain the solution.
    42	- The check on the product-owner (PO) role's output — acceptance scenarios, briefs, backlog
    43	  order — against the framing, under criteria the definitions state: a
    44	  pass, a fail with the criterion named, or a definition change where
    45	  the criteria proved insufficient.
    46	- Roadmap priority: which framed problems come first, recorded with
    47	  reasons.
    48	- Convergence of each discovery conversation recorded on its anchor
    49	  (see the [glossary](../glossary.md)).
    50	- The provenance chain: any activity traceable to its originating
    51	  expression without ambiguity, and every decision applied to the
    52	  governed artifacts it changes with a Document History entry.
    53	
    54	**Domain (exclusive):** the framing of intent — what the shop takes an
    55	expression to be asking for is decided by this role alone.
    56	
    57	**Decision rights.**
    58	- *Decides:* the framing (exclusive); and, as the accountable role,
    59	  value and viability, roadmap priority, and whether the PO's output
    60	  passes its check — each recorded with its reasons and open to another
    61	  role's evidence.
    62	- *Recommends:* scope changes to the PO with reasons; the outcome a
    63	  shape must serve to the solutions architect.
    64	- *Resolves:* a conflict between this role's framing and another
    65	  role's domain, recorded on the affected artifacts; no role overrides this
    66	  one's framing.
    67	- *Never decides:* feasibility and the stack (the
    68	  [solutions architect](lead-solutions-architect.md)'s);
    69	  authorship of acceptance scenarios and backlog order within the
    70	  framing (the PO's); usability (the
    71	  [product designer](lead-product-designer.md)'s); how a
    72	  Bounded Context builds a behavior (the BC-shop's).
    73	
    74	**Admissible evidence:** the originator's own words, recorded;
    75	discovery-conversation anchors; measured outcomes of shipped work; a
    76	screen verdict — a cold reviewer's judgment against a named fitness
    77	set. Not authoritative: a stakeholder's feature list read as a
    78	requirement; the PO's backlog read as the product's intent; the
    79	role's own reading of every artifact in place of a stated criterion.
    80	
    81	**Interfaces:**
    82	- Originators: intent in; the recorded framing back.
    83	- The PO role: framed intent and its outcome out; scenarios, briefs,
    84	  backlog order, and scope questions in for the check.
    85	- The solutions architect role: framed problem and outcome out;
    86	  feasibility verdict, technical risks, and questions in.
    87	- The product designer role: framed problems and outcomes out;
    88	  usability evidence, prototypes, and observed user needs in.
    89	- Assisting agents: each assisted activity's process names what the
    90	  agent prepares and what this role decides.
    91	- Asks from any role: answered here, and a recurring ask becomes a
    92	  definition change.
    93	
    94	**Knowledge and skills:** product discovery and interviewing; the
    95	four product risks — value, usability, feasibility, viability — and
    96	which role answers for each (value and viability here; feasibility
    97	with the solutions architect; usability with the product designer); the shop's
    98	definition corpus (every activity run through the basis); the
    99	[stakeholder-presentation](../processes/stakeholder-presentation.md)
   100	and [discovery-conversation](../processes/discovery-conversation.md)
   101	processes; the [working principle set](../principles.md) every
   102	activity runs under.
   103	
   104	**Anti-rationalization:**
   105	- "I'll just read the scenarios myself." → A check without a stated
   106	  criterion is a read, not a check; the criterion is the missing
   107	  definition.
   108	- "The PO knows what I meant." → A framing is recorded or it does not
   109	  exist.
   110	- "This feature is obviously worth it." → Value is a judgment with
   111	  evidence named; the feature list is not the evidence.
   112	- "It's infeasible, so the framing is wrong." → Infeasible returns the
   113	  problem for re-framing; it does not decide what is worth solving.
   114	- "I'll decide the how while I'm here." → The how belongs to the
   115	  architect and the shops; deciding it here removes their check.

    16	# Lead PO
    17	
    18	You hold the role that makes the requirements. From the
    19	[PM role](lead-pm.md)'s *framing* — its recorded statement of the problem a request is about
    20	and the outcome it serves (see the [glossary](../glossary.md)) — you
    21	author the artifacts that say what the product is supposed to do:
    22	briefs (a bounded statement of a problem and its scope for a Bounded
    23	Context shop), product decision records (the record of one
    24	product-level decision and its reasons), and acceptance scenarios (see
    25	the glossary). The brief and product decision record typedefs are
    26	pending on this branch; until they exist, the frozen corpus on `main`
    27	is the reference. You order
    28	the backlog. Scope and vocabulary questions from any Bounded Context
    29	shop resolve against your artifacts. The PM role checks what you make
    30	against the framing; you make, you do not check your own work.
    31	
    32	**Standard of judgment:** you own the commitment, you do not take
    33	orders — you decline scope that serves no framed outcome and record
    34	the reason. You say what, never how. You author with the shop that
    35	owns a behavior, never alone: it supplies the steps and edge cases.
    36	
    37	**Accountable for:**
    38	- Requirements artifacts the rest of the shop can act on — briefs,
    39	  product decision records, acceptance scenarios — each traceable to
    40	  the framing it serves.
    41	- Acceptance scenarios written in Gherkin: each tagged, identified by
    42	  a hash of its text so that a changed scenario is a new scenario,
    43	  testable against the running system, and co-produced with the
    44	  Bounded Context shop that owns the behavior.
    45	- The backlog: its content and order within the framing, structured
    46	  to mirror the [solutions architect](lead-solutions-architect.md)'s
    47	  decomposition.
    48	- Scope and vocabulary answers to clarify questions from Bounded
    49	  Context shops, grounded in the requirements artifacts.
    50	- The requirements picture readable from the artifacts alone, without
    51	  asking their author; new domain vocabulary added to the glossary.
    52	
    53	**Domain (exclusive):** backlog order — this role alone decides which
    54	requirement the shops take up next, within the framing.
    55	
    56	**Decision rights.**
    57	- *Decides:* backlog order (exclusive). The content of the artifacts
    58	  it submits is this role's authorship and the PM role's check; which
    59	  scenario a clarify resolves against is this role's answer, open to
    60	  the shop's evidence.
    61	- *Recommends:* scope changes to the PM role, with reasons; scenario
    62	  splits to the owning Bounded Context shop.
    63	- *Places or declines in the backlog, with reasons:* enabler work the
    64	  solutions architect recommends — an exercise of the exclusive
    65	  domain, not a recommendation.
    66	- *Escalates to the PM role:* scope conflicts; a scenario the
    67	  solutions architect reports as infeasible or as crossing contexts; a
    68	  framing the artifacts cannot be written from.
    69	- *Never decides:* whether a problem is worth solving (the PM's); the
    70	  pass or fail of its own output (the PM's check); how a behavior is
    71	  built (the shops' and the architect's); which context owns a
    72	  behavior (the architect's).
    73	
    74	**Admissible evidence:** the PM role's recorded framing; every
    75	Bounded Context's scenario register read in full, never one context's
    76	copy of another's; the solutions architect's decomposition, for where
    77	a scenario lands; the owning shop's steps for a scenario. Not
    78	authoritative: a stakeholder document transcribed into tickets; a
    79	scenario written without the owning shop; this role's own memory of
    80	what the PM meant.
    81	
    82	**Interfaces:**
    83	- The PM role: framed intent in; requirements artifacts, backlog
    84	  order, and scope questions out, for the check.
    85	- The solutions architect role: PM-checked scenarios out, for
    86	  assignment to the Bounded Context that owns each; enabler recommendations, non-functional constraints, and
    87	  decomposition changes in.
    88	- Bounded Context shops: scenarios co-produced — this role supplies
    89	  scope and wording, the shop supplies steps and edge cases, the
    90	  architect supplies context ownership and feasibility; clarify
    91	  questions on scope and vocabulary in, answers out.
    92	- Asks out, to the PM role: a question the framing cannot answer,
    93	  sent with a proposed default.
    94	
    95	**Knowledge and skills:** requirements authoring; Gherkin as an
    96	acceptance language, and the practice it assumes — scenarios written
    97	by the business, testing, and development perspectives together
    98	("three amigos"); backlog ordering by outcome; the product's domain
    99	language; the [working principle set](../principles.md).
   100	
   101	**Anti-rationalization:**
   102	- "The stakeholder's list is the requirement." → A list is input; the
   103	  framing decides what serves an outcome.
   104	- "I know the domain best, I'll write the scenarios myself." → The
   105	  owning shop writes the steps; a scenario without them is not
   106	  co-produced.
   107	- "It's obviously done." → Done is the PM role's check against the
   108	  framing, not this role's opinion of its own output.
   109	- "The architect said it can't be built, so drop it." → Infeasible
   110	  escalates to the PM role for re-framing; it is not a scope decision
   111	  here.
   112	- "I'll specify how, it's faster." → The how is the shops'; specifying
   113	  it removes their ownership and the architect's check.
