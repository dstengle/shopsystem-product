# Fitness set: brief

A brief is the PO role's bounded statement of a problem and its scope
for a Bounded Context shop (the typedef is pending on this branch; the
frozen corpus on `main` is the reference). These scenarios are the
criteria set the [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md)
screens a brief against, alongside the PM role's framing, which is
always the criterion named `framing`. They are evaluated by the
`cold-reviewer` role, never executed — no step definitions exist or
will exist. The judge's model and prompt version are recorded with each
round verdict.
The judge reads only the criteria set, the framing, and the artifact;
every scenario therefore asks for what the artifact itself carries, and
a fact it must carry — a term's definition, a reference, a reason — is
what these scenarios make it carry. These scenarios stand in for the
pending typedef and are its first draft; the typedef inherits them.
A "competent reader" is the cold-reviewer role as its definition
describes it.

## Scenarios

Scenario 1: declined scope carries its reason
  Given each statement the brief marks out of scope
  When it is read
  Then it carries the reason it serves no framed outcome

Scenario 2: the problem, not the solution
  Given the brief's statement of what the shop is asked for
  When it is parsed for design or implementation choices
  Then it states the problem and the outcome and names no how — no
  technology, structure, or interface form the shop would otherwise
  choose; naming an interaction type the behavior must hold on is a
  what, not a form

Scenario 3: scope has an edge
  Given the pieces of work the framing or the brief itself names as
  neighbouring
  When each is read against the scope
  Then the brief says whether it is in or out, or names the rule that
  decides

Scenario 4: the shop can act from it alone
  Given the brief without its author present
  When the receiving shop reads it
  Then every term is defined in the brief or the framing, every
  referenced artifact is linked, and every question the shop must
  resolve before starting is either answered or listed as open

Scenario 5: the first paragraph states the problem and the outcome
  Given the brief alone
  When the reader stops after the first paragraph
  Then the problem and the outcome are already stated (the lead-po
  role: the requirements picture readable from the artifacts alone)

## Compile mapping (each Then → one judge-rubric assertion)

| Scenario Then | Judge-rubric assertion |
|---|---|
| 1 — declined scope reasoned | "For each out-of-scope mark: is a reason stated? Cite any without one." |
| 2 — problem, not solution | "List every sentence that chooses a technology, structure, or interface form (an interaction type named is not a form). Empty list = pass." |
| 3 — scope has an edge | "For each neighbouring piece of work the framing or brief names: does the brief say in or out, or name the deciding rule? Cite." |
| 4 — actionable alone | "List undefined terms, unlinked references, and questions neither answered nor listed as open. Empty list = pass." |
| 5 — first paragraph | "Does paragraph 1 state the problem and the outcome? Quote it, or answer no." |

# Fitness set: product decision record

A product decision record is the PO role's record of one product-level
decision and its reasons (the typedef is pending on this branch; the
frozen corpus on `main` is the reference). These scenarios are the
criteria set the [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md)
screens a record against, alongside the framing (criterion `framing`).
Evaluated by the `cold-reviewer` role, never executed. The judge's
model and prompt version are recorded with each round verdict.
The judge reads only the criteria set, the framing, and the artifact;
every scenario therefore asks for what the artifact itself carries, and
a fact it must carry — a term's definition, a reference, a reason — is
what these scenarios make it carry. These scenarios stand in for the
pending typedef and are its first draft; the typedef inherits them.
A "competent reader" is the cold-reviewer role as its definition
describes it.
The record's parts — decision, alternatives, consequences, decider,
reversibility — follow the architecture decision record form (Nygard),
adopted per `external-standards-first`; whether the named role held the
right it exercised is the PM role's ruling at the decide step, not the
judge's.

## Scenarios

Scenario 1: one decision
  Given the record
  When its decision statement is parsed
  Then it states exactly one decision, as a sentence a reader can act
  on

Scenario 2: the alternatives were real
  Given the record's alternatives
  When each alternative is read
  Then at least one alternative is stated that the deciding role could
  have chosen, with the reason it was not

Scenario 3: the decider and the right are named
  Given the record's statement of who decided
  When it is read
  Then it names the role that decided and the decision right it
  exercised, or the escalation that settled it

Scenario 4: consequences are priced
  Given the record's consequences
  When each consequence is read
  Then it names what changes, for whom, and what it costs or forecloses

Scenario 5: reversibility is stated
  Given the record
  When a reader asks how hard the decision is to reverse
  Then the record says so and, for a decision it calls hard to reverse,
  names what would trigger revisiting it

## Compile mapping (each Then → one judge-rubric assertion)

| Scenario Then | Judge-rubric assertion |
|---|---|
| 1 — one decision | "Quote the decision sentence. Is it one decision, actionable? Any no = fail." |
| 2 — real alternatives | "Is at least one alternative stated that the deciding role could have chosen, with the reason against it? Cite it or its absence." |
| 3 — decider and right named | "Does the record name the deciding role and the right exercised, or the escalation? Cite the sentence or its absence." |
| 4 — consequences priced | "For each consequence: what changes, for whom, at what cost? Cite any consequence missing one." |
| 5 — reversibility | "Does the record state how hard the decision is to reverse and, if hard, what would trigger revisiting it? Cite the sentence or its absence." |

# Fitness set: acceptance scenarios

An acceptance scenario is a Gherkin scenario that states, as a
requirement, what counts as done for one behavior; a set of them is
what the PO role submits for a behavior (the acceptance scenarios
themselves are executable by the owning shop; this fitness set judges
their quality and is never executed). These scenarios are the criteria
set the [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md) screens a
submitted set against, alongside the framing (criterion `framing`).
Evaluated by the `cold-reviewer` role. The judge's model and prompt
version are recorded with each round verdict.
The judge reads only the criteria set, the framing, and the artifact;
every scenario therefore asks for what the artifact itself carries, and
a fact it must carry — a term's definition, a reference, a reason — is
what these scenarios make it carry. These scenarios stand in for the
pending acceptance-scenario typedef and are its first draft; the
typedef inherits them (the co-production statement and the tag's
content are what this set asks the typedef to carry).
A "competent reader" is the cold-reviewer role as its definition
describes it.

## Scenarios

Scenario 1: each scenario is one observable behavior
  Given a scenario in the set
  When its Given/When/Then is read
  Then the When is one action or event, the Then an outcome observable
  in the running system, and no step names an implementation detail

Scenario 2: co-produced with the owning shop
  Given the set's statement of who contributed
  When it is read
  Then the owning Bounded Context shop is named as the source of the
  steps and edge cases, and, where the behavior has an interaction, the
  product designer role's usability and accessibility criteria are
  present

Scenario 3: identity fields are present
  Given each scenario
  When its header is read
  Then it carries a tag naming the behavior and its Bounded Context and
  a hash field (whether the hash matches the text is a mechanical
  check, a filed work item, not this judge's)

Scenario 4: the named edges are covered
  Given the failure and boundary cases the framing, the brief, or the
  owning shop's contribution names
  When each is read against the set
  Then it is a scenario in the set or is marked out of scope with a
  reason

Scenario 5: interaction types are named
  Given a behavior a person or agent reaches through an interaction
  When the set is read
  Then it names the interaction types the behavior must hold on (the
  experience principle `core-task-parity`)

## Compile mapping (each Then → one judge-rubric assertion)

| Scenario Then | Judge-rubric assertion |
|---|---|
| 1 — one observable behavior | "For each scenario: is the When one action, the Then observable in the running system, and no step implementation-specific? Cite any failing scenario." |
| 2 — co-produced | "Is the owning shop named as the source of steps and edge cases? Where the behavior has an interaction, are usability and accessibility criteria present? Cite or name the absence." |
| 3 — identity fields | "For each scenario: tag naming behavior and context present? hash field present? Cite any scenario without both." |
| 4 — named edges covered | "For each failure or boundary case the framing, brief, or shop contribution names: cite the scenario or the out-of-scope note. Any uncovered = fail." |
| 5 — interaction types named | "Does the set name the interaction types the behavior must hold on? Cite the sentence or its absence." |

# Fitness set: backlog order

A backlog order is the PO role's ordered list of requirements within
the PM role's framing — the role's exclusive decision — as submitted for
the PM role's check. These scenarios are the criteria set the
[PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md) screens an order
against, alongside the framing (criterion `framing`). Evaluated by the
`cold-reviewer` role, never executed. The judge's model and prompt
version are recorded with each round verdict.
The judge reads only the criteria set, the framing, and the artifact;
every scenario therefore asks for what the artifact itself carries, and
a fact it must carry — a term's definition, a reference, a reason — is
what these scenarios make it carry. These scenarios stand in for the
pending backlog-order typedef — no definition yet treats the order as an
artifact — and are its first draft; the typedef inherits them. What the
judge cannot catch from the order alone — a recommendation omitted, a
wrong context — is the solutions architect role's to raise through its
interface with the PO role.
A "competent reader" is the cold-reviewer role as its definition
describes it.

## Scenarios

Scenario 1: every item serves a framing
  Given each item in the order
  When its framing reference is read
  Then it names the framing it serves, or is marked declined with a
  reason (the process passes one framing; an order spans many, so this
  scenario is not the `framing` criterion)

Scenario 2: the order states the priority it follows
  Given the order's statement of the roadmap priority it was made
  against
  When the order is compared with that statement
  Then items serving a higher-priority framing precede items serving a
  lower one, or the order states a reason for each exception

Scenario 3: enabler work is placed or declined with reasons
  Given the order's list of enabler recommendations received from the
  solutions architect role
  When the order is read
  Then each recommendation appears in the order with its position
  reasoned, or is declined with a reason

Scenario 4: the order names each item's context
  Given each item
  When it is read
  Then it names the Bounded Context that owns it, and an item crossing
  contexts is marked as such with the escalation to the PM role named

Scenario 5: the next item says whether it is ready
  Given the first item not yet taken up
  When it is read
  Then the order marks it ready and links its checked brief or scenario
  set, or marks it not ready and names what it waits on

## Compile mapping (each Then → one judge-rubric assertion)

| Scenario Then | Judge-rubric assertion |
|---|---|
| 1 — serves a framing | "For each item: framing named, or declined with a reason? Cite any item with neither." |
| 2 — follows stated priority | "Compare the order with the priority it states; cite each inversion and whether a reason is stated. Any unreasoned inversion = fail." |
| 3 — enablers placed or declined | "For each recommendation the order lists: placed with a reasoned position, or declined with a reason? Cite any that is neither." |
| 4 — context named | "Does each item name its owning Bounded Context, with cross-context items marked and their escalation named? Cite any that does not." |
| 5 — next item ready | "Is the first untaken item marked ready with its checked artifact linked, or not ready with what it waits on? Cite." |

## Systemic finding, stated once (applies to every file)

The screen step loads three things: "Read the criteria set at criteria_path, the framing at framing, and the artifact at artifact — nothing else." Several scenarios put in their Given something the judge is not given: the glossary (brief 4), the role definitions (PDR 3), the roadmap priority, the architect's enabler recommendations, the decomposition, and other artifacts' `checked` status (backlog 2, 3, 4, 5). A Then the judge cannot obtain the facts for is not falsifiable *by this judge under this process*, whatever its wording. Two repairs are possible and the owner must pick one: (a) the artifact carries the fact inline (the order states the priority it was made against and the recommendations it received; the record names the decision right it exercised), so the judge reads it from the artifact; or (b) po-output-check's `screen` gains inputs. The sets as written silently assume (b) without the process saying so. The exemplar (decision-brief scenario 3, "in the glossary") has the same flaw; that it was approved does not make it decidable.

## basis/fitness/brief.fitness.md — verdict: findings

1. **Scenario 1 duplicates the `framing` criterion.** "Then it serves the framed outcome, or is marked out of scope with a reason." The process prompt already asks, of every part, whether it traces to the framing or serves no framed outcome, and names that finding `framing`. A judge will report one defect under two criterion names, and the PM's "fail: name the criterion" gets two answers. The only residual is the out-of-scope marking (grounded: lead-po "you decline scope that serves no framed outcome and record the reason"). Required: recast to the residual only — "a statement the brief excludes carries the reason it is excluded" — or delete the scenario and say the framing criterion covers it.
2. **Scenario 4 cites a source the judge does not have.** "every term is the glossary's or defined in the brief" — the judge reads criteria, framing, artifact, nothing else. Required: either "every term is defined in the brief or in the framing" or fix the process (systemic finding). "every referenced artifact is linked" and "no question the shop must ask before starting is left open — or the open questions are listed" are grounded (lead-po: "readable from the artifacts alone, without asking their author") and decidable.
3. **Scenario 5's title does not match its Then.** Title "readable in one sitting"; Then "the problem and the outcome are already stated" after "the first paragraph". Those are different tests. Also no definition on this branch says a brief leads with problem and outcome — it is borrowed from the decision-brief exemplar. Required: retitle "the first paragraph states the problem", and name the source (the `main` brief form or lead-po's "the rest of the shop can act on") or drop it.
4. **Scenario 3 is judge-invented.** "When a competent reader imagines a neighbouring piece of work" — any brief can fail if the judge picks an odd neighbour; the rubric row confirms the judge picks ("Name a neighbouring piece of work"). Grounded in principle (a brief is "a bounded statement ... and its scope"), but bound the pick: "a neighbouring piece of work the framing names or the brief itself mentions". Wobbly as written.
5. **Scenario 2 wobble.** "names no how — no technology, structure, or interface form". Grounded ("You say what, never how"). But the experience principles require the PO to name interaction types (core-task-parity implication); a judge may read "reachable from the command line" as an "interface form". Add: "naming an interaction type is a what, not a form."
6. Honesty on `target-type`: the intro says "the typedef is pending on this branch; the frozen corpus on `main` is the reference". Honest, and the history says the scenarios "rest on the lead-po role's accountabilities and the framing". Does not block use — po-output-check's Data already anticipates no criteria set at all — but see the cross-file note on the chain order.

Stumbles in reading order: "competent reader" (undefined judge standard, recurs across files); "Bounded Context shop" in the intro before it is linked (glossary term, fine for a reader who knows the corpus, not for the judge who reads only this file).

## basis/fitness/product-decision-record.fitness.md — verdict: findings

1. **Scenario 3 misstates the PO's decision rights.** "scope and backlog order the PO's". lead-po: *Decides:* backlog order (exclusive); *Recommends:* scope changes to the PM role; *Escalates to the PM role:* scope conflicts. lead-pm: *Recommends:* scope changes to the PO; *Resolves:* domain conflicts. Scope is not the PO's decision. A judge applying this row would pass a record that the role definitions say is out of domain. Required: fix, and — single-source-of-truth — do not restate the four roles' decision rights here as a second home. The rubric row says "per the role definitions", which the judge cannot read (systemic finding); the honest form is: "Then the record names who decided and the decision right it exercised, or the escalation that settled it" — a presence check the judge can score, leaving whether the right was really theirs to the PM (lead-pm *Resolves:* "a conflict between this role's framing and another role's domain"). As written the scenario asks the judge for a domain ruling that is the PM's.
2. **Sections no definition carries.** "Given the record's alternatives section", "Given the record's consequences section", "Given the record's statement of who decided", and scenario 5's reversibility. The only definition on this branch is "the record of one product-level decision and its reasons". These are the ADR form (Nygard) — a good external standard, and `external-standards-first` supports adopting it — but the set must say so: name the form the sections come from (or the `main` PDR form), or the maker has no definition to produce them from. Required: one sentence in the intro naming the source of the sections.
3. **Scenario 2 wrong actor.** "an alternative ... that a competent shop could have chosen" — a product-level decision is the PM's or PO's, not a shop's (lead-po: shops decide "how a behavior is built"). Change to "that the deciding role could have chosen". Wobbly as written.
4. **Scenario 1 partial duplication of `framing`.** "and names the framing it serves" — tracing is the `framing` criterion. Keep "exactly one decision, as a sentence a reader can act on" (grounded, falsifiable); drop the framing clause or keep it only as "names the framing" (a presence check the framing criterion does not make).
5. Scenarios 4 and 5 are falsifiable as written (each consequence: what changes, for whom, at what cost — cite any missing one; reversibility stated or absent). "hard-to-reverse" also appears in the experience principles in a different sense (actions the corpus classes); no conflict, but a reader of both stumbles.
6. Honesty on `target-type`: says the typedef is pending, and the history says scenario 3 "rests on the four approved roles' decision rights" — which it then misquotes (finding 1). Not blocking; correct the rest.

## basis/fitness/backlog-order.fitness.md — verdict: findings
