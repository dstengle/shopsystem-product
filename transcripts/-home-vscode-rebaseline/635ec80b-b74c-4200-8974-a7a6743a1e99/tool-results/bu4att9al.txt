    13	# Artifact type: feature
    14	
    15	## Identity and ancestry
    16	
    17	- **Type:** `feature` — a Gherkin Feature: one capability described
    18	  from the user's or agent's point of view, with the scenarios that
    19	  state, as requirements, what counts as done for it. A product-level
    20	  artifact: it belongs to the framing it serves (the initiative, once
    21	  that type exists — the product-level problem artifact the
    22	  product-process research proposes) and may hold scenarios owned by
    23	  several Bounded Contexts. The scenario is the atom; the feature is
    24	  its grouping; a Bounded Context's scenario register is the subset of
    25	  scenarios, across all features, tagged to it. The instance is a
    26	  markdown document with frontmatter whose Scenarios section holds one
    27	  fenced Gherkin block — the executable unit the shops receive; the
    28	  other sections are the document's, not Gherkin's. Scenarios are
    29	  executed by the owning shops; the feature as a document is judged by
    30	  the [feature fitness set](../fitness/feature.fitness.md), never
    31	  executed there. `ancestry` names no generic root: a feature is
    32	  neither a request nor a definition.
    33	- **Produced by:** the [PO role](../roles/lead-po.md), with the shops
    34	  that own the behaviors (scope and wording from the PO; steps and edge
    35	  cases from each owning shop; usability and accessibility criteria
    36	  from the product designer role); checked by the
    37	  [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md); its scenarios
    38	  assigned to Bounded Contexts by the solutions architect role in the
    39	  [scenario assignment](../processes/scenario-assignment.md) process.
    40	  **Consumed by:** the shops, each receiving the scenarios tagged to it;
    41	  the [interaction conformance check](../processes/interaction-conformance-check.md)
    42	  for the interaction types named; the changelog rendering the
    43	  product-process research proposes, when its scenarios are reconciled.
    44	
    45	## Required frontmatter
    46	
    47	`type: feature`, `id`, `status`, `version`, `name` (the Feature's
    48	name), `framing` (link to the framing it serves), `owner`, `created`,
    49	`updated`. `approved` does not apply: a feature's terminal state is
    50	`assigned`. Status values and their writers: `draft` (the PO role);
    51	`checked`, `returned`, `pending-definition` (the PO output check's
    52	record step, replacing `draft`); a returned or pending feature the PO
    53	role resubmits goes back to `draft`; `assigned` (the scenario
    54	assignment process's record step, replacing `checked` when every
    55	scenario carries its tag); a feature the assignment process returns
    56	goes to `returned`.
    57	
    58	## Required sections
    59	
    60	1. **Feature** — the Gherkin `Feature:` line with its name, and the
    61	   narrative beneath it: who the capability is for, what they can do,
    62	   and the outcome it serves, in the framing's words.
    63	2. **Contributors** — for every scenario, the shop named as the source
    64	   of its steps and edge cases — the shop the solutions architect's
    65	   decomposition places the behavior in; where the capability has an
    66	   interaction, the product designer role's usability acceptance
    67	   criteria and the accessibility criteria.
    68	3. **Interaction types** — always present: the interaction types the
    69	   capability must be available on, from the
    70	   [core-task list](../experience/core-tasks.md), or "none" with the
    71	   reason when the capability has no interaction.
    72	4. **Scenarios** — one fenced `gherkin` block: the `Feature:` line
    73	   repeated, an optional `Background:` (its Given steps judged as any
    74	   step), then each `Scenario:` carrying the tags `@feature:<id>` (so a
    75	   scenario travels alone to a register and still names its feature —
    76	   the reason the Feature line's tag inheritance is not relied on),
    77	   `@hash:<sha>` (a hash of the scenario's text), and, once assigned,
    78	   `@bounded-context:<name>`; Given/When/Then with one action or event
    79	   in the When and an outcome observable in the running system in the
    80	   Then; no step names an implementation detail.
    81	5. **Edges** — a table of every failure and boundary case the framing
    82	   or a contributing shop named: case · who named it · the scenario
    83	   that covers it, or "out of scope" with the reason.
    84	
    85	## Rules
    86	
    87	- A scenario's `@bounded-context:` tag is written by the solutions
    88	  architect role at assignment, never by the PO role; at the PO output
    89	  check no scenario carries one. The tag confirms the Contributors'
    90	  shop or corrects it; a corrected scenario returns to the PO role for
    91	  co-production with the newly named shop.
    92	- A changed scenario text is a new scenario with a new `@hash:`.
    93	  Whether the hash matches the text is a mechanical check, to be filed
    94	  as a lint.
    95	- A scenario written without the shop named as its source is not
    96	  co-produced and fails the check.
    97	
    98	## Commitment (Definition of Done)
    99	
   100	A feature is done when it has passed the PO output check against its
   101	fitness set and the framing, and every scenario carries a
   102	`@bounded-context:` tag. **Consequence on failure:** it is returned
   103	with the criterion named and no scenario enters a register.
   104	
   105	## Sources
   106	
   107	Gherkin (Feature, narrative, Background, Scenario, tags — the block's
   108	form); Cucumber's guidance on the three amigos (co-production); the
   109	experience principles `core-task-parity` and `accessible-by-standard`.
   110	
   111	## Derived review checklist
   112	
   113	- Feature line and narrative present, tied to the framing. *(§Required sections 1; fitness 6)*
   114	- Each scenario one observable behavior, no how. *(§Required sections 4; fitness 1)*
   115	- Every scenario has a named source shop; designer criteria where there is an interaction. *(§Required sections 2; fitness 2)*
   116	- `@feature:` and `@hash:` on every scenario; no `@bounded-context:` at the check. *(§Required sections 4; §Rules; fitness 3)*
   117	- Every listed edge covered or excluded with reason. *(§Required sections 5; fitness 4)*
   118	- Interaction types section present, named or "none" with reason. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 5)*

    14	
    15	# Fitness set: feature
    16	
    17	A feature is a Gherkin Feature — one capability from the user's or
    18	agent's point of view with the scenarios that state what counts as
    19	done for it — submitted by the PO role, product-level, its scenarios
    20	later assigned to Bounded Contexts by the solutions architect role.
    21	The scenarios are executable by the owning shops; this set judges the
    22	feature as a document and is never executed. These scenarios are the
    23	criteria set the [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md)
    24	screens a feature against, alongside the framing (criterion
    25	`framing`). Evaluated by the `cold-reviewer` role. The judge reads only
    26	the criteria set, the framing, and the feature; a fact the feature must
    27	carry — a source shop, a tag, an edge — is what these scenarios make it
    28	carry. Assignment is not judged here: the `@bounded-context:` tag is
    29	set after the check.
    30	
    31	## Scenarios
    32	
    33	Scenario 1: each scenario is one observable behavior
    34	  Given a scenario in the feature's Gherkin block, Background steps
    35	  included
    36	  When its steps are read
    37	  Then the When is one action or event, the Then an outcome observable
    38	  in the running system, and no step names an implementation detail
    39	
    40	Scenario 2: every scenario has a named source
    41	  Given the Contributors section
    42	  When each scenario is read against it
    43	  Then a shop is named as the source of that scenario's steps and edge
    44	  cases, and, where the Interaction types section names a type, the
    45	  product designer role's usability acceptance criteria and the
    46	  accessibility criteria are present (the lead-product-designer role's
    47	  accountabilities; the experience principle `accessible-by-standard`)
    48	
    49	Scenario 3: identity tags are present and assignment is not
    50	  Given each scenario's tags
    51	  When they are read
    52	  Then `@feature:<id>` and `@hash:<sha>` are present and no
    53	  `@bounded-context:` tag is (assignment writes it after this check;
    54	  whether the hash matches the text is a lint, not this judge's)
    55	
    56	Scenario 4: every listed edge is covered
    57	  Given the Edges table and the cases the framing names
    58	  When each case is read
    59	  Then it names a scenario that covers it or is marked out of scope with
    60	  a reason, and every case the framing names appears in the table
    61	
    62	Scenario 5: interaction types are stated
    63	  Given the Interaction types section
    64	  When it is read
    65	  Then it names the types the capability must be available on, or
    66	  "none" with a reason the framing bears out (the experience principle
    67	  `core-task-parity`)
    68	
    69	Scenario 6: the feature says who it is for and why
    70	  Given the Feature line and its narrative
    71	  When they are read
    72	  Then they name who the capability is for, what they can do, and the
    73	  outcome it serves, and that outcome is the framing's
    74	
    75	## Compile mapping (each Then → one judge-rubric assertion)
    76	
    77	| Scenario Then | Judge-rubric assertion |
    78	|---|---|
    79	| 1 — one observable behavior | "For each scenario and the Background: is the When one action, the Then observable in the running system, and no step implementation-specific? Cite any failing step." |
    80	| 2 — named source | "For each scenario: is a source shop named in Contributors? Where a type is named, are usability and accessibility criteria present? Cite or name the absence." |
    81	| 3 — identity tags, no assignment | "For each scenario: `@feature:` present? `@hash:` present? `@bounded-context:` absent? Cite any scenario failing one." |
    82	| 4 — edges covered | "For each row of the Edges table and each case the framing names: a covering scenario or a reasoned exclusion? Any uncovered or missing case = fail." |
    83	| 5 — interaction types stated | "Does the Interaction types section name types, or 'none' with a reason the framing bears out? Cite the sentence or its absence." |
    84	| 6 — narrative | "Does the Feature narrative name who, what, and the outcome, and is the outcome the framing's? Cite the lines or their absence." |

    12	# Guideline: feature
    13	
    14	**Voice principle.** Write the feature for the person or agent it
    15	serves and for the shops that will run its scenarios against the real
    16	system: a narrative that says who and why, then scenarios that each
    17	state one action and one observable outcome, with nothing about how.
    18	
    19	**Highlights (the layer compiled into generating context):** a Feature
    20	line and a narrative — who, what, the outcome, in the framing's words ·
    21	one When, one observable Then, no how · each owning shop named as the
    22	source of its scenarios' steps and edges · usability and accessibility
    23	criteria where there is an interaction · `@feature:` and `@hash:` on every
    24	scenario, no `@bounded-context:` at the check · every named edge
    25	covered or excluded with a reason · the interaction types stated, or "none" with a reason.
    26	
    27	**Layers:** this guideline adds feature rules on top of the
    28	[base writing style](base-writing-style.md); the base always applies
    29	and is never overridden. When rules conflict, an approved principle
    30	beats the [feature typedef](../artifacts/feature.md), which beats this
    31	guideline. Gherkin's own syntax governs the Feature, Background, and
    32	Given/When/Then form. Every rule feeds the
    33	[feature fitness set](../fitness/feature.fitness.md), scored in the
    34	[PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md).
    35	
    36	---
    37	
    38	## Rules
    39	
    40	**1. A narrative that says who and why.**
    41	Before: "Feature: Run list" with scenarios beneath and no narrative.
    42	After: "Feature: Failed runs visible in the run list / An operator
    43	checking last night's runs / can see which failed without opening each
    44	/ so that a failed run is noticed within one glance (the framing's
    45	outcome)."
    46	*Test:* read the Feature line and the lines beneath it. *Criterion:*
    47	they name who the capability is for, what they can do, and the outcome
    48	it serves, and the outcome is the framing's. *Decision:* yes/no per
    49	feature.
    50	*Derived check:* judged — feature fitness scenario 6.
    51	
    52	**2. One action, one observable outcome, no implementation.**
    53	Before: "When the user clicks the red button and the API returns 200,
    54	Then the database row is updated."
    55	After: "When the operator lists runs, Then each failed run is marked as
    56	failed in the list."
    57	*Test:* for each scenario, count the actions in the When and check the
    58	Then against what a person or agent could observe from outside the
    59	system. *Criterion:* one action or event; an outcome observable in the
    60	running system; no step names an implementation detail — a component,
    61	call, storage, algorithm, format, or vendor. *Decision:* yes/no per scenario.
    62	*Derived check:* judged — feature fitness scenario 1.
    63	
    64	**3. Name a source shop for every scenario.**
    65	Before: a feature with no contributors section.
    66	After: "Contributors: the reporting shop supplied the steps and edge
    67	cases for the list scenarios; the export shop for the CSV scenarios;
    68	the product designer role supplied the usability criterion (marked
    69	within one glance) and the accessibility criterion (failure not by
    70	color alone)."
    71	*Test:* read each scenario against the Contributors section.
    72	*Criterion:* every scenario has a named source shop; where the
    73	Interaction types section names a type, both designer criteria are
    74	present. *Decision:* yes/no per scenario.
    75	*Derived check:* judged — feature fitness scenario 2.
    76	
    77	**4. Tag every scenario with its feature and hash; none with a
    78	context.**
    79	Before: a scenario with a title only; or a scenario carrying
    80	`@bounded-context:reporting` at the check.
    81	After: `@feature:failed-runs-visible @hash:3f9a…` on the line above
    82	`Scenario:`; no `@bounded-context:` tag until assignment writes it.
    83	*Test:* read each scenario's tag line. *Criterion:* `@feature:` and
    84	`@hash:` are present and `@bounded-context:` is absent. *Decision:*
    85	yes/no per scenario.
    86	*Derived check:* judged — feature fitness scenario 3.
    87	
    88	**5. List every edge and cover or exclude it.**
    89	Before: a happy path only, with the cancelled-run case the shop named
    90	absent from the Edges table.
    91	After: an Edges row "cancelled run · reporting shop · scenario
    92	@feature:failed-runs-visible/cancelled" and a row "runs older than
    93	the retention window · framing · out of scope: the archive feature
    94	owns them".
    95	*Test:* read the Edges table and the cases the framing names.
    96	*Criterion:* every row names a covering scenario or a reasoned
    97	exclusion, and every case the framing names has a row. *Decision:*
    98	yes/no per case.
    99	*Derived check:* judged — feature fitness scenario 4.
   100	
   101	**6. State the interaction types, or "none" with a reason.**
   102	Before: no Interaction types section.
   103	After: "Interaction types: cli, gui — from the core-task list's 'read
   104	a decision' row." or "Interaction types: none — the capability is a
   105	nightly reconciliation with no person or agent at an interface."
   106	*Test:* read the Interaction types section. *Criterion:* it names the
   107	types the capability must be available on (the typedef requires them
   108	to come from the [core-task list](../experience/core-tasks.md);
   109	whether they do is not this judge's check), or "none" with a reason
   110	the framing bears out. *Decision:* yes/no per feature.
   111	*Derived check:* judged — feature fitness scenario 5.

    18	## 1. `basis/artifacts/feature.md` — verdict: **findings**
    19	
    20	**F1. The instance's file form is unstated, and the required sections are not
    21	Gherkin-legal — an author cannot write an instance and a validator cannot check
    22	one.** (Commitment: "a validator can check any instance from it alone and an
    23	author can write a conforming instance".) The artifact-typedef rule says "A
    24	section is a markdown heading", and §Required sections lists five headings:
    25	Feature, Contributors, Interaction types, Scenarios, Edges. §Sources says
    26	"Gherkin (Feature, narrative, Background, Scenario, tags)". Gherkin has no
    27	headings, no "Contributors" or "Edges" block, and no "hash field" — free text is
    28	legal only under `Feature:`/`Scenario:` lines, and a bare `hash: 3f9a…` line above
    29	a Scenario (the guideline's rule 4 After) is a parse error. So either the instance
    30	is a markdown document with an embedded Gherkin block that shops extract, or it
    31	is a `.feature` file and the non-Gherkin sections and the hash must be carried as
    32	tags or comments. The typedef says neither. Quote: "each scenario with a tag
    33	naming the feature, a `@bounded-context:<name>` tag once assigned, and a hash
    34	field". *Required:* state the instance form (recommend: markdown with YAML
    35	frontmatter; §4 Scenarios holds one fenced Gherkin block that is the executable
    36	unit shops receive), and state where the hash lives in Gherkin-legal form (a tag
    37	such as `@hash:3f9a…`, or a `#` comment line) so the lint the Rules promise can
    38	find it.
    39	
    40	**F2. Who "owns a behavior" before assignment is not stated, so §2 Contributors
    41	and the co-production Rule cannot be decided against the later tag.** §2 requires
    42	"each owning shop named as the source of the steps and edge cases"; §Rules says
    43	"A scenario written without the shop that owns its behavior is not co-produced and
    44	fails the check"; but the same Rules say ownership is written by the architect
    45	"at assignment, never by the PO role" — after the check. The lead-po role resolves
    46	this (the decomposition is admissible evidence "for where a scenario lands"; the
    47	architect "supplies context ownership" during co-production), but the typedef does
    48	not, and it does not say what happens when the tag the architect writes names a
    49	context other than the Contributors' shop. *Required:* one sentence in §2 or
    50	§Rules: the owning shop named in Contributors is the one the solutions architect's
    51	decomposition places the behavior in; at assignment the tag confirms or corrects
    52	it, and a corrected assignment returns the scenario for co-production with the
    53	newly named shop (or whatever the owner decides).
    54	
    55	**F3. The named edges have no required home, so §5 and fitness 4 have nothing to
    56	read.** §5 covers "each failure or boundary case the framing or a shop's
    57	contribution names", and the guideline's rule 5 test reads cases "the Contributors
    58	section names" — but §2 only requires each shop to be "named as the source of the
    59	steps and edge cases", not that the edge cases be listed. A judge can find the
    60	framing's edges; the shops' edges exist only if a scenario or an exclusion already
    61	mentions them, which makes the check circular. *Required:* §2 (or §5) requires the
    62	list: the failure and boundary cases each contributing shop and the framing named,
    63	each pointing at its scenario or its out-of-scope reason.
    64	
    65	**F4. Status transitions are incomplete.** "`status` (draft | checked | returned |
    66	pending-definition | assigned — replaces `checked` when the solutions architect
    67	role has tagged every scenario)". Stated: checked → assigned. Not stated: what
    68	follows `returned` and `pending-definition` (back to draft? resubmitted as is?),
    69	and which process writes `assigned` (the linked scenario-assignment process,
    70	presumably — say so, since the PO output check declares its values "this
    71	process's until the artifact typedefs define their own" and this typedef now
