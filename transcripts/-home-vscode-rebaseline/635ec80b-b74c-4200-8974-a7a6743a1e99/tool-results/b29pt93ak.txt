    12	# Base writing style
    13	
    14	These rules apply to everything written for a human reader, regardless of
    15	document type. Document types add a format layer on top of this base; they
    16	never override it.
    17	
    18	## Goal
    19	
    20	The reader gets the point of any paragraph in one read, any section in 30
    21	seconds, and the whole document in two minutes. Write for the first read.
    22	
    23	## Lead with the point
    24	
    25	At every scale, state the conclusion first and the support after.
    26	
    27	- A document opens with what it found or decided, not how it got there.
    28	- A section opens with its main claim, then evidence.
    29	- A paragraph opens with its point, then elaboration.
    30	
    31	If a document type defines a structure, that structure is just this rule
    32	applied to that type. If no structure is defined, use this rule to invent
    33	one.
    34	
    35	## Detail placement
    36	
    37	Precision serves the point; it never precedes it. Formal specification
    38	language, methods, raw data, scoring tables, and citations go after the
    39	claim they support — or in an appendix. The body is for understanding.
    40	
    41	## Sentences
    42	
    43	- One idea per sentence.
    44	- Active voice, with a named actor: "Reviewers delete unlinked checks,"
    45	  not "Unlinked checks are subject to deletion."
    46	- Prefer the short common word: "use" not "utilize," "end" not
    47	  "terminate," "before" not "prior to."
    48	
    49	## Terminology
    50	
    51	- Never use a metaphor as a technical term. If a word describes a thing
    52	  by what it resembles instead of what it literally does, replace it with
    53	  the literal description. Test: could a competent reader from outside
    54	  the team work out the meaning from the words alone? If not, rewrite.
    55	- Banned words. Never use these, in any form:
    56	  - "load-bearing" → say "other work depends on it" or "critical to
    57	    operations"
    58	  - "surface" (as in context surface, config surface) → say "input,"
    59	    "source," or "channel"
    60	  - "seam" → say "boundary" or "integration point"
    61	  - "scar tissue" → describe the actual problem, e.g. "an unreviewed
    62	    workaround that was never removed"
    63	- Explain every insider reference — a past incident, an internal system,
    64	  a person — in one plain sentence, or cut it. Assume the reader knows
    65	  none of our history.
    66	
    67	## Style examples
    68	
    69	These examples exist only to show sentence style. The domain (a public
    70	library) is deliberately unrelated to our work. Copy the style; never
    71	copy the content or vocabulary into real documents.
    72	
    73	bad:
    74	
    75	> Unreturned inventory constitutes scar tissue on the circulation loop
    76	> and rapidly becomes load-bearing for downstream shelving throughput.
    77	
    78	good:
    79	
    80	> Overdue books pile up at the front desk, and staff spend their time
    81	> reshelving late returns instead of helping visitors.
    82	
    83	The bad sentence uses two metaphors as terms and never names an actor.
    84	The good sentence says who is affected and what actually happens, in
    85	plain words.

    12	# Glossary
    13	
    14	## How the list combines
    15	
    16	The defined-term list is this glossary combined with every schema element
    17	name. Per the `use-defined-terms` principle, a writer choosing between
    18	terms uses one of these when one fits.
    19	
    20	## Terms
    21	
    22	- **artifact type** — a named, schema-defined document type (e.g.
    23	  `decision-brief`); the term the schema registry uses. Not "kind".
    24	- **data type** — a named, schema-defined structure that is not a
    25	  human-readable document (e.g. `review`); passed between process steps.
    26	- **simple type** — a JSON Schema primitive (`string`, `integer`,
    27	  `boolean`, `array`, `object`), usable inline without registration.
    28	- **typedef** — the single source a type is generated from; templates and
    29	  schema fragments are its renderings (owned by shopsystem-knowledge).
    30	- **schema** — the machine-checkable shape of a type: fields, types,
    31	  enums, required sections.
    32	- **process definition** — the source of truth for a process: header
    33	  (purpose, outcomes, roles), data section, steps section.
    34	- **step** — one unit of a process: an agent step (carries a prompt) or a
    35	  runtime step (carries `set`, `run`, or `branches`; no prose).
    36	- **rendering** — a generated output of a definition (a skill, a
    37	  diagram, a template); never edited by hand, never the source of truth.
    38	- **guiding statement** — an optional header element of a process
    39	  definition that directs judgment across the whole process; compiled
    40	  into every rendering.
    41	- **fitness set** — judged (never executed) Given/When/Then scenarios
    42	  scoring an artifact type's quality.
    43	- **guideline** — prose quality rules for an artifact type, each with a
    44	  test, a criterion, and a yes/no decision.
    45	- **role** — a named capability contract and set of accountabilities,
    46	  assigned by process steps to whoever fills it; sequencing lives in
    47	  process definitions, not roles.
    48	- **principle** — a standing rule about how we work: name, statement,
    49	  rationale, implications (see the principles document's opening
    50	  definition).
    51	- **seed layer** — the hand-approved definitions the regress terminates
    52	  at: the principle set plus one typedef per definition document type.
    53	- **owner** — the role that approves changes to a definition; named in
    54	  every definition's frontmatter.
    55	- **definition chain** — the six linked definitions of good for one
    56	  artifact type; required before instances are authored or rewritten.
    57	- **keeper** — a corpus record whose action is keep-rewrite; rewritten
    58	  forward through its type's approved chain, never used as-is.
    59	- **rewrite-forward** — the default for keepers: re-author to the
    60	  approved standard; repair-in-place only for records already at the bar.
    61	- **rebaseline** — Phase 1 of the re-founding arc: the active tree comes
    62	  to hold only chain-conforming records; retired mass moves to the
    63	  archive.
    64	- **scope** (of a principle set) — the level the set governs and whose
    65	  context it loads into: working (every activity) or architecture (the
    66	  designed system).
    67	- **transcript** — the runtime log of turns; unscoped, not a governed
    68	  artifact, never loaded into context.
    69	- **conversation** — a scoped, bounded discussion attached to exactly one
    70	  anchor; every conversation belongs to a process.
    71	- **anchor** — the governed record a conversation attaches to: a session
    72	  record, a review record, or a work item; carries the conversation's
    73	  state across transcript boundaries.
    74	- **conversation type** — discovery (anchor: a session record), review
    75	  (anchor: a review record and its outcomes), or work (anchor: a work
    76	  item).
    77	- **session record** — the anchor record of a discovery conversation:
    78	  outcome, produced and revised lists, open threads, select quotes.
    79	- **review** — one bounded conversation in which the authority decides
    80	  on presented material. Replaces "sitting".
    81	- **review record** — the anchor record of a review: the material
    82	  presented and the outcomes applied; each decision traces in the
    83	  changed artifacts' Document History, never in a standing ledger.
    84	- **park** — the failsafe for work that cannot pass review within its
    85	  round cap: set aside with a filed finding.
    86	- **round cap** — the declared maximum review rounds before a loop's
    87	  failsafe exit fires.
    88	- **action** — the approved choice for one record: keep-rewrite, keep,
    89	  retire, or terminal, with its target.
    90	- **authority-call** — a row marker on a migration-plan row awaiting a
    91	  decision from the authority; not an action — the row has no effect and
    92	  is out of scope for any run until ruled.
    93	- **action table** — the table of records and their actions; approved row
    94	  by row or in blocks at a review. Drives any bulk record change.
    95	- **close-out** — the mechanical execution of the migration plan's
    96	  pre-decided retire and terminal actions: snapshot, delete, archive
    97	  move, a loud post-check, and the branch promotion; no review loop.
    98	  All stages run consecutively at cut-over.
    99	- **snapshot tag** — the annotated git tag (`pre-migration`) on `main`'s
   100	  pre-execution commit, preserving the full corpus for terminal-recovery;
   101	  after close-out a terminal record exists only there.
   102	- **migration plan** — the rebaseline's action table plus the order of
   103	  the per-type migration runs. Replaces "rebaseline bill".
   104	- **run** — one execution of a process, anchored to a work item; states:
   105	  running, held, done, cancelled.
   106	- **hold** — a run state: paused with its step and data preserved in its
   107	  anchor, by inactivity or by an ask; a held run is resumed or
   108	  cancelled, never dropped.
   109	- **checkpoint** — updating a conversation's anchor when a transcript
   110	  ends mid-conversation; a checkpoint is not a close.
   111	- **branched conversation** — a conversation run as a sub-process of
   112	  another run; its anchor records the parent (`branched-from`).
   113	- **Bounded Context** — a deliberately drawn region of the product with
   114	  one internal language, one model, and one set of contracts to other
   115	  regions; drawn by design, never discovered after the fact (Evans,
   116	  *Domain-Driven Design*).
   117	- **shop** — the entity that builds, operates, and evolves part of the
   118	  product. Two types: the lead shop (the system-level coordinator; owns
   119	  product-level artifacts and no Bounded Context) and the BC-shop (owns
   120	  and produces exactly one Bounded Context).
   121	- **activity** — a discrete unit of work performed inside a shop,
   122	  following a contract or process, with inputs, outcomes, and provenance
   123	  recorded. What an actor is stays unconstrained; the activity has the
   124	  same shape whoever performs it.
   125	- **contract** — a named, versioned statement of what one entity
   126	  promises another: the schemas exchanged, the meaning of each
   127	  operation, the error behavior, and the relationship kind. Offered as a
   128	  product contract (on a Bounded Context) or an operational contract (on
   129	  a shop); nothing outside a contract is promised.
   130	- **relationship kind** — the named pattern a contract declares for how
   131	  two Bounded Contexts relate, drawn from the context-mapping catalogue
   132	  (Evans, *Domain-Driven Design*): customer–supplier, conformist,
   133	  anti-corruption layer, open host service, published language, shared
   134	  kernel, or separate ways.
   135	- **vehicle** — the message type a request to a Bounded Context shop
   136	  travels in; chosen from the pre-state, never from the request's
   137	  wording.
   138	- **pre-state** — what a Bounded Context actually is before a change,
   139	  read from its contracts and its scenario register, never from its
   140	  internals.
   141	- **framing** — the PM role's recorded statement of what a request is
   142	  about: the originator, the problem taken to be worth solving, and the
   143	  outcome it serves; the exclusive decision of `lead-pm`.
   144	- **acceptance scenario** — a Gherkin scenario that states, as a
   145	  requirement, what counts as done for one behavior; co-produced by the
   146	  PO role and the owning Bounded Context shop; held in that shop's
   147	  scenario register.
   148	- **scenario register** — a Bounded Context shop's own list of the
   149	  acceptance scenarios it holds, with their hashes and states; read in
   150	  full across every context when a claim spans contexts.
   151	- **ask** — a question one activity puts to another role in place of
   152	  its output, carrying a default and a checkpoint; the run holds, the
   153	  role answers, the step resumes — never a wait in place (data type
   154	  `ask`; process-definition typedef §Run lifecycle).
   155	- **clarify** — an ask from a Bounded Context shop to the lead shop on
   156	  scope, vocabulary, structure, or contract; answered by the role whose
   157	  domain the question falls in.
   158	- **decomposition** — the solutions architect's record of which Bounded
   159	  Context owns which capability, and the relationship kind of each
   160	  contract between contexts.
   161	- **enabler work** — technical work that makes a feature possible;
   162	  recommended by the solutions architect, placed in the backlog by the
   163	  PO role.
   164	- **interaction type** — one way a person or an agent reaches the
   165	  product: command line (CLI), full-screen terminal (TUI), graphical or
   166	  web (GUI), API and SDK, conversational, voice, or a generated document
   167	  or notification; each honours its own conventions within one
   168	  experience.
   169	- **experience guidance corpus** — the product designer role's
   170	  principle set and guidelines for the experience: design principles,
   171	  vocabulary and voice, core-task parity across interaction types,
   172	  interaction patterns per type, and the accessibility target; the
   173	  role's exclusive domain.
   174	- **intent** — a desired outcome expressed by an originator at the
   175	  product's edge; enters the product through a contract and keeps its
   176	  provenance through every translation and delegation.

    13	
    14	# Artifact type: process-definition
    15	
    16	## Identity and ancestry
    17	
    18	- **Type:** `process-definition` — the single source of truth for a
    19	  process: what it is for, what a run produces, and every step in a form
    20	  a runtime can construct a workflow from. Skills, flow
    21	  diagrams, and fabro graphs (fabro is the fleet's workflow-orchestrator
    22	  runtime) are its renderings.
    23	- **Produced by:** seed drafting or governed evolution. **Consumed by:**
    24	  the compiler (renderings), agents (via the rendered skill), reviewers
    25	  (conformance and loop-exit review), fabro (via annotations).
    26	
    27	## Required frontmatter
    28	
    29	`type: process-definition`, `id`, `owner`, `status`, `created`,
    30	`updated`; `produces` (artifact
    31	types a run creates; the generic root `definition` covers runs creating
    32	definition documents; empty when the run's value is state change);
    33	`condition-language: cel`; optional: `carried-by` (the rendered skill's
    34	id), `condition-functions` (declared extensions, name and signature),
    35	`annotations` (process-level rendering metadata, keyed by rendering
    36	target); `hold-after` (ISO 8601 duration — the inactivity window after
    37	which the runtime holds a run; required for any process that carries a
    38	conversation); `ask-cap` (ISO 8601 duration — how long an ask may stand
    39	unanswered before its default applies; required for any process whose
    40	steps carry `asks`).
    41	
    42	## Required sections
    43	
    44	1. **Purpose** — one short paragraph.
    45	2. **Guiding statement** (optional) — directs judgment across the whole
    46	   process; the compiler copies it into every rendering.
    47	3. **Outcomes** — each names the step or check that witnesses it.
    48	4. **Roles** — roles and their accountability; role files carry identity,
    49	   never sequencing.
    50	5. **Flow (compiled)** — the diagram generated from the steps; never
    51	   edited by hand.
    52	6. **Data** — process-local value names. Simple types (JSON Schema
    53	   primitives) inline; every structured shape is a `$ref` to a defined
    54	   type with an explicit source: `from:` is a relative link to the
    55	   defining file, or `pkg:<package>/<type>` when the type lives in
    56	   another package and is fetched through that package's contract tool
    57	   (e.g. `shop-knowledge schema session-record`). Every distinct `$ref`
    58	   carries `from:` at least once; the linter verifies local sources
    59	   define the referenced type. No structured shape is ever defined here.
    60	   A value may declare `initial` — its value at run start.
    61	7. **Steps** — the executable part (shape below).
    62	8. **Derived checks** — a table: outcome, check, kind, where; every row
    63	   cites its clause or is deleted.
    64	
    65	Header elements 1–4 are bolded labels before the first heading; 5–8 are
    66	markdown headings in order (a section is a heading, per the
    67	artifact-typedef rule).
    68	
    69	## The steps section
    70	
    71	Top-level keys: `start` (first step id); optional `parameters` (data
    72	values supplied at instantiation rather than produced by any step);
    73	optional `result` (the data value a run returns — the artifact, not a
    74	status record; omit only when the outcomes pin the run's value). `end`
    75	is the reserved terminator id for `next`. Each step:
    76	
    77	- `id`, `name`; `run-by` — `{role, execution: agent}`,
    78	  `{role, execution: human}` (a role a person holds, e.g. an authority review), or `{execution: runtime}`; `fresh-context: true` where a role
    79	  must not carry memory between runs.
    80	- `inputs` / `outputs` — lists of declared data names; the typed contract
    81	  is the isolation mechanism (a step reads only what it lists).
    82	- Agent and human steps carry `prompt` — **the only prose allowed in a
    83	  step** (for a human step it is the review's ask).
    84	- Runtime steps carry `set` (CEL assignments to data values or their
    85	  fields), `run` (command templates
    86	  with `${...}` interpolation from typed inputs; `atomic: true` binds the
    87	  lines into one all-or-nothing act), or `branches`.
    88	- `checks` — CEL expressions over declared data.
    89	- Routing: `next`, or `branches` of `{label, when (CEL), next}` rows plus
    90	  one `else`. **Every loop declares its exits as labeled branch rows** —
    91	  a reached-state success exit, a round or budget cap, or both.
    92	- `asks` — optional list of role ids the step may put an ask to (the
    93	  [`ask`](../types/ask.md) data type). An agent or human step listed
    94	  with `asks` may return an ask in place of its outputs; a step without
    95	  `asks` may not ask. Every ask names a `default` and a `checkpoint`; the
    96	  step's prompt states what kind of information it may ask for — never
    97	  a judgment the step's role owes. Steps that frame or plan ask early;
    98	  an ask from a producing step wastes the work not in its checkpoint.
    99	- `annotations` — per-step rendering metadata keyed by target (e.g.
   100	  `fabro: {model, max_attempts}`); the definition itself ignores them.
   101	- `run-by: {execution: sub-process, process: <process-id>, from: <source>}`
   102	  runs another process definition as one step: the step's inputs map to
   103	  the child's parameters and its outputs receive the child's result. A
   104	  conversation invoked this way is a branched conversation: the child's
   105	  anchor records the parent run.
   106	
   107	## Run lifecycle
   108	
   109	A run is one execution of a process, anchored to a work item in the
   110	registry. Run states: `running`, `held`, `done`, `cancelled`.
   111	
   112	- **Hold** pauses a run: the current step and every data value persist in
   113	  the run's anchor, and the work item records the state. A held run is
   114	  resumed at its recorded step or cancelled with a reason — never
   115	  silently dropped.
   116	- The `hold-after` window makes parking automatic: a run with no activity
   117	  inside the window is held by the runtime. Unfinished work parks itself
   118	  with a named resume point; nothing dangles in the lead repo.
   119	- **Ask** holds a run the same way, with the ask recorded on the anchor
   120	  and routed to whoever fills the role it names — a person or an agent;
   121	  the answer is written to the ask and the run resumes at the asking
   122	  step with the ask in its inputs, in a fresh context loaded from the
   123	  step's declared inputs and the checkpoint. There is no synchronous
   124	  form: no step waits in place for an answer. An unanswered ask resolves
   125	  to its `default` at the process's `ask-cap` (an ISO 8601 duration,
   126	  required for any process whose steps carry `asks`), and the run
   127	  resumes on the default with the ask marked `defaulted`. An ask that
   128	  recurs across runs is a gap in a definition; the answering role files
   129	  the definition change, and the ask stops.
   130	- **Cancel** closes the run's work item with a reason and files the
   131	  resulting actions the outcomes demand.
   132	
   133	## Rendering contract
   134	
   135	The compiler generates: the in-document flow diagram (typed inputs and
   136	outputs on every node; the result on the end node) and the skill —
   137	front-matter (`generated: true`, `generated-by`, `derived-from`,
   138	`source`, `source-digest`) and body (purpose, guiding statement, result,
   139	diagram, one section per step with the prompt verbatim). Conformance
   140	check: re-run the compiler and diff.
   141	
   142	## Commitment (Definition of Done)
   143	
   144	A process definition is done when it compiles — every `$ref` resolves,
   145	the diagram and renderings generate, `result` (if declared) names a data
   146	value — and every loop declares its exit. **Consequence on failure:** it
   147	cannot be dispatched, carried by a skill, or cited by a check.
   148	
   149	## Sources
   150	
   151	ISO/IEC/IEEE 24774 (name/purpose/outcomes header); GitHub Actions (step
   152	records with typed inputs/outputs); CNCF Serverless Workflow
   153	(data-condition transitions); CEL — Common Expression Language
   154	(mechanically translatable conditions); Essence-style state exits plus a
   155	round cap (the dual-exit rule).
   156	
   157	## Derived review checklist
   158	
   159	- Compiles clean: refs resolve, diagram and renderings regenerate
   160	  byte-stable. *(Commitment)*
   161	- No prose inside step records outside `prompt` fields; section
   162	  introduction prose is body text, not step content. *(§The steps section)*
   163	- Every loop has labeled success and/or cap exits. *(§The steps section)*
   164	- Every step with `asks` belongs to a process with `ask-cap`; every ask
   165	  names a default and a checkpoint. *(§The steps section, §Run lifecycle)*
   166	- Outcomes each name a witness. *(§Required sections 3)*
   167	- `result`, if absent, is justified by outcomes that pin the run's value.
   168	  *(§The steps section)*

    12	# Data type: ask
    13	
    14	## Purpose
    15	
    16	A question one activity puts to another role, returned by an agent or
    17	human step in place of its output. Produced by any step whose process
    18	lists it under `asks`; consumed by the runtime, which holds the run,
    19	routes the ask to the named role, and re-enters the step with the
    20	`answer` filled — the held-and-resumed form defined in the
    21	[process-definition typedef](../artifacts/process-definition.md) (§The
    22	steps section, §Run lifecycle). An ask is for information the asker
    23	cannot have — a pre-state, an intent the originator did not state, a
    24	decision reserved to another role — never for a judgment the asking
    25	role owes; the `kind` field names which. The `checkpoint` carries the
    26	asker's partial output so the resume repeats no work. A `clarify` is an
    27	ask from a Bounded Context shop to the lead shop; it conforms to this
    28	shape.
    29	
    30	## Schema
    31	
    32	```yaml
    33	schema:
    34	  type: object
    35	  fields:
    36	    to: {type: string}                      # the role asked; must appear in the step's `asks` list
    37	    kind: {type: string, enum: [pre-state, intent, reserved-decision, term, contract]}
    38	    question: {type: string}
    39	    default: {type: string}                 # what the asker will do if unanswered at the cap
    40	    checkpoint: {type: string}              # the asker's partial output, enough to resume from
    41	    answer: {type: string, optional: true}  # filled by the answering role
    42	    answered_by: {type: string, optional: true}
    43	    resolution:
    44	      type: string
    45	      enum: [answered, defaulted, cancelled]
    46	      optional: true
    47	```

    13	# Working principles (compiled into every session)
    14	
    15	These statements govern every activity in this shop. The full set —
    16	rationales, implications, fitness screen — is the source document
    17	named in the front-matter; on conflict the source wins.
    18	
    19	- **Define what good looks like up front** (`define-good-up-front`):
    20	  - Every activity MUST operate from a stated definition of what good looks like.
    21	  - That definition MUST drive both the performance of the activity and its check.
    22	  - The check MUST sit with a different role holding a different accountability.
    23	- **Govern the generating context** (`governed-context`):
    24	  - Everything loaded into an agent's generating context — prompts, skills, memories, primers — MUST trace to an approved definition or a governed record.
    25	  - An unsanctioned context channel MUST NOT be created or retained.
    26	- **Every activity belongs to a process** (`no-orphan-activities`):
    27	  - Every activity in the system MUST be part of a defined process with stated expected outcomes, expected outputs, and possible resulting actions.
    28	  - Every long-running loop MUST declare its exit — a reached-state success exit, a round or budget cap, or both.
    29	- **Use defined terms** (`use-defined-terms`):
    30	  - Important terms MUST be defined in the system, in the glossary or as a schema element.
    31	  - A term is important when a reader must know it to perform or check the work.
    32	  - When more than one term could carry a statement, the writer MUST use a defined term if one is available.
    33	- **Use external standards first** (`external-standards-first`):
    34	  - A definition MUST adopt an established external form where one fits.
    35	  - Bespoke structure MUST be justified by a recorded gap in the form it rejects.
    36	- **Single source of truth** (`single-source-of-truth`):
    37	  - Every fact, rule, or definition MUST have exactly one authoritative home.
    38	  - Every other appearance MUST be a reference or a generated rendering.
    39	- **Feedback loops have consumers** (`feedback-loops-with-consumers`):
    40	  - Every feedback channel MUST name its consumer and the resulting action.
    41	  - The effectiveness of processes, tools, and prompts MUST be measured.
    42	  - The definitions of processes, tools, and prompts MUST be updated from what is measured.
    43	- **Delivery is verified in the running system** (`delivery-verified`):
    44	  - Work MUST be counted done only when its effect is demonstrated in the running system.
    45	  - Artifacts existing, checks passing, or reviews approving MUST NOT count as done on their own.
    46	- **Load the least context** (`least-context`):
    47	  - An activity MUST load the minimum context necessary to accomplish its task.
    48	  - The activity's process MUST name what loads into context and the source each input comes from.
    49	  - Context from an unapproved source MUST NOT be loaded.
