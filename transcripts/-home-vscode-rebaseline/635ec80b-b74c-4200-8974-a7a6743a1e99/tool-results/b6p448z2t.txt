# Artifact type: brief

## Identity and ancestry

- **Type:** `brief` — the PO role's bounded statement of a problem and
  its scope for a Bounded Context shop: what the shop is asked for,
  which framing it serves, what is in and out, and what the shop must
  know to start. A request, not a specification: it names the problem
  and the outcome, never the how.
- **Produced by:** the [PO role](../roles/lead-po.md), from the PM
  role's framing; checked by the
  [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md) against the
  [brief fitness set](../fitness/brief.fitness.md). **Consumed by:** the
  receiving Bounded Context shop; the PM role at the check.

## Required frontmatter

`type: brief`, `id`, `status` (draft | checked | returned |
pending-definition), `version`, `date`, `reader` (the
receiving shop), `framing` (link to the framing it serves), `owner`,
`created`, `updated`.

## Required sections

1. **What is requested** — the request ancestor's first section: the
   first paragraph states what problem the shop is asked to solve and
   the outcome the framing states; the reader named in `reader` is the
   request ancestor's "from whom".
2. **Scope** — what is in; what is out, each exclusion with its reason;
   for each neighbouring piece of work the framing or this brief names,
   in or out or the rule that decides.
3. **What the shop needs** — every term defined here or in the framing,
   every referenced artifact linked, and every question the framing or
   this brief raises either answered or listed as open.
4. **Interaction types**, where the problem has one — the interaction
   types the behavior must hold on (permitted by the fitness set's
   scenario 2 as a what; required of the acceptance scenarios by their
   own typedef), taken from the core-task list in the experience
   record `core-tasks`.

## Rules

- The brief names no technology, structure, or interface form the shop
  would otherwise choose; naming an interaction type is a what, not a
  form.
- Declined scope carries its reason.

## Commitment (Definition of Done)

A brief is done when it has passed the PO output check against the
brief fitness set and the framing. **Consequence on failure:** it is
returned to the PO role with the criterion named, and no shop receives
it.

## Sources

The [brief fitness set](../fitness/brief.fitness.md), whose scenarios
this typedef inherits section by section; the request typedef as
ancestor; the [decision-brief typedef](decision-brief.md)'s answer-first
opening.

## Derived review checklist

- The first paragraph states problem and outcome. *(§Required sections 1; fitness 5)*
- No how; interaction types named as what. *(§Rules; fitness 2)*
- Every neighbour the framing or brief names is placed. *(§Required sections 2; fitness 3)*
- Terms, links, and open questions complete. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 4)*
- Declined scope reasoned. *(§Rules; fitness 1)*

## Document History

# Artifact type: product-decision-record

## Identity and ancestry

- **Type:** `product-decision-record` — the record of one product-level
  decision and its reasons: what was decided, by which role under which
  right, against which alternatives, with what consequences, and how
  hard it is to reverse. The product-side counterpart of the
  architecture decision record.
- **Produced by:** the [PO role](../roles/lead-po.md), for a decision the
  PM role or the PO role has taken; checked by the
  [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md) against the
  [product-decision-record fitness set](../fitness/product-decision-record.fitness.md).
  **Consumed by:** every role whose later decision the record bounds;
  Bounded Context shops; the PM role at the check.

## Required frontmatter

`type: product-decision-record`, `id`, `status` (draft | checked |
returned | pending-definition | superseded — set by the PO role when a later
record names this one as superseded), `version`, `date`,
`decided-by` (the role), `right` (the decision right exercised, or
`escalation` with the escalation named), `owner`, `created`, `updated`.

## Required sections

1. **Decision** — exactly one decision, as a sentence a reader can act
   on.
2. **Alternatives** — at least one the deciding role could have chosen,
   with the reason it was not.
3. **Consequences** — each: what changes, for whom, and what it costs or
   forecloses.
4. **Reversibility** — how hard the decision is to reverse and, if hard,
   what would trigger revisiting it.

## Rules

- Whether the named role held the right it exercised is the PM role's
  ruling at the check, not the record's claim.
- A superseding record links the one it supersedes.

## Commitment (Definition of Done)

A record is done when it has passed the PO output check against its
fitness set and the framing. **Consequence on failure:** it is returned with the
criterion named and binds nothing.

## Sources

Nygard's architecture decision record (decision, consequences); MADR
(considered alternatives); decider and reversibility as the shop's own
additions, from the roles' decision rights; the
[product-decision-record fitness set](../fitness/product-decision-record.fitness.md).

## Derived review checklist

- One decision, actionable. *(§Required sections 1; fitness 1)*
- A real alternative with its reason. *(§Required sections 2; fitness 2)*
- Decider and right, or the escalation, named. *(§Required frontmatter; fitness 3)*
- Each consequence names what changes, for whom, at what cost. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 4)*
- Reversibility and its trigger stated. *(§Required sections 4; fitness 5)*

## Document History

|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | 2026-08-26 | update | Repairs: ancestry corrected; PO role sole producer; framing added; escalation named when right is escalation; attribution dropped; superseded's setter named; checklist cites this typedef's clauses. |

# Artifact type: acceptance-scenarios

## Identity and ancestry

- **Type:** `acceptance-scenarios` — the set of Gherkin scenarios that
  states, as requirements, what counts as done for one behavior; the
  set the PO role submits and the owning Bounded Context shop executes.
  The scenarios are executable by the shop; the set as a document is
  judged by the [acceptance-scenarios fitness set](../fitness/acceptance-scenarios.fitness.md),
  never executed there.
- **Produced by:** the [PO role](../roles/lead-po.md) with the owning
  shop (scope and wording from the PO; steps and edge cases from the
  shop; context ownership and feasibility from the solutions architect;
  usability and accessibility criteria from the product designer);
  checked by the [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md).
  **Consumed by:** the owning shop's scenario register; the solutions
  architect role for assignment; the
  [interaction conformance check](../processes/interaction-conformance-check.md)
  for the interaction types named.

## Required frontmatter

`type: acceptance-scenarios`, `id`, `status` (draft | checked |
returned | pending-definition | assigned — set by the solutions
architect role on assignment), `version`, `date`,
`behavior` (the behavior's name), `context` (the owning Bounded
Context), `framing` (link), `owner`, `created`, `updated`.

## Required sections

1. **Contributors** — the owning shop named as the source of steps and
   edge cases; where the behavior has an interaction, the product
   designer role's usability acceptance criteria and the accessibility
   criteria.
2. **Interaction types** — those the behavior must hold on, from the
   core-task list in the experience record `core-tasks`, where the
   behavior has an interaction.
3. **Scenarios** — each with a tag carrying the set's `behavior` and
   `context` values and a hash field; Given/When/Then with one action or event
   in the When and an outcome observable in the running system in the
   Then; no step names an implementation detail.
4. **Edges** — each failure or boundary case the framing or the shop's
   contribution names, as a scenario above or marked out of scope with
   a reason.

## Rules

- The hash is of the scenario's text; a changed text is a new
  scenario. Whether the hash matches is a mechanical check, to be filed
  as a lint.
- A scenario written without the owning shop is not co-produced and
  fails the check.

## Commitment (Definition of Done)

A set is done when it has passed the PO output check against its
fitness set and the framing. **Consequence on failure:** it is returned
with the criterion named and no scenario enters a register.

## Sources

Gherkin (Given/When/Then, tags); Cucumber's guidance on the three
amigos (co-production); the experience principles `core-task-parity`
and `accessible-by-standard`; the
[acceptance-scenarios fitness set](../fitness/acceptance-scenarios.fitness.md).

## Derived review checklist

- Each scenario one observable behavior, no how. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 1)*
- Owning shop and both designer criteria present. *(§Required sections 1; fitness 2)*
- Tag and hash present on every scenario. *(§Required sections 3; §Rules; fitness 3)*
- Named edges covered or excluded with reason. *(§Required sections 4; fitness 4)*
- Interaction types named. *(§Required sections 2; fitness 5)*

## Document History

| Version | Date | Kind | Entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-08-26 | update | Authored by owner direction from the approved fitness set; carries the co-production statement and the tag's content the set asked the typedef to carry. |
| 1 | 2026-08-26 | review | Screened: findings — definition wrongly in the ancestry; fitness 2's two criteria collapsed; an undefined consumer; the tag's content in two homes; checklist cited another document. |
| 2 | 2026-08-26 | update | Repairs: ancestry corrected; both criteria named; the conformance check linked; the tag carries the frontmatter values; assigned's setter named; checklist cites this typedef's clauses. |

# Artifact type: backlog-order

## Identity and ancestry

- **Type:** `backlog-order` — the PO role's ordered list of requirements
  within the PM role's framing, as submitted for the PM role's check:
  which requirement the shops take up next, the PO role's exclusive
  decision, with the priority, recommendations, and contexts it was
  ordered against stated in the document itself.
- **Produced by:** the [PO role](../roles/lead-po.md); checked by the
  [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md) against the
  [backlog-order fitness set](../fitness/backlog-order.fitness.md).
  **Consumed by:** Bounded Context shops taking up work; the solutions
  architect role, which raises an omitted recommendation or a wrong
  context through its interface with the PO role; the PM role at the
  check.

## Required frontmatter

`type: backlog-order`, `id`, `status` (draft | checked | returned |
pending-definition | current | superseded — the PO role sets
`current` on the order the shops take up from and `superseded` on the
one it replaces), `version`, `date`, `priority` (link to the PM role's
recorded roadmap priority; provenance — the check judges the priority
stated in §1), `owner`, `created`, `updated`.

## Required sections

1. **Priority followed** — the roadmap priority stated, and a reason for
   each exception to it in the order.
2. **Enabler recommendations received** — each recommendation from the
   solutions architect role, placed with its position reasoned or
   declined with a reason.
3. **The order** — each item: the framing it serves or a declined mark
   with reason; the owning Bounded Context, with a cross-context item
   marked and its escalation to the PM role named; and for the first
   untaken item, ready (with its checked brief or scenario set linked)
   or not ready with what it waits on.

## Rules

- An order that omits a recommendation received or misnames a context
  is the solutions architect role's to raise; the check judges only
  what the order states.
- A superseding order links the one it supersedes.

## Commitment (Definition of Done)

An order is done when it has passed the PO output check against its
fitness set and the framing. **Consequence on failure:** it is
returned with the criterion named and the current order stands.

## Sources

The Scrum Guide's product backlog (ordered, transparent, one owner);
the shop's own role definitions (the PO role's exclusive domain; the
PM role's roadmap priority; the solutions architect's enablers and
decomposition); the
[backlog-order fitness set](../fitness/backlog-order.fitness.md).

## Derived review checklist

- Every item names its framing or is declined with reason. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 1)*
- Priority stated and exceptions reasoned. *(§Required sections 1; fitness 2)*
- Recommendations placed or declined. *(§Required sections 2; fitness 3)*
- Contexts named; cross-context escalations named. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 4)*
- Next item's readiness stated. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 5)*

## Cross-cutting findings

**A. `ancestry: [definition, …]` is wrong for product-decision-record, acceptance-scenarios, backlog-order (confident).**
`definition` is "the generic root for documents that state what good looks like for something else … so a validator that knows only `definition` can check any definition document against the two requirements below." Declaring it means an instance must carry definition's frontmatter — `status (draft | approved | superseded)`, `owner`, `version`, `approved` — and body — an opening before the first heading, "a closing checks section (its heading contains 'check')", Document History last. A decision record, a scenario set, and a backlog order carry none of the checks-section requirement, and each typedef's status set (`draft | checked | returned | pending-definition | …`) contradicts definition's closed set. A validator that knows only `definition` would fail every instance. Required change: drop `definition` from the ancestry of the three (`[product-decision-record]`, `[acceptance-scenarios]`, `[backlog-order]`, after the research-report exemplar's `[research-report]`), or declare a root whose frontmatter and sections these instances actually carry. (If the authors intended "a typedef is a definition": that is already true of the typedef document via `artifact-typedef`'s own ancestry; the `ancestry` field is the instances' lineage — "from the generic root to the type this document defines".)

**B. Derived review checklist entries cite the fitness set, not this typedef's clauses (confident).**
artifact-typedef §Required sections 6: "every entry cites the clause it projects (cite-or-delete)"; the exemplars cite `*(§Rules)*`, `*(§Required sections 4)*`, `*(Commitment)*`. All four checklists cite `*(fitness 1)*` … `*(fitness 5)*` — a clause in another document. The commitment "a validator can check any instance from it alone" fails when the checklist's clauses live outside. Required change: cite the typedef's own section (`§Required sections 2; §Rules`), keeping the fitness scenario number as a secondary note if wanted.

**C. Opening before the first heading (wobbly, inherited).** definition §Required sections 1: "An opening that states what the document defines before the first section heading." All four go `# Artifact type: x` → `## Identity and ancestry` with no prose between. The approved exemplars (artifact-typedef, definition, request, research-report) do the same, so this is a corpus convention, not a v1 defect; recording it so the owner can rule once. No change required from these four unless the owner rules the exemplars wrong too.

**D. Status sets vs po-output-check (confident on the shared core; wobbly on the extras).** All four carry `checked`, `returned`, `pending-definition` plus `draft` — consistent with the process ("`checked` on pass, `returned` on fail, `pending-definition` on definition change"). Each also adds a value nothing read here sets: brief `delivered`; product-decision-record `superseded`; acceptance-scenarios `assigned`; backlog-order `current | superseded`. An author cannot tell from the typedef when to set them; a validator can only check spelling. Required change: for each extra value, name who sets it and on what (e.g. "`assigned` — set by the solutions architect role on assignment to the owning context"), or drop it.

**E. Undefined terms arriving cold.** "core-task list" (brief §4, acceptance-scenarios §2) — the glossary defines *interaction type* and the principle is `core-task-parity`; no "core-task list" exists in anything read. "interaction conformance check" (acceptance-scenarios, Consumed by) — no such process or check is named anywhere read. "decision-brief form" (brief Sources; also in the fitness set) — unlinked; the glossary only mentions `decision-brief` as an example type name. Required change: link or define each, or cut.

Banned words: no occurrence of "seat", "surface", "seam", "load-bearing" in the four files (checked).

## basis/artifacts/brief.md — findings

1. **Request ancestry not carried into the section list (confident).** Brief declares `ancestry: [request, brief]`. request §Required sections: "1. **What is requested** — named early, not implied. 2. **From whom** — the reader, named." artifact-typedef §Rules: "A **section** is a markdown heading. Required sections appear as headings in the listed order." Brief's sections are "The problem and the outcome", "Scope", "What the shop needs", "Interaction types" — neither ancestor heading appears, and the reader is carried only in frontmatter (`reader (the receiving shop)`). A validator that knows only `request` fails every brief. Frontmatter does carry through: `type`, `status`, `date`, `reader` are all present. Required change: either state in §Required sections that §1 is the `request` "What is requested" section and add a heading (or a stated equivalence) for "From whom", or amend the request typedef so its two items are content requirements rather than headings. Note the request typedef itself also puts Commitment before Sources, against artifact-typedef's order — not under screen, but the owner should know the ancestor is out of form.

2. **Fitness 3 narrowed (confident).** Scenario 3: "the pieces of work the framing **or the brief itself** names as neighbouring". Typedef §2: "for each neighbouring piece of work the framing names, in or out or the rule that decides" — drops neighbours the brief itself names; checklist likewise: "Every neighbour the framing names is placed." Required change: "the framing or this brief names".

3. **Added requirement not grounded (confident).** §4 "**Interaction types**, where the problem has one — the interaction types the behavior must hold on, from the core-task list." No brief scenario asks for it (scenario 2 only permits: "naming an interaction type … is a what, not a form"); the interaction-types requirement lives in the acceptance-scenarios fitness set (scenario 5) and lead-po carries nothing on it. Required change: cut §4, or ground it (a brief fitness scenario, or a role clause) and define "core-task list".

4. **Consumer not grounded (wobbly).** "Consumed by: … the solutions architect role for assignment". lead-po's architect interface sends "PM-checked scenarios out, for assignment" — scenarios, not briefs. Quote the whole: "**Consumed by:** the receiving Bounded Context shop; the solutions architect role for assignment; the PM role at the check." Required change: drop the architect, or say what the architect does with a brief.

5. Frontmatter closed set: stated as a list with no "open" marker — closed per artifact-typedef, fine. `framing (link to the framing it serves)` grounded in lead-po ("each traceable to the framing it serves"). `delivered` — see D.

## basis/artifacts/product-decision-record.md — findings

1. Ancestry — cross-cutting A.

2. **Producer clause not grounded and conflicts with the check (confident).** "**Produced by:** the PO role … for a decision the PM role or the PO role has taken, or by the PM role's assisting agent when the decision is the PM's own; checked by the PO output check". lead-po: the PO role authors "product decision records"; lead-pm names no record-writing by its assisting agent, and the po-output-check's maker is lead-po only ("maker — lead-po (submits and revises; never decides its own pass)"). Required change: keep the PO role as sole producer, or ground the PM-agent path in lead-pm and say which check it goes through.

3. **Commitment omits the framing (confident).** "A record is done when it has passed the PO output check against its fitness set." Frontmatter: "`right` (the decision right exercised, or `escalation`)" — the literal word `escalation` names that an escalation happened, not "the escalation that settled it" (to whom, settled how). Checklist: "Decider and right named in frontmatter." Required change: require the escalation itself named (a field or a sentence in §1) when `right: escalation`.
