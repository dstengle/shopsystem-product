    14	# Artifact type: initiative
    15	
    16	## Identity and ancestry
    17	
    18	- **Type:** `initiative` — the product-level problem artifact: one
    19	  problem worth solving, stated for the product, not for any Bounded
    20	  Context. It carries the outcome the problem serves and how that
    21	  outcome is measured, the appetite the product will spend, what it
    22	  will not do, and — once attached — the feasibility verdict and the
    23	  decomposition. It is the home of the framing: the PM role's recorded
    24	  statement of what a request is about is its first section and lives
    25	  nowhere else. Features are made from it and link it. It is bounded
    26	  to 500 words by rule, so its check reads the whole of it and there
    27	  is no second document to keep in step. `ancestry` names no generic root: an initiative is
    28	  neither a request nor a definition, as the feature typedef also
    29	  states.
    30	- **Produced by:** the [PM role](../roles/lead-pm.md), in the
    31	  [discovery conversation](../processes/discovery-conversation.md) —
    32	  the role's assisting agent drafts, the PM decides its content.
    33	- **Attached and screened in** the
    34	  [initiative-check](../processes/initiative-check.md) process: it
    35	  mirrors the PO output check with maker
    36	  `lead-pm`; before the screen, two attach steps: the solutions
    37	  architect role attaches the decomposition and its feasibility verdict,
    38	  then the product designer role attaches its usability evidence or
    39	  hypothesis; then the cold reviewer's screen against the
    40	  [initiative fitness set](../fitness/initiative.fitness.md); then the
    41	  authority's decision. That screen is the *check of record*:
    42	  the check whose verdict the definitions rely on, since the authority
    43	  holds the PM role in person and no other role checks the PM's
    44	  framing. What it checks is completeness and form — originator
    45	  quoted, one measure, a bound, no solution, attachments present. It
    46	  does not check whether the problem is worth solving; that value
    47	  judgment stands unchecked by design and rests on the recorded
    48	  originator's words and the measure. The authority *bets* on a
    49	  screened initiative — decides to spend the appetite — in that
    50	  process's own decide step, a human step.
    51	- **Consumed by:** the PO role, which makes features from it; the
    52	  solutions architect role; the PO output check, where a feature's
    53	  `framing` input names this document's first section.
    54	
    55	## Required frontmatter
    56	
    57	`type: initiative`, `id`, `status`, `version`, `name`, `owner`
    58	(`lead-pm`), `created`, `updated`. `approved` does not apply; the
    59	lifecycle stands in. Status values and their writers:
    60	- `proposed` — the PM role, when the discovery conversation's `frame`
    61	  step records it: the framed-but-unbet state.
    62	- `planned` — the authority's bet, taken and recorded in the
    63	  [initiative-check](../processes/initiative-check.md) process's
    64	  decide and record steps, replacing `proposed`.
    65	- `active` — the PO output check's record step, when the first feature
    66	  made from it passes, replacing `planned` — the only status it writes
    67	  over; the step reaches this document through its declared framing
    68	  input, since a feature's framing names this document's first
    69	  section.
    70	- `completed` — the [reconcile-and-close](../processes/reconcile-and-close.md)
    71	  process, when the last delivery under it is reconciled — read from
    72	  §6 and each feature's state — and the measure is recorded against
    73	  its target (amendment pending).
    74	- `cancelled` — with the reason recorded, from `proposed`, `planned`,
    75	  or `active`: a request declined at discovery is recorded `proposed`
    76	  and cancelled in the same discovery conversation (its `frame` step),
    77	  so the record of what was declined survives; a cancellation at the
    78	  bet is taken in the initiative-check decide step and written by its
    79	  record step; a later cancellation is
    80	  the PM role's decision recorded as an outcome of the
    81	  [review conversation](../processes/review-conversation.md)
    82	  (amendment pending).

    84	## Required sections
    85	
    86	1. **Framing** — the originator (who expressed the intent, quoted in
    87	   their own words), the problem taken to be worth solving, the outcome
    88	   it serves, and the contract — product or operational — it entered
    89	   through. The PM role's exclusive decision.
    90	2. **For whom** — who has the problem; one measure with its current
    91	   condition, quantified, and its target; and the interaction types the
    92	   outcome must hold on, from the
    93	   [core-task list](../experience/core-tasks.md), or "none" with the
    94	   reason.
    95	3. **Appetite** — the bound the product will spend — time or capacity
    96	   — that the features stay within; and the no-gos: what this
    97	   initiative will not do, each with its reason.
    98	4. **Feasibility and usability** — the solutions architect role's
    99	   feasibility verdict with its reasons, present; and, where §2 names
   100	   an interaction type, the product designer role's usability evidence
   101	   or the hypothesis it stands on, or "not yet" with the text of the
   102	   ask that requests it.
   103	5. **Decomposition** — attached by the solutions architect role: the
   104	   Bounded Contexts the initiative touches, the relationship kind of
   105	   each contract between them it relies on, and the cross-context flow
   106	   in one place — the saga or process manager (the one component that
   107	   routes the flow between contexts) that will carry it, or "none" when
   108	   the contexts need no flow between them. "Not yet" until attached.
   109	6. **Features** — the features made from it, by id, as they are made
   110	   — the feature-authoring draft step adds each; empty until the
   111	   first.

   113	## Rules
   114	
   115	- The framing is written here and nowhere else; a check that names the
   116	  framing as a criterion reads §1.
   117	- Sections 1–3 name no technology, structure, or interface form; an
   118	  interaction type named in §2 is a what.
   119	- At most 500 words outside the Document History; the bet — what is
   120	  spent on what, for which outcome — must be statable from §1–3 alone.
   121	- The go/no-go and a cancellation are each carried by a
   122	  [product decision record](product-decision-record.md) the PO role
   123	  makes for the decision and the PO output check screens, linked from
   124	  the Document History state entry; for the go/no-go, `decided-by` is
   125	  `product-authority` and `right` is `bet`, values the decision-record
   126	  typedef admits.
   127	- The count of initiatives whose §5 names more than one Bounded
   128	  Context, per quarter, is a report the solutions architect role
   129	  reads through its interface with the PO role; a rising count is its
   130	  signal to review the decomposition, recorded as an architecture
   131	  decision record.

   133	## Commitment (Definition of Done)
   134	
   135	An initiative is done — able to be bet on — when it has passed the
   136	cold reviewer's screen against its fitness set, which requires the
   137	feasibility verdict present and the measure with a current condition
   138	and a target. **Consequence on failure:** it stays `proposed` with the
   139	criterion named; no feature is made from it.

    14	# Artifact type: feature
    15	
    16	## Identity and ancestry
    17	
    18	- **Type:** `feature` — a Gherkin Feature: one capability described
    19	  from the user's or agent's point of view, with the scenarios that
    20	  state, as requirements, what counts as done for it. A product-level
    21	  artifact: it belongs to the [initiative](initiative.md) it is made
    22	  from and may hold scenarios owned by
    23	  several Bounded Contexts. The scenario is the atom; the feature is
    24	  its grouping; the feature repository — the lead shop's directory of
    25	  feature artifacts — is the record of what is specified, and a
    26	  context's assigned set is the subset of scenarios, across all
    27	  features, tagged to it. The instance is a
    28	  markdown document with frontmatter whose Scenarios section holds one
    29	  fenced Gherkin block — the executable unit the shops receive; the
    30	  other sections are the document's, not Gherkin's. Scenarios are
    31	  executed by the owning shops; the feature as a document is judged by
    32	  the [feature fitness set](../fitness/feature.fitness.md), never
    33	  executed there. `ancestry` names no generic root: a feature is
    34	  neither a request nor a definition.
    35	- **Produced by:** the [PO role](../roles/lead-po.md), as sole author —
    36	  the scenarios' steps included; the product designer role contributes
    37	  usability and accessibility criteria, the solutions architect role
    38	  its non-functional constraints where the decomposition names them.
    39	  The owning shops do not co-author: conflicts with behavior already
    40	  specified are caught by the repository sweep at assignment, and a
    41	  shop's objection travels as a clarify or a returned dispatch after
    42	  it receives its scenarios. Checked by the
    43	  [PO output check](../processes/po-output-check.md); its scenarios
    44	  assigned to Bounded Contexts by the solutions architect role in the
    45	  [scenario assignment](../processes/scenario-assignment.md) process.
    46	  **Consumed by:** the shops, each receiving the scenarios tagged to it;
    47	  the [interaction conformance check](../processes/interaction-conformance-check.md)
    48	  for the interaction types named; the changelog rendering the same report proposes, when the
    49	  [reconcile-and-close](../processes/reconcile-and-close.md) process
    50	  closes the work that delivered its scenarios.

    58	`assigned`. Status values and their writers: `draft` (the PO role);
    59	`checked`, `returned`, `pending-definition` (the PO output check's
    60	record step, replacing `draft`); a returned or pending feature the PO
    61	role resubmits goes back to `draft`; `assigned` (the scenario
    62	assignment process's record step, replacing `checked` when every
    63	scenario carries its tag); a feature the assignment process returns
    64	goes to `returned`.
    65	
    66	## Required sections
    67	
    68	1. **Feature** — the Gherkin `Feature:` line with its name, and the
    69	   narrative beneath it: who the capability is for, what they can do,
    70	   and the outcome it serves, in the framing's words.
    71	2. **Contributors** — for every scenario, the owning shop named — the
    72	   shop the solutions architect's decomposition places the behavior in
    73	   (ownership for assignment, not authorship); where the capability has
    74	   an interaction, the product designer role's usability acceptance
    75	   criteria and the accessibility criteria; where the decomposition
    76	   names them, the solutions architect role's non-functional
    77	   constraints, riding as criteria on the scenarios they bound.
    78	3. **Interaction types** — always present: the interaction types the
    79	   capability must be available on, from the
    80	   [core-task list](../experience/core-tasks.md), or "none" with the
    81	   reason when the capability has no interaction.
    82	4. **Scenarios** — one fenced `gherkin` block, the executable unit:
    83	   it opens with the `Feature:` line and narrative of §1 verbatim (§1
    84	   is the document's reading of the block's head; the block is the
    85	   source, and the repeat is accepted so the document reads without
    86	   the block), then an optional `Background:` (its Given steps judged as any
    87	   step), then each `Scenario:` carrying the tags `@feature:<id>` (so a
    88	   scenario travels alone to a shop and still names its feature —
    89	   the reason the Feature line's tag inheritance is not relied on),
    90	   `@hash:<sha>` (a hash of the scenario's text), and, once assigned,
    91	   `@bounded-context:<name>`; Given/When/Then with one action or event
    92	   in the When and an outcome observable in the running system in the
    93	   Then; no step names an implementation detail.
    94	5. **Edges** — a table of every failure and boundary case the framing
    95	   or a contributor's criteria name: case · who named it · the
    96	   `Scenario:` name that covers it, or "out of scope" with the reason.
    97	
    98	## Rules
    99	
   100	- A scenario's `@bounded-context:` tag is written by the solutions
   101	  architect role at assignment, never by the PO role — a writer rule
   102	  the assignment process enforces; the check does not judge the tag's
   103	  presence, since a re-verified or resubmitted feature may carry it. In
   104	  the assignment process, a scenario whose owning context differs from
   105	  its Contributors' shop is treated as unowned with that reason, so the
   106	  feature returns (`returned`) for the PO role to correct the owning
   107	  shop it names.
   108	- A changed scenario text is a new scenario with a new `@hash:`.
   109	  Whether the hash matches the text is a mechanical check, to be filed
   110	  as a lint.
   111	- A scenario's conflicts with behavior already specified are caught
   112	  by the sweep of the feature repository at assignment, never by
   113	  asking the shop during authoring; the shop's voice after dispatch
   114	  is the clarify and the return.
   115	
   116	## Commitment (Definition of Done)
   117	
   118	A feature is done when it has passed the PO output check against its
   119	fitness set and the framing, and every scenario carries a
   120	`@bounded-context:` tag. **Consequence on failure:** it is returned
   121	with the criterion named and no scenario is dispatched.
   122	
   123	## Sources
   124	
   125	Gherkin (Feature, narrative, Background, Scenario, tags — the block's
   126	form); the
   127	experience principles `core-task-parity` and `accessible-by-standard`.
   128

   130	
   131	- Feature line and narrative present, tied to the framing. *(§Required sections 1; fitness 6)*
   132	- Each scenario one observable behavior, no how. *(§Required sections 4; fitness 1)*
   133	- Every scenario has a named owning shop; designer criteria where there is an interaction; architect constraints where named. *(§Required sections 2; fitness 2)*
   134	- `@feature:` and `@hash:` on every scenario. *(§Required sections 4; fitness 3)*
   135	- Every listed edge covered or excluded with reason. *(§Required sections 5; fitness 4)*
   136	- Interaction types section present, named or "none" with reason. *(§Required sections 3; fitness 5)*

===== primer =====
     1	# Rebaseline migration branch — session primer
     2	
     3	The product is the **shopsystem**: the system by which a lead shop
     4	and its Bounded Context shops — agent-run execution units — take a
     5	problem from discovery to verified, running software; it is built by
     6	the same kind of shops it defines, and this repository is its lead
     7	shop. For whom: the people who stand up and run products on it — a
     8	product authority who frames and bets, and the builders and operators
     9	who meet the work through its interaction types. The shop's operating
    10	process is
    11	[product-flow](../../basis/processes/product-flow.md): discovery →
    12	initiative check and bet → backlog ordering → feature authoring →
    13	scenario assignment, each stage a defined sub-process with its own
    14	check.
    15	
    16	This is the `rebaseline` branch: the greenfield tree of the
    17	shopsystem-product migration, seeded 2026-08-22 under the approved
    18	migration plan. Nothing exists here except through an explicit import
    19	step; nothing from the old corpus is read here except through the
    20	curated feed.
    21	
    22	## Governing instruments
    23	
    24	- `basis/` on this branch — the definition corpus. Every activity here
    25	  operates through these definitions; the index is
    26	  [`basis/README.md`](../../basis/README.md).
    27	- The migration plan: `main:drafts/migration-plan.md` (read via
    28	  `git show`; reference only) — phases, entry conditions, action table.
    29	- The frozen corpus: `main` — the BC shops' contract of record until
    30	  cut-over, and the pre-migration reference. Never copy from it outside
    31	  an import step or the curated feed. The branch pulls from `main`;
    32	  `main` never publishes to the branch.
