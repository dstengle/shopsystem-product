## Context

pdr-035 establishes the two-views requirement: the accepted set is the
current-system view (self-contained, mutually consistent) and the supersede
chains are the transformation view. That requirement is only real if a reader can
*obtain* each view mechanically. Today the corpus has no read tooling — a reader
gets the current view only by hand-reading `status` and manually dropping
superseded and changelog material, which is the error-prone practice the
requirement exists to eliminate. The restructuring draft carried this as three
slices — navigation (#5), rendering with section filtering (#6), and query by
frontmatter (#7) — and noted they share one CLI.

## Options considered

- **No dedicated tooling** — rely on the gate plus manual frontmatter reading.
  Rejected: the two views stay theoretical; self-containment cannot be relied on
  if obtaining the current view is a manual, error-prone step.
- **Three separate tools** (one per capability). Rejected: navigation, rendering,
  and query all read the same frontmatter graph through the same loader; three
  tools would fork the graph reader and drift.
- **One read-only CLI with three verbs** over a shared corpus loader (this
  decision). Chosen: single loader, single view-filtering implementation, one
  surface to keep coherent with the schema.

## Decision

The corpus gains **one read-only CLI** over the artifact frontmatter graph, with
three verbs, at capability altitude (this PDR fixes the need and boundaries; field
and flag mechanics are the mechanism ADR's job):

- **Navigate** — from a document, follow the materialized edges and return the
  neighbourhood.
- **Render with view filtering** — emit a document in the current-system view
  (accepted content sections; changelog and transformation material dropped) or
  the transformation view (full), selected on the `status` axis.
- **Query by frontmatter** — select documents by type, status, tag, distribution,
  or edge participation; return a compact list or matching documents rendered with
  the same view filtering.

The CLI is **read-only** — it never maintains or mutates edges (that is the gate's
job under the base-schema ADR). All three verbs share one corpus loader — the
knowledge BC's existing loader, reused, not reimplemented — and support md/json/yaml
output.

**Sequencing.** The *need* rests on slice #1 (this PDR). The *mechanism* (the
CLI's field/edge behavior) rests on slice #2's materialized-edge schema, so the
mechanism ADR is authored after adr-067 and dispatched to the owning BC on
acceptance. **Folding note:** this direction folds the draft's slices #5/#6/#7
into one capability — an autonomous router call under the draft's explicit
open-slicing latitude, subject to the product authority's ratification.

## Consequences

- The two-views requirement becomes obtainable rather than merely declared; the
  current-system view can be produced reliably and consumed by readers and tools.
- One CLI over one loader keeps the read side coherent with the schema and avoids
  a second, drift-prone graph reader.
- The mechanism ADR is gated behind slice #2 (materialized edges) — navigation and
  query over edges are only meaningful once back-edges are materialized. On
  acceptance of that ADR, the build dispatches to the BC that owns the loader/CLI
  (shopsystem-knowledge), not decided here.
- If the product authority re-splits the fold, this PDR re-opens into per-verb
  directions; the shaped candidate (cand-007) records the fold as provisional.
