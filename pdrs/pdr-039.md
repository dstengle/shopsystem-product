## Context

The 2026-08-03 assessment (sess-2026-08-03-b, intent-013) found the quality
standard existed only as unwritten practice: the writing skills carry structure
without doctrine, the validator is permissive by design, and "what a good
artifact is" depended on who authored on which day. The product authority
directed an iterative capture process: review real artifacts, distill the
authority's verdicts into rules, ratify them the next sitting. Iteration 1
(sess-2026-08-04-a) reviewed adr-072, adr-067, and adr-029 under a
three-artifact-per-sitting cap and yielded the rules below. A further finding
from that sitting: the corpus contains no exemplar — the authority adjudicated
the assessment's candidate exemplar (adr-029) as failing the bar — so positive
rules come from verdicts, never from in-corpus models.

## Options considered

- **A — a one-shot standard document.** Rejected: it contradicts the
  authority's explicit direction, repeats the batch-authoring pattern (a
  standard written before evidence), and leaves no ratification trail per rule.
- **B — the standard expressed only as gate checks.** Rejected: machines
  cannot check thought quality, so the checkable subset would masquerade as
  the whole bar — the same verification-by-proxy failure the assessment ranked
  as root cause 1.
- **C — a governed decision record grown by changelog per ratified iteration
  (chosen).** Rules stay one-line with their rationale in the iteration's
  session record; each rule carries a machine-checkable or carrier-only tag
  routing it to Phase-2 enforcement or the Phase-3 writing skills; until those
  phases land, application is manual. Honest cost: authority attention on
  every authored artifact for the duration of the arc, and the record itself
  must not become the aggregate its own R7 forbids.

## Decision

The following rules bind every artifact authored or accepted in this shop.
Tags: **[M]** machine-checkable (Phase-2 enforcement queue), **[C]**
carrier-only (Phase-3 writing-skill queue).

- **R1** — A decision record must decide something that is not already the
  default. Keep-the-default answers and deferrals are not decisions: they are
  recorded where the question was asked, or as work items. **[C]**
- **R2** — Every numbered decision item passes triage: real decision → its own
  record; tightening → an amendment or note on the decision it tightens;
  implementation detail → scenario, brief, or bead. Multiple decision items in
  one record triggers the test. **[C; item-count flaggable [M]]**
- **R3** — Record length is proportional to decision weight; a simple ruling
  gets a short record. **[C; length signal [M]]**
- **R4** — Every proposal reaches a terminal state: accepted, superseded, or
  rejected. Rejection is a first-class recorded outcome. **[M: proposed-staleness]**
- **R5** — The title names the one decision taken; the description is the
  decision as an abstract. Neither enumerates parts nor recites prior
  records. **[C; enumeration/reference-density flaggable [M]]**
- **R6** — Active decisions stand on active ground: no reliance on superseded
  decisions in body or description (lineage lives in frontmatter edges), and
  no grounding through findings or other non-authoritative chains. **[M:
  superseded-id references in active prose]**
- **R7** — Supersession must simplify: a record replacing N decisions
  collapses them into a smaller, clearer active set, never a larger
  aggregate. **[C; soft size signal [M]]**
- **R8** — One plane per record: schema (fields), semantics (relationships
  between documents), and process (gating and tools) are decided
  separately. **[C]**
- **R9** — No ephemeral content in the permanent record: acceptance mechanics
  and status scaffolding live in session records, beads, or the changelog, and
  are scrubbed at status flip. **[M: scaffolding phrases in accepted records]**
- **R10** — The acceptance gate: no flip to accepted with open forks, a failed
  granularity check, or ephemeral scaffolding present; a flip requires
  resolved forks with recorded outcomes, a changelog entry, and realization
  evidence or named realization work. Granularity failure alone stops
  acceptance. **[M: fork sections, changelog presence, scaffolding; realization
  evidence [C]]**
- **R11** — A recorded structural directive from the product authority binds
  authoring; deviation requires the authority's explicit sign-off in the
  artifact. **[C]**

## Consequences

- Every artifact authored during the re-founding arc is reviewed against this
  record in the same sitting, within the three-artifact cap; acceptance
  anywhere in the corpus now requires R10.
- The record grows only through ratified iterations; an iteration that adds no
  rule is the loop's exit signal, after which this record moves to acceptance.
- Machine-tagged rules are queued, not enforced, until Phase 2 lands them as
  checks demonstrated red on planted defects; until then a green check proves
  nothing about this doctrine.
- Application of carrier-only rules is judgment and can drift; the mitigation
  is the loop itself — each iteration re-applies the current rules to fresh
  artifacts before adding new ones.
- The division of decision kinds and roles (ADR vs PDR/brief; PO vs Architect)
  is under investigation as a possible cause of records taking on the wrong
  content; rules may be re-homed per kind when that lands.
- First applications: adr-072 rejected under R1/R2; adr-067 annotated as
  accepted-in-violation-of-R10 with its forks transferred to the Phase-1
  restructure.
