## Context

The corpus is typed, plural, and gate-green — `cand-005` closed the
knowledge/schema precondition chain — but the *founding requirement* of the
artifact system has no authoritative home. Why the product keeps artifacts at
all, what the eight kinds are for, how they compose, and the two-views /
self-containment rule live split across PDR-031 (the BC founding, discovery-first
decision) and PDR-032 (which bounded context owns the type system and taxonomy),
and are otherwise captured only in the non-authoritative
`drafts/artifact-system-restructuring.md`. `current-state.md` does not narrate
the artifact system at all.

With no anchor for the founding requirement, authoring keeps jumping the
lifecycle: freehand decision docs with no provenance, the withdrawn
ADR-067/PDR-035-draft (2026-07-20), and the work-tracking drift where a bead
over-reports open work because nothing states the coherence requirement the
system is meant to hold to. This PDR is slice #1 of the artifact-system
restructuring (`lead-uhxoc`): the anchor the other six slices supersede into or
reference. It states the requirement; it deliberately does **not** specify the
schema that serves it — that is slice #2.

## Options considered

- **Leave the founding requirement scattered** across PDR-031, PDR-032, and the
  draft (status quo). Rejected: no single accepted doc states the requirement, so
  the current-system view has no anchor and authoring keeps improvising — the
  root cause this initiative exists to remove.
- **Fold the needs into the schema decision** (author the requirement and the
  field/edge mechanics together in one document). Rejected: it blurs the
  needs/schema line the whole restructuring is trying to establish, and couples a
  slow-changing requirement to a faster-changing mechanism.
- **A standalone foundational needs statement** (this PDR) that states the
  requirement at capability altitude and hands the mechanics to slice #2.
  Chosen: it gives the corpus one authoritative anchor, keeps the requirement
  legible independent of its encoding, and lets the schema evolve under a stable
  statement of what it must satisfy.

## Decision

The artifact system rests on a foundational needs statement with four parts,
stated at capability altitude (no field names, no edge mechanics, no CLI):

1. **Why the product keeps artifacts.** Decisions, intent, and shape are durable
   product facts that must be reasoned about *as a set* — the current system —
   rather than reconstructed from history each time. Artifacts exist so that the
   present state of the product's thinking is legible without replaying how it
   got there.

2. **The eight kinds and how they compose.** The system recognizes eight artifact
   kinds. They compose along a **provenance spine** — a scenario is defined by a
   PDR, which derives from a candidate, which derives from an intent, which is
   produced in a session — so every artifact traces back to a stakeholder intent
   and the session that raised it. Alongside the spine, kinds **reference** and
   **derive from** one another (a decision builds on prior decisions; a
   current-state view incorporates the decisions it reflects). This part names the
   kinds and these composition relationships as *concepts* — what each kind is for
   and which kinds it hangs from — and nothing about how the relationships are
   stored.

3. **The two-views / self-containment requirement.** The corpus supports two
   views over one body of artifacts. The **current-system view** is the set of
   `accepted` documents read together; it must be *self-contained* — no accepted
   document reaches into a superseded one to be understood — and *mutually
   consistent* — no two accepted documents contradict. The **transformation
   view** is the full graph including superseded documents and the supersede
   chains: how the system got to its present state. `status` is the axis that
   separates the two — filter to `accepted` and what remains must stand on its
   own. Coherence is therefore the **founding requirement the schema serves**, not
   a technical choice that happens to be made downstream.

4. **The changelog exception.** Self-containment binds the *content* sections of a
   document. The **changelog** is the one sanctioned place an accepted document
   may name its superseded predecessor (the "carried forward from X" line); it
   belongs to the transformation view and is filtered out of the current-view
   render.

**Supersession (materialized at acceptance).** On acceptance this PDR supersedes
**PDR-031** in full and the *founding/needs portion* of **PDR-032** (PDR-032 is
jointly superseded across slices #1, #2, and #3, which carry its founding, base
fields, and per-type taxonomy respectively). Per the corpus's current practice
for a proposed successor (cf. PDR-034), the materialized `supersedes` frontmatter
edges and the `current-state.md` `incorporates` update are added at acceptance
time, not while this PDR is `proposed`.

## Consequences

- The corpus gains one authoritative anchor for its founding requirement; slice
  #2 (the base schema ADR) encodes the requirement stated here, and slices #3–#7
  supersede into or reference it.
- The needs/schema boundary the restructuring depends on is established by
  example: this document states *what must hold* and names no mechanism, so the
  schema can evolve under a stable requirement.
- On acceptance, the current-system view becomes self-consistent (PDR-031 and
  PDR-032's founding portion leave the accepted set as they are superseded), and
  `current-state.md` must narrate the artifact system and add `pdr-035` to
  `incorporates` per the acceptance gate.
- Two things are deliberately left open for their own slices: the schema
  mechanics — materialized edge pairs, tags, distribution, external references —
  are slice #2; and the `current-state` versioned-vs-singleton typedef conflict
  is slice #3. Neither is decided here.
