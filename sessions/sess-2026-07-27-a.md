## Outcome

**Mode: discovery.** The product authority raised a directional, solution-shaped
ask and directed the lead-pm to engage PM discovery rather than accept the stated
solution. The dialogue opened wide on the problem, not the proposed tools.

**Reframe.** The stated solution ("knowledge tools as the basis, eliminate grep,
progressive disclosure, current-only defaults, more skills") is a *means*. The
problem underneath, surfaced and confirmed by the authority: he is a **mandatory
safety net** — he cannot delegate operation of the shop and step away, because
the agents cannot reliably or affordably build, nor visibly show, an accurate
picture of the system's decided state; so work comes out wrong or inconsistent and
he must catch it, and agents cannot even locate the relevant decisions without his
context cues. The confirmed optimization target is **verifiable grounding**: trust
earned by *inspecting* that a decision stood on the true current state, not by a
clean error record — with a residual correction becoming a diagnosable signal
(grounding gap vs. downstream cause) rather than a reason to supervise.

**Key move that shaped the intent.** Two of the authority's corrections this
session were not retrieval failures (the agent had access) — they were the agent
proceeding confidently on a partial/wrong basis without verifying. So "trust"
joins two mechanisms: retrieval/cost *and* discipline/self-awareness — and tools
an agent merely *may* use prove nothing; grounding must be routed through them by
doctrine and *shown*. This matched the empirical finding that the read-tools
already exist but nothing routes through them.

**Grounding.** Grounded in `current-state.md` (the artifact system now live) and
the solution-space research captured this session in
`drafts/knowledge-tools-and-skills-analysis.md` (tool-surface + process + skills
gap analysis), which becomes evidence/hypotheses for shaping — not the problem.
