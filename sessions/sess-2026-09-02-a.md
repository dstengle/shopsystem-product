## Outcome

**Mode: discovery** (lead-pm as interlocutor; work item `lead-50ecj`).
Opened in interview form — the migration plan's R31 names this the Phase 2
opening interview — and shifted to brainstorm at the authority's direction.
**Closed without convergence at the authority's direction: no initiative is
framed.** The authority's parking rationale, verbatim: "We do not actually
have a fully fleshed out decision system, so this initiative is actually
premature at this point." Restart is bead `lead-jwsl1`; this record is the
resume anchor.

### Positions reached in dialogue (authority's words quoted)

- **Purpose.** "The purpose of progressive disclosure is efficient knowledge
  management AND use." Two distinct purposes underneath: (1) decision-time
  context fitness — "relevance and completeness of the context during
  decision making"; "Completeness AND relevance are important to get least
  context right"; (2) consistency with the record — "avoiding conflicting
  with earlier decisions and general product information." Bloat's three
  harms: "it can crowd out relevant information and it is expensive and
  slow." The terminology itself may need adjusting — "progressive
  disclosure" names a mechanism, not these purposes.
- **Levels are the traversal medium, not the deliverable.** Depth is
  "somewhat a proxy," but "there needs to be some mechanism to find the
  extent of the graph of needed information without loading all of it
  directly and progressive disclosure (multiple levels of information on a
  topic) is a useful method for being able to do breadth first search."
- **Levels are authored, not generated.** "Summarization levels should be
  authored when the artifact is created to achieve maximum fidelity."
  Accepted consequence: amending processes acquire a re-touch obligation —
  "there is no choice but to update the summaries to be accurate if we want
  a progressive disclosure system to provide any long-term value." Decisions
  themselves freeze once accepted and are superseded by new decisions, so
  the obligation falls mainly on living artifacts (principles, processes).
  Level-conformance checking connects to lead-iixm's scenario sets.
- **Edges enter several ways.** The product-definition chain is followable;
  "authoring requires referencing relevant material"; "the glossary can be
  used as part of the graph"; "tags should be added to documents to group
  and categorize them" — and "tags are vocabulary and all vocabulary is
  explicitly managed." "Within reason there should be backwards and forwards
  links in the artifacts for simple relationships and the rest can be
  populated when read using purpose-built tools."
- **The contract is the product; storage is implementation.** "This is
  purely about an interface contract. The decision to build the graph on the
  fly, maintain a database or a cache is all about implementation and
  independent of the contract. What matters is that when an artifact is
  loaded, all of the relevant links to other artifacts are available."
- **Retrieval check is a sampling audit, not exhaustive.** "Likely not
  possible to make it truly exhaustive without incurring a lot of the
  expense we are trying to avoid in the first place." Shape: inspect
  indexes plus positive and negative spot checks — the check "can do some of
  its own light research and make sure the documents it finds are in the
  retrieved set and for a few documents in the retrieved set it can rate the
  relevance." A downstream check on the decision itself is the second net.
- **For whom and the measure.** "It is the lead shop that feels this. The
  measure of success is tokens required for lead shop decision activities
  over time and having that growth be limited to at least less than linear
  and at best settling on a fixed upper bound for any size system." The
  lead-pm challenge stands recorded, unresolved: the token measure needs a
  welded-on counterweight (a held retrieval-check pass rate) or it is
  satisfiable by loading less and deciding worse.

### Appetite items — ruled too broad for one initiative, kept as fuel

The authority ruled the assembled scope "far too broad. My laundry list of
details is not appropriate. We should be defining the problem and what a
good initiative outcome would be, and this doesn't need to be the only
progressive disclosure or knowledge-related initiative." The list, kept for
sibling initiatives: behavioral contract defined with a simple initial
file-based implementation; queries across artifacts; graph navigation;
progressive loading for graph navigation; artifact loading with section
filtering; efficient context-package creation from discovered sub-graphs
(deciding agents don't need front-matter and boilerplate); no embeddings at
the start. Retrofit extent (all existing artifacts vs. proof-first
sub-graph) was posed and not decided.

### Scope-cut options on the table at close

Three cut axes were proposed (lead-pm), none ratified: cut by purpose
(consistency-with-record first, as the sharper silent-failure problem); cut
by consumer (one decision activity as vehicle — initiative-check's screen
recommended); cut by outcome type (a validated-or-refuted contract
hypothesis, explicitly not a fully working system). Candidate problem
statement recorded for the restart: "A lead-shop decision activity cannot
cheaply establish what the record already says that bears on its decision;
missing it is silent and produces conflicting decisions." The authority
had directed: scope must be "a straightforward statement," and "if the
outcome of the initiative is not a fully working system, that is fine, but
we need to state that up front."
