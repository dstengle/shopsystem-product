## Outcome

Four executions, each on its own anchor: two of
principle-set-authoring (working scope, lead-qbvap; architecture
scope, lead-q5lkv), one of feature-authoring (lead-k8ruw), one of
adr-authoring (lead-ft3ad).

### The removal is in force

`delivery-verified` is gone from the working set. The authority's
verdict at `authority-approve`, 2026-09-16: **tradeoffs-accepted**.
`basis/principles.md` v12 carries the removal — both statements, the
rationale, the implications, and the principle's column struck from
the fitness screen with every row re-counted to nine.
`basis/architecture-principles.md` v7 reworded the one sentence in
`bidirectional-conformance`'s rationale that cited it as the
forward-direction flank; no statement moved.
`.claude/shop/principles.md` was recompiled and renders nine
principles. Until that recompile, every session was still being told
the opposite of the decision.

**Both screens returned findings, and none of them concerned the
change.** The cold judges scored each whole set against the
principle-set fitness set and found defects that predate this
amendment: in the working set, three statement bullets carrying two
obligations each and three implications asserting obligations their
statements never make; in the architecture set, decision-turning terms
undefined where a reader can reach them, an internal forward reference
on "the design", and the same bullet-fusion pattern. In both, the two
judged screen rows claim passes the judge could not reproduce.

The authority declined the repair — "don't repair those, they're out
of scope for this work" — so none was made. Each finding is marked in
its set's text as an accepted tradeoff with its reason, under a new
`### Accepted tradeoffs` heading below the fitness screen, and filed
for its own decision (lead-zddhu, lead-fstd0). The notes say plainly
what the screen rows overclaim rather than leaving the contradiction
implicit: the rows state the author's claim, the note states what an
independent read of the same text found, and the difference is named
as open work.

### The implementation bet's first feature

[feat-implementation-good](../features/feat-implementation-good.md) —
one stated definition of good for implementation engineering and the
two roles that carry it, seven scenarios, session one's content only;
the process, the lead shop's permission, and the proof execution are
excluded in Edges as the second session's. `checked`;
`init-implementation-process` moved `planned` → `active`.

**The draft was returned once.** Its scenario on the checking role
required an implementation's effect "shown in the running system" —
verbatim the mechanism being removed, written into a role definition,
which would have reinstated through the role what the authority took
out of the set. Revised to require evidence that the effect occurred
without fixing the form that evidence takes. The lead-po had been told
not to depend on the principle and reached for its wording anyway,
because the compiled block still carried it: the stale rendering was
teaching the removal's opposite while the removal was being made.

The designer's and architect's steps both verified rather than
assumed. The designer read all seven scenarios against every entry of
the core-task list before confirming the initiative's "none". The
architect verified no decomposition exists, screened `knowable-shape`
and `bidirectional-conformance` against every scenario and found
neither binds, and — on the question the removal leaves open — wrote a
flag rather than a verdict.

**The PO's self-check passed fitness scenarios 1–6 and recorded three
fails, accepted unfixed**: Contributors carrying reasoning rather than
criteria (7), and both word targets (8, 9). It refused to repair them
inside a step whose prompt is evaluate-and-record, and raised the
structural cause: the step prompts require a recorded self-evaluation
in one Document History row while the base writing style targets 25
words for such a row, which makes a conforming row and an honest row
mutually exclusive (lead-tr7mq).

### The decision the removal left open

The authority answered it in this session. First: "Evidence is the
shop presenting the pass/fail status of the scenario tests." Asked
what a pass/fail is for a scenario that is not executable — this
feature's seven are judged reads of definition documents — they
answered: "Scenario tests means executable tests. For non-executable
tests, the checking role can mark the scenarios as passed."

Recorded as
[adr-2026-09-16-scenario-evidence-form](../decisions/adr-2026-09-16-scenario-evidence-form.md),
`right: guardrail`, conformance on all six architecture principles
with no escalation. The two things the subject did not settle were put
to the lead-pm as asks and defaulted on the first pass, recorded in
Consequences and again as reversibility triggers.

This is what the removal was for. The bar that went was one no product
could meet; what replaced it is per-form and answerable, and the lead
shop's own corpus work — where the trap was — now falls in the second
form and has a mechanism for the first time.

### Three defects found by running the flow

- **lead-ibme0 (P0, silent).** `product-flow`'s `find-decision` and
  `resolve-decision` both match `needs decision:` with a single-line
  grep. The feature guideline wraps Contributors prose, so a correctly
  written flag put "needs" at the end of one line and "decision:" at
  the start of the next, and neither step could see it.
  `route-decision` then reads the flag as empty and routes to `assign`
  — a feature with an unrecorded decision is assigned as if it had
  none, and nothing reports it. This session's decision was recorded
  only because the runner read the line by hand.
- **lead-tmahe.** `backlog-ordering` is `status: approved` and its
  `check` step routes into `po-output-check-process`, which is
  `retired`. Unnoticed because `product-flow` no longer calls
  `backlog-ordering` at all.
- **lead-dg6t9.** The migration-plan typedef's required section 9
  cites the removed principle in a parenthetical.
